# THE PAVEMENT CHANTIER — roads & runways, full PBR (2026-09-21)

The user: "roads and runways. I'd like these to be beautiful. Full PBR, non repetitive, proper
detailed zones on the side, proper terraforming with configurable distance, great textures
avoiding noticeable tiling, crack and decals, convincing PBR weathered markings ... blend great
with their environment, support dirt, grass, sand, asphalt and concrete. Tyre marks, traces in
the soft ground, natural markers like stones ... look at the state of the art way of doing such
objects in today's best engines ... We have a massive challenge with the old WWII airport at
Jolene island. The runway is huge, and we have to make it interesting."

The reference photographs (Annette Island, the real Jolene): poured-concrete PAVING LANES
(4-6 m) with longitudinal joints and rare transverse ones, per-lane tone differences, damp dark
stains, wet lanes reflecting the sky, yellow-green moss and lichen, chalky faded white STOP
letters, a crossing paved road, bare sand/gravel cleared bands beside the runway with vehicle
tracks and puddles, forest edges.

## 1. Where we start from

- A runway in the game: `render_world.js` standStrip — a draped Lambert plane with ONE diffuse
  texture tiled along it (`mkLook`, no normal, no roughness) and a 512x64 canvas of markings.
  Jolene's 13/31 = the `cracked` set repeated 580 times; its 140 m cleared bands a flat `dry`
  material polygon.
- A road: `render_premises.js` buildRoads — a flat-colour Lambert ribbon per surface class.
- The island's ground programs sit at 10/14/15 of 16 samplers (`splat_ground.js`): nothing may
  be added there. A pavement has to be its OWN mesh with its own material.

## 2. The state of the art, and what was taken

| Source | Technique | Adopted as |
|---|---|---|
| Mikkelsen 2022 *Practical Real-Time Hex-Tiling* (JCGT; UE Hextile, Unity) | 3 hashed-rotated samples on a hex lattice, a height-weighted blend | the base sets hex-tiled IN THE STRIP'S UV (the lanes stay axis-aligned) — the splat's own sTile |
| UE5 Runtime Virtual Texture roads | the road writes into the landscape's texture; shoulders blend, grass yields | the mesh carries its own shoulder zone with the SAME library sets as the ground and alpha-fades into it; tufts/stones read the edge distance |
| Spline road meshes (UE / Unity / Houdini road tools) | per-vertex distance-to-edge, a V profile across (2 ruts + grass) | `aPav = (u, v, dEdge, shoulderW)`; ruts analytic in v, meandering in u |
| RDR2 / Forza Horizon 5 / Driveclub | layered decals (cracks, patches, skids), authored wetness maps, puddles = roughness + albedo + normal | a procedural crack network + hashed patches + a `wet` knob with a puddle field |
| MSFS 2024 / REX airport textures | PBR pavement, rubber in the touchdown zone, weathered markings, water build-up | rubber bands + anisotropic streaks; markings as crisp SDF rectangles weathered in the shader |
| Far Cry 5 terrain | detail -> macro by distance | the detail fades to the set's macro layer; the normal strength fades (the grazing eye down 2 km) |

## 3. The design (what landed on the bench)

ONE `MeshStandardMaterial` + `onBeforeCompile` (`water.js`'s idiom): `src/viewer/pavement.js`.
`ATMO.inject` first; the hook's source is the program's key, so every pavement on an island is
one program; a material per strip (its markings are uniforms) and one per road class.

THE RIBBON: every vertex knows u (metres along), v (across, 0 on the centreline, + on the
right-hand side = polyRoad's `n`), dEdge (the pavement's box SDF: + inside, - on the shoulder),
shoulderW; `aPavK` = (class, seed, halfW, halfL); `aPavT` = the world tangent (x, z) with the
SHOULDER KEEP in y (0 where a road crosses a strip: no gravel band over the concrete). Row lines
are pinned at the edge, the band and the fade so the laws land on a vertex.

THE LIBRARY: two `DataArrayTexture`s (colour rgb + height a; normal xyz + rough a), assembled
per page from exactly the sets the recipe names (`PAVEMENT.library`) — 2 samplers of 16, any
number of sets. 23 sets baked by `tools/pavement_tex_prep.js` (14 fetched off Poly Haven by
`tools/pavement_tex_import.py --fetch`, 4 of the airfield's, the lot's 5), each with its real
size in metres from the API and a per-set grade (gain, saturation) in the recipe.

THE CHAIN (fragment, at map_fragment, one struct through): hex-tiled base -> macro variation
(8/40/200 m: luma, hue turn, roughness) -> the far tier (the macro slot) -> LANES & JOINTS
(concrete: lane width, a per-lane and per-slab tone hash, a groove with a normal from its
profile's derivative, chipped by noise, dirt-filled, spalled; the slab length varies per lane)
-> CRACKS (a Voronoi edge network on a warped domain, gated per cell edge so it never reads as
a honeycomb, only where a 40 m density field admits it - more near the edges and the joints;
the DAMAGE set height-blended in there) -> PATCHES (hashed cells, a darker repair inheriting the
surface's texture) -> MOSS (lichen-like noise, reaching in from the edge in proportion to the
width, along the joints and cracks, never in the touchdown zone) and DAMP STAINS -> MARKINGS
(the rect list, AA'd by fwidth; the paint's age = the share of it gone, worn first where the
slab is cracked, jointed, spalled or patched; chalking toward the concrete; the paint's own
roughness) -> RUBBER (a band from each threshold, a gaussian across at the gear track, 25:1
streaks) -> RUTS (a soft road's two ruts about a meandering centre with a grass stripe between;
the shoulder's vehicle paths - N per side, each wandering 6 m at 80 m, coming and going,
confined to the band, only where the shoulder is wide enough) -> THE EDGE ZONE (a chipped edge,
the gravel band, the grass creeping in by a torn noise, a 3-way height blend, then the alpha
fade past the band so the island's ground shows through) -> WET (a film with per-lane variation
on concrete - some lanes hold the water and reflect the sky, as the photographs do - and
puddles from a field biased to the crown, the ruts and the joints: roughness 0.03, the normal
flattened) -> the normal into the world through (T, cross(Ng, T), Ng), the roughness, the
alpha; the r186 haze rule at lights_fragment_end (both specular lobes x smoothstep(0.9, 0.6,
roughness)); ten debug views.

THE MARKINGS are not a picture: `PAVEMENT.marksOf(R, sitePaintStrip)` hands the game's own
painter a METRIC recording context and gets back the rectangles in metres; arithmetic
progressions (the centre dashes every 29 m, the hold-short dashes every 3 m) collapse to
RULES, the chevrons to capsule segments: 73 fills -> 13 rects/rules + 4 segments, crisp at 2 m
and at 2 km, `sitePaintStrip` still the one source of truth. `roadMarks` adds a road's edge
lines and centre dashes by class.

THE BENCH `tools/_pavement.html`: a noise terrain GRADED by the strip and the road with the
core's feather (the falloff sliders = the terraforming distance), one strip with its shoulders
and one S-curved road crossing it, both draped; every layer a knob in the aside (63 knobs, the
recipe in localStorage + export); a sun, a hemisphere and a procedural sky baked to a PMREM
environment (the wet lanes need something to reflect); tufts (site_ground's blades) in the
grass band and stones (an instanced squashed dodecahedron) in the gravel band; the views of the
reference photographs (cockpit / threshold / aerial / topdown / shoulder / road / junction).

THE RIG `tools/pavement_shot.js` (headless Chrome, the bench's GPU judge): `--sheet`, `--gate`
= the rough view never mirror-black, the alpha ramp a ramp, and the TILING SCORE: the
top-down luma high-passed and its autocorrelation's BUMP at the base tile's period - 0.973
with hex tiling off (the control), 0.060 with it on. The measured answer to "no tiling".

GATE PAVEMENT `tools/_pavement_check.js` (core): the builders' attributes with the real vendor
three, field.at = the attributes, the recorder against sitePaintStrip, the hook rules (ATMO
first, one program key, uniform loop bounds, no textureGrad on arrays, every anchor present in
r186's standard shaders, every GLSL uniform in the table, 2 samplers), the recipe's sets baked.

## 6. THE GUARDRAIL (2026-09-22) — `src/viewer/guardrail.js`

The user, after the port landed: *"could add metal guardrails on the large road sections with
nothing but forest, in the bends. Or where you see fit? Something like the attached picture.
Procedural of course, maybe with an option to turn them on and off."* The picture: a galvanised
W-beam — two of them stacked — on the sea side of a coast road, posts every four metres, each
panel its own tone.

**Where a rail goes is a rule, not a decoration.** The module measures, every 2 m and per side:

| measure | how | rails when |
|---|---|---|
| the DROP | the ground at the post line against the ground 3 / 7 / 14 m further out, and against the WATER where that is higher (the picture's sea cliff) | the WARRANT — the fall must reach **0.8 m at 3 m, 1.7 m at 7 m, 3.0 m at 14 m**: a 1:4 batter (recalibrated 2026-09-22, below) |
| the CURVATURE | the tangent's turn over ±8 m; its sign names the bend's OUTSIDE | `kappa >= kCurve` (0.011 /m, r ≈ 90 m) **and** this is the outside **and** the warrant is half met |
| the CALLER'S `keep` | the premises: not over a plot or within 8 m of one, not within a road's width of another road (a junction), not on a strip. The analytic world: never inside a settlement's core (`roadNet.inCore`), never within 20 m of one of its buildings | — |

The warrant is a TABLE, not one number, and that is the whole difference between a rule and a
rash: the first cut asked for 1.6 m of fall anywhere within 14 m, which is a 12 % hillside — two
thirds of the analytic world's road network wore a rail (10.8 km).

**Calibrated twice, the second time on the real island.** A 1:3 table (1.0 / 2.2 / 4.0) was set
against the analytic world alone, where it rails 4.1 km — all of it the one road cut into the
mountain. Measured in the game on JOLENE's own DEM it rails **60 m of the entire island**, because
her roads sit on coastal flat: the airport road falls 0.4 / 1.6 / 2.5 m at 3 / 7 / 14 m, the shore
road 0.7 / 1.6 / 2.9, and only `v_north` has a real bank at 1.2 / 3.3 / 6.1. The 1:4 table
(0.8 / 1.7 / 3.0) rails 221 m of Jolene — which is where it stood when the user judged the
pictures — and 6.2 km of the analytic world's mountains. That is the shipped table.

Then hysteresis, because a six-metre rail in the middle of nowhere is what reads as procedural:
gaps under 10 m closed, runs under 18 m dropped, every run extended 5 m each end, and those ends
ramped into the ground and flared outward — a buried terminal. A run whose worst fall is over
5.5 m gets the second beam. A road under 3.2 m wide (a track, a path) carries none.

**What is drawn.** One merged non-indexed BufferGeometry PER RUN — not per road: the mountain
road's rails are 34 runs over 11 km, and one mesh's bounding sphere would cover the island, so it
would be drawn from everywhere; a run is a hundred metres and the frustum throws away all but the
one or two you are over. The group is the road's. ONE
`MeshStandardMaterial` for every rail in the world (`0xb6babd`, metalness 0.58, roughness 0.48),
the panel-to-panel tone, the grime climbing from the post feet and the one-in-seven rust stain
carried by VERTEX COLOUR — no texture, no shader hook, so a rail is one draw call, casts and takes
shadow, and rides the atmosphere through three's prototype hook. The section is the AASHTO
W-beam's (312 mm tall, 83 mm deep, the belly bolted at mid-height, the top 760 mm over the road),
13 points with the crests slightly convex so each facet takes its own highlight; the normals are
per-facet, because an averaged normal rounds a stamped beam into a pipe. The beam does **not**
drape: it follows its own GRADE, the ground averaged over ±6 m, and the posts make up the
difference — which is what a beam does. 27 triangles a metre for one beam, 54 for two; a build
raises at most 4 km of rail, the steepest runs first.

**The switch**: GRAPHICS > guardrails (`gfx_settings.js`), on in every preset. Every mesh is
registered with the module and the switch flips `visible` — nothing is rebuilt, so it is free
either way. The editor's road inspector has `guardrail`: *where the ground says* (the rule),
*the whole road*, *none* — stored as the road's `rail` (contract v1.18).

Measured by GATE PAVEMENT §7: a flat straight road rails nothing; a shelf bend rails its outside
only, with the beam outside the pavement and between −0.25 m and +1.20 m of the ground; a 6.3 m
fall takes two beams; an 8 m dip is dropped by the 18 m rule; `keep` forbids; the switch hides.

**Owed**: the splice bolts and the lapped panel ends (the tone step is all we draw today); a
timber-post variant for a village lane; the rail's own shadow in the near map is three's
(measured, not tuned); nothing reads the rail as a collision surface — an aeroplane taxis through it.

## 8. THE POWER LINE (2026-09-22) — `src/viewer/powerline.js`

The user, with a Google Maps still of a road on Revillagigedo: *"do we have the electric poles?"*
We had the POLES and never stood one: three Poly Haven presets (`pole_a` transformer, `pole_b`
plain, `pole_c`) baked into the yard pack at G285 with two LOD rungs, and a village-bench rule
(`_village_gen.js planPoles`, G285/G370) that placed them every 32–40 m on the inland verge with a
street lamp on every second one — whose ONLY consumer was `tools/_village.html`. Nothing in
`src/viewer/**` ever read `vil.poles`. And there had never been a CABLE, which is the part that
makes a line read: the theme doc has carried *"the CABLE between utility poles (the poles stand
alone today — a catenary between consecutive poles is what makes a line read)"* since 2026-09-14.

**The module owns the rule and the wire, and nothing else.** It knows no props, no house kit, no
lamp: the renderer hands it `place(q)` and `lamp(q)` and gets back a group.

| | |
|---|---|
| the SIDE | one verge for the whole road: whichever takes more poles once the caller's `keep` has spoken, and never the verge a guardrail already took (`userData.guardrail.sides`, when the rail took exactly one). A tie goes inland, the village's own convention. |
| the PITCH | every 34 m ± 4, the three presets spread by a hash, a street lamp on every second pole |
| the KEEP | not INSIDE a plot (a pole stands on the verge in front of a frontage — unlike the guardrail, which keeps 8 m clear of one), not in a junction, not on a strip |
| the CABLE | three conductors on the crossarm (which runs across the road, so they spread along the road's normal) and one service cable 1.6 m lower, each span a parabola through `TRAM_RUN.ropeCurve` — the codebase's one sag, written for the tram's haul rope. Sag is a fraction of the span, so a long crossing hangs deeper on its own; a gap over 110 m breaks the line rather than striding it. |

**Cost.** A four-sided tube at 35 mm, smooth-shaded, ~90 triangles a wire a span, 350 for the
four: Jolene's eight lines are 221 poles, 212 spans and 80 k triangles of cable. The cable is
merged per `chunk` (10 spans, ~350 m) — a single mesh for a road would carry an island-wide
bounding sphere and be drawn from everywhere; a mesh per span would be a draw call every 34 m.
The poles are props with their own LOD ladder, and their geometry is shared across every pole in
the world.

**The lamps** are the bench's `VILLAGE_GEN.streetLamp` — an arm over the road, a brace, a cobra
head, a glowing lens — drawn into the road's own bags with the galvanised finish, the lens
registered in `LAMPS.glass` so it follows the night like every other pane, and each head pushed
into `LAMPS.pub`, the renderer's nearest-eight point-light pool. No new light budget.

**Switches**: GRAPHICS > power lines, and per road in the editor — `poles`: *poles and cable* /
*none* (contract v1.19), on unless it says otherwise.

Measured by GATE PAVEMENT §8: the pitch and its jitter, every pole on one side at the edge plus
0.55 m, a lamp every second one, a `keep` that refuses the inland verge moving the line to the
other, four hooks a pole all under its top, the cable in more than one mesh and fewer than one a
span with a bounding sphere a fraction of the road, no NaN, and the switch.

**THE BUG THIS CHANTIER FOUND (and §7 had shipped with):** `world.waterH(x, z)` is a FIELD — the
water surface at that point, `-Infinity` where there is none, a lake's 137 m where there is one.
The guardrail was handing its rule a single number sampled at the world's ORIGIN, and one lake
under the origin clamped every probe on the island above its own bank: **every guardrail on Jolene
disappeared** (937 m planned, 0 built). `waterY` takes a function now and is evaluated at the probe.

**Owed**: a service drop from the line to each frontage (the picture's lower cable runs into the
shed); guy wires at an angle pole; a transformer's hum at night; the PLANT tool and a `line`
feature kind in the record, which is what would let a hand draw a spur the roads do not follow.

## 9. THE TREES OBEY THE PAVEMENT (2026-09-22)

The user, on the power-line screenshots: *"feels to me like we have a lot of trees on the roads,
several of your screenshots show trees in or very close to the road."* They were right, and the
cause is an asymmetry this chantier created and did not finish: **the grass tufts have obeyed the
pavement since `coverAt` (v1.17); the trees never did.**

Traced, the whole chain:

| planter | what stopped it near a road | |
|---|---|---|
| the streamed forest fill (`render_world.js` `walk`, the layer you see) | `forestHere`/`openHere` — **nothing about roads at all**. Their only road awareness was `world.surface(x, z)`, which returns the premises' road class *inside the carriageway*: a clearance of **0 m from the edge** | ✗ |
| …and worse, `ROAD_CLS` maps `track`/`path` → `SURFACE.GRASS`, which is exactly what `openHere` accepts | so trees planted **on** a track, 0 m from its centreline | ✗ |
| the collidable woodland (`20_world.js`) | `SET.roadNear(x, z) < 12` — but on an ISLAND `SET` is a stub whose `roadNear` returns 1e9, so it is dead code on Jolene | ✗ |
| its clump neighbours (`render_world.js`) | nothing: a legal tree 12 m off throws 2–4 satellites 4–18 m in any direction | ✗ |
| the far stand cards | `forestHere` again | ✗ |
| premises `forest` zones (`planForest`) | 3 m past the road's edge — the only real clearance in the codebase, and Jolene declares no forest zone | ✓ |

**The fix is the law the grass already keeps.** `forestHere` and `openHere` — the one pair shared by
the fill, the stand cards, the terrain colour bake and the far canopy mask — reject where
`world.coverAt(x, z).kill > 0`: the pavement, its drawn band and the 6 m fade past it, which is
**6.5–7.2 m of clearance from a road's edge** (9–10 m from a village street's centreline, measured).
The woodland bake and the clump neighbours take the same test, so a collidable tree cannot stand on
a street either.

`coverAt` gained a third argument for it: `coverAt(x, z, 'pave')` answers the pavement half and
skips the plot walk, which is the query's cost. Measured on Jolene: **0.19 µs against 1.34 µs**, the
same `kill` to the bit, and the fill runs it on every lattice point of every chunk (25 600 a chunk →
5 ms). GATE PAVEMENT §6 holds the equality, the speed and the clearance.

### 9.1 …and so do the rocks and the debris (G502, the terrain session, 2026-09-22)

The user asked for the same rule for *"debris, rocks and branches/logs"* — and the terrain session's
user asked them for it in the same hour, so it exists once, in their hand, in `cover_ring.js`
`placeRocks` (which the far rock map shares):

    if (G.kill[gk] >= 1 || (G.cls[gk] && G.kill[gk] >= 0.5)) continue;
    if (G.kill[gk] > 0 && Rq() > 1 - G.kill[gk]) continue;

It reads the ring's own 4 m NODE (`G.kill` / `G.cls`, sampled once for the tufts) rather than
querying per piece, so it costs nothing in the inner loop. The second line is the taper — a piece
survives at `1 - kill`, so the verge keeps a thinning few, which is what a graded gravel road looks
like. The first is the case my own note to them had flagged and my duplicate had dropped: a GRASS
pavement (a track, a grass strip) only thins to `kill` 0.6 and never goes bare, so a log would have
survived on a grass runway at 40 %; where there is a class at all and a real grip, nothing lies.

I wrote the same test per piece before their landing surfaced; theirs is better on both counts and
mine was dropped. The lesson for the chantier is the one in §9: **the law has to be one line in every
planter, and the gate has to name them all** — GATE PAVEMENT §6 now walks the three planters'
bodies (the fill's `forestHere`/`openHere` through `paved`, and `placeRocks`) and fails if one stops
reading the cover.

## 10. THE RUTS ARE A VEHICLE'S TRACK (2026-09-23)

The user: *"an important check on your tyre marks. Do they represent a real interaxle distance? They
seem big?"* They did not, and they were.

`rutTrack` is the HALF-TRACK in metres — a pair of ruts sits at its centre ±that — and it shipped at
**1.55**, clamped to `halfW * 0.5`. So a 6 m road wore its ruts **3.0 m apart** and anything wider
3.1 m apart. Real tracks: a car 1.5, a pickup 1.7, a light truck 1.8–2.0, a heavy truck 2.1 (the
legal vehicle maximum is 2.55 m overall). Three metres is a vehicle nobody builds.

Now `rutTrack` is **0.85 m — a 1.7 m pickup**, and the road's width decides how many tracks it
carries, which is the user's own suggestion and also what a rural road looks like:

| road | tracks |
|---|---|
| under ~5.3 m | ONE shared track down the middle (everyone drives the same line), with the fainter second pass where the traffic went round — a 200 m noise says where |
| over ~5.3 m | ONE TRACK PER DIRECTION, on the lane centres (`±halfW/2`), each wandering on its own noise, both as worn as each other |

The threshold is not a magic number: it is `2·ht + 2·rw + 0.35` — the width at which two tracks and
their ruts fit side by side with a hand's breadth between, i.e. the width at which two vehicles can
pass. A 6 m road puts its four ruts at ±0.65 and ±2.35 m; a 3 m track puts two at ±0.75 (the clamp
still applies, so a narrow track is a narrow vehicle's).

The grass stripe follows: between the WHEELS on a single track, between the two TRACKS on a road
that carries one each way — which is where it really grows, because nobody drives the crown.

Unchanged by this: the aeroplane strips, whose wheel pairs are the gear track (`rubberTrack`), not
this one. Improved by it for free: the shoulder's vehicle paths, which read the same half-track and
were equally wide.

## 11. THE LANES, THEIR PAINT AND ITS WEAR (2026-09-23)

The user, in the same breath as the ruts: *"let's do the same for the roads; have a dynamic number
of lanes function of road width please, and proper road marking, including its wear."*

**`lanesOf(w, cls, laneW)` is the one lane rule** — the paint and the wear both call it, so they
cannot disagree about where a lane is. A lane is `roadLane` metres, 3.5 by default (a rural
highway's; 3.0 a town street, 3.7 an interstate), capped at six:

| width | lanes | painted |
|---|---|---|
| under 5.2 m | 1 | the edge lines and nothing else — one shared track, which is also the one set of ruts §10 draws |
| 5.2 – 8.7 m | 2 | edge lines, and **one dashed yellow** down the middle |
| 8.8 – 12.2 m | 3 | edge lines, a dashed yellow where the directions divide (2 lanes one way, 1 the other), a **white dashed** divider between the two |
| 12.3 m + | 4–6 | edge lines, a **double solid yellow** between the directions, white dashed dividers within each |
| any soft class | — | **nothing**: gravel and dirt are not painted |

The convention is the American one, which is Alaska's: white edge lines, yellow between opposing
traffic, white between lanes going the same way, 3 m of line and 9 m of gap.

**The wear is the traffic's own.** A paved road is polished in the WHEEL PATHS — two to a lane, at
the vehicle's half-track (§10's number) from the lane's centre: the aggregate there is smoother and
a shade darker, and **the paint that crosses a wheel path is scrubbed away**. That needs no special
case for the centre line: it lies between the wheels of both directions, which is exactly why a
centre line outlives an edge line on a real road, and why the edge line — 30 cm outside the outer
