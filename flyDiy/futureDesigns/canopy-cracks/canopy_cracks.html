Canopy cracks

How to draw a broken windscreen or canopy that reads like a real one, per glazing material, on a phone budget.

12 Nov 2026 · for A0 (Performance Coordinator) and the Deform Coordinator · implementation after the perf round (13–14 Nov), on the user's choice

The short answer

  • Why it looks childish: every crack is the same thick, wobbly, glowing chalk line, and the pattern is a spider web (radials + rings + a honeycomb). That web belongs to laminated car windscreens. Acrylic cracks in a few straight, forking hairlines that glint only where they catch the light. Polycarbonate barely cracks at all.
  • The garage already has the material row: glazeMat (acrylic / polycarbonate / glass, carried as cabin.glazingMat). The crack shader ignores it. No new row is needed.
  • Recommended: Option B, a baked crack sheet per material (one texture, distance-field lines), read by the glass shader that already draws the marks. It looks better and costs less per pixel than today's procedural star. Then Option C for broken marks: a hole and shards held in the frame, with no new bodies and nothing floating.
1 · The current drawing

What the game draws today

Since G3623 (27 Oct) a frame member that yields or breaks near a pane puts a star on it. The star is drawn by the glass's own shader (dmg_glass.js GLSL_CRACK, spliced into aeroskin.js AEROGLASS_HOOK), for every aeroplane alike. These are the gate's own stills at full severity, from the seat:

Why it reads like a child's drawing

  1. A chalk stroke, not a hairline. Each ray carries a haze band of 0.55 out to six times its width or two pixels (haze = 0.55·(1 − sm(w, max(6w, 2px), d))). From the seat the lines come out 3–5 px wide and evenly glowing, like a marker pen. A real crack is a hairline that is invisible along most of its length.
  2. Hand tremor. The ray angle wobbles with two noise octaves (0.10·fbm(9r) + 0.12·fbm(2.3r)). Brittle cracks run straight and turn at sharp kinks. They also fork, which the angular-cell method cannot do.
  3. Same brightness everywhere. Every ray adds the pane's irradiance at the same 0.85 from centre to tip. Real cracks are small mirrors inside the sheet: a few segments flash where they face the light, the rest are faint, and some read darker than the sky. An additive pane can only brighten, so the dark side is missing.
  4. A spider web and a honeycomb. The concentric arcs and the Voronoi net (dgVor at 60 cells per metre) are the cartoon cues for "broken glass". Rings belong to laminated glass, held by its plastic interlayer. A uniform net of cells is tempered glass dicing. Neither happens on a 3 mm acrylic sheet.
  5. Wrong scale and count. 5–9 rays spread over up to 36 cm, all starting at one point and tapering the same way, make a symmetrical asterisk. Real acrylic has a small crushed white spot, many short cracks around it and a few long runners that carry on to the frame.
  6. One look for every material. The polycarbonate row exists in the garage, but its pane breaks exactly like acrylic.
2 · Real materials

What light-aircraft transparencies actually do

The pictures below are drawn by this page with the proposed look (Option B, full severity). They are mock-ups, not game stills.

Acrylic (Perspex, Plexiglas) glazeMat 0

Brittle and glassy. A hit leaves a small crushed, whitened spot, a fringe of short cracks, and a few long running cracks that branch and carry on until they reach a frame, a hole or an edge. Shards are sharp and can come out (the 2010 Jersey bird strike: the windscreen "shattered on impact", the pilot cut by the pieces). The S-76 N748P bird strike shattered both cast-acrylic windshield halves. Rings are rare and short. Cast sheet is the more brittle; stretched (biaxially oriented) sheet resists running cracks, so they stay shorter. The maintenance answer to a crack is a stop-drill hole at its tip (FAA AC 43.13-1B, Fig. 3-21), which shows how readily acrylic cracks run.

The whole fleet today, list:41 Super Petrel LS included.

Polycarbonate (Lexan) glazeMat 1

Ductile. On impact it deforms plastically instead of shattering. It dents. Around the dent it stress-whitens to a milky ring and crazes: hundreds of fine micro-cracks across the stress, so they lie in rings round the hit and sparkle together in one direction. A severe hit punctures and tears into a few folded petals, with no long running cracks. Common on ultralights, kit doors and military canopies.

No listed plane uses it yet. The row exists and costs the same weight.

Laminated glass (windscreen) glazeMat 2, windscreen pane

Two glass plies on a plastic interlayer (PVB). A hit gives the familiar spider web: many straight radials, chords between them, a powdered white cone at the centre. The pieces stay stuck to the interlayer, so the pane holds its shape and bulges. Each fragment tilts a little and catches the light differently. Bigger types and old airliner-style screens (list:12 DC-3-alike).

Tempered glass (side window) glazeMat 2, side panes

Holds or fails whole. When it fails, the whole pane dices into 1–2 cm cubes at once and mostly falls out, leaving a fringe of dice in the frame. Today's Voronoi core is this pattern, put on the wrong material and at the wrong scale.

The glazeMat 2 split by pane role (windscreen laminated, other panes tempered) is a proposal. It reads the panes' existing material names (windshield against pilotWindow and the rest) and needs no new row.

6 · Proposal

Three options

A · Retune the procedural crack

Look
Hairlines without the halo, straight with kinks, glint per ray, a small whitened core in place of the honeycomb, no rings on acrylic. Per material through uCrackMat: the polycarbonate dent, whitening and craze rings are analytic. No forks, no thickness.
Cost
About a third of today's math per fragment (the 34-cell Voronoi goes). No texture. No new program.
Effort
About 1 session: rewrite GLSL_CRACK and its mirror crackJS, re-pin GATE DMGGLASS rows G and R, add the material row to U.

B · Baked crack sheet per material recommended

Look
The professional one: forking runners to the frame, the hackle fringe, the crushed white spot, a glint that changes along each crack, a faint dark companion for the 3 mm thickness. Polycarbonate gets its dent, whitened ring and crazing. Glass gets the laminated web or tempered dice.
Cost
1–2 texture reads and about 40 ALU a fragment, cheaper than today. One RGBA8 sheet of 1024², 4 variants of 512², ≈5.3 MB of GPU memory with mipmaps (512² on the low tier ≈1.3 MB). It loads only at the flown aeroplane's first mark, for its own material. No new program.
Effort
About 2 sessions: an offline generator (tools/crack_sheet.js, node, seeded, the same growth rules as this page) writing three PNGs; the shader read; a lazy load; GATE DMGGLASS rows for the sheet hash, monotone reveal, the material switch and 0 new programs.

C · B plus the broken pane then

Look
At a broken member (severity 1): acrylic punches a jagged hole at the hit, and the shards between the runners stay in the frame, each catching the light a little differently. Polycarbonate tears into petals. Laminated glass holds and bulges. Tempered glass falls out and leaves a fringe. No new bodies and nothing floating. The user asked for fewer pieces and shapes kept.
Cost
A shard-id channel (a second 512² R8, ≈0.35 MB). The tint companion pass reads the sheet once to discard the hole too.
Effort
About 1 session after B, with gate rows for the hole seen from both sides and no new bodies or programs.

7 · How B would work

Implementation sketch

  • The sheet. R: distance to the nearest crack (0–8 mm). G: when that crack arrived as it grew (reveal by severity, taper toward the tips). B: the crack facet's phase (the glint). A: whitening and craze density. Three sheets (acrylic, polycarbonate, glass), each 2×2 variants picked by the mark's seed, and rotated by it too.
  • The read. The existing mark loop keeps its gating (craft space, 4 cm of the plane, facing the normal) and its tangent frame. It turns q into sheet coordinates (the pattern is about 1 m across, so runners can reach the frame) and reads once (twice for the thickness companion).
  • The light. As G3623 does: the pane's own irradiance, scattered near-white, plus the roughness bump. The glint multiplies it. A dark companion needs the multiply pass, so it comes in C, where that pass reads the sheet anyway.
  • The triggers stay G3623's. Members within 0.35 m that set or break, merging, MAX 4, nothing on hard landings. One better trigger for later, flagged to the Deform Coordinator: the contact point on the pane, so a trunk hitting glass between members marks it.
  • Smooth shading holds. No geometry and no normal writes. The hole in C is a discard in both glass passes.
