# GROUND-FX (15 Nov 2026): one ground particle system at professional quality, and mud on the plane computed from its geometry

The user, 15 Nov: "the mud projections, or whatever what was spawned at the cabin strip, is not good enough. Right now they
are ellipses with a single color, that's OK only as a proof-of-concept. ... aim for professional quality, unreal/snowrunner
like particle projections for the ground. It's essential. I also suspect the plane's projections to be a little slapped. I'd
really want that we do some calculations for projecting on the right places, as a function of the actual plane geometry".

Owner: GROUND-FX (local, GPU in A0's slots). Branches `claude/ground-fx-*`; A0 copies them to ready.
Base: master after L40d (SNOW-MUD FINISH dc8667014 in). The "before": DIRT-DUST's 15:15 stills, `D:/Dev/flydiy-notes/evidence/mud-fx-2026-11-15/`.

## 1. The system: one pool, one contact API, one batch per kind

`dust.js` stays the ONE system (no rename: `window.DUST` plus a `window.GROUND_FX` alias). It gains:

### 1.1 The contact API (agreed with DIRT-STRIP, DIRT-DUST, SNOW-MUD; nobody forks)
```
GROUND_FX.contact(sys, {                 // once a frame per touching contact (wheel, ski, float step, tail skid)
  key, x, y, z,                           // the contact patch, world
  vx, vy, vz,                             // the patch's velocity, world (node motion over the frame, as today)
  R, W,                                   // tyre radius and width (or the ski's / float's width)
  slip, lock,                             // the side slip (m/s across the rolling axis) and the brake lock (0..1): the solver has no wheel spin
  sink, brake, ski,                       // as today
  cls, wet, puddle, col                   // the ground under the patch: class, water 0..1, puddle depth, ALBEDO (linear rgb)
})
GROUND_FX.deposit(sys, sink)               // the airframe sink (section 3): clods that hit the aeroplane go here
```
Agreed with DIRT-STRIP (15 Nov):
- `puddle` is the depth in metres, exactly what `STRIP_MICRO.puddleAt(x, z, wet)` returns. Its splash law scales by pud / 0.02 m.
- `wet` is the page's `PAVEMENT.WET.value` (SOIL_WET.force overrides it on a rig), so the splash and the drawn water agree.
- The solver has NO wheel spin: wheels are contact nodes, and `sim.wheelContacts()` gives on-ground booleans only. So
  `omega` is dropped. Slip is a proxy:
  - SIDE SLIP: the node's velocity across the wheel's rolling axis (`sim.axes()`, or the castoring tailwheel's own line);
  - LOCK: the brake input x on-ground at V > 0;
  - SPIN-UP: the first frames of a contact after a sink (the touchdown).
- Static ruts and worn lanes stay in pavement.js's shader. Dynamic tyre marks, if ever added, are a separate decal layer.
- Base for dust.js: DIRT-STRIP's 02a0e7062 (claude/dirt-faults, L40e): the puddle splash by rate, 15 % big drops.

`sources()` already reads exactly this shape from `st.wheels`; the change is that the ground under the patch (class, water,
puddle, colour) is resolved once by the caller and passed in, so DIRT-STRIP's puddles and tyre marks and SNOW-MUD's classes
read the same numbers. DIRT-STRIP's tyre marks keep their own decal path (`ground_scar.js` / strip_micro) and read the same
contact record; the splats (landed mud) become theirs to draw if they want (question 6).

### 1.2 The ground's own colour
The terrain bake's own `colorAt(x, z)` (render_world.js, today a closure: exposed read-only for the contact; its five height
taps are too dear for every frame, so it is sampled at most every 0.1 s a wheel and cached) and the premises' pavement colour where a paved/strip
record covers the patch, sampled at each contact every ~0.1 s, passed as `col`. Every emitted particle carries its own colour
(an instance attribute, jittered +-8 % in value and a few % in hue per particle, darkened by wetness: wet soil is ~0.5-0.7 of
its dry albedo). A clod thrown off a brown strip's grass edge is that edge's colour; snow is snow's; mud is the mud under
THAT wheel.

### 1.3 The kinds and their draw (one instanced batch each)
| Kind | Draw | What makes it professional |
|---|---|---|
| CLOD (mud, 6-30 mm, and snow clumps) | INSTANCED MESH: 4 low-poly rock variants (an icosphere, 42 verts, noise-displaced; baked in code), one draw | Real volume and silhouette; tumbles (axis + spin per instance); lit by the world sun/sky with its normal, wet specular on fresh mud; size from a log-normal (few big, many small); ballistic with drag by its own size (big ones fly further, as in SnowRunner). Phone: the sprite version below. |
| SPATTER / DROP (mud water, slush, puddle water) | SPRITE from a baked flipbook atlas (clusters of droplets, 16 frames, code-baked like the six-way) | Motion-stretched along the screen velocity (capped), a wet sheen (sky reflection + sun glint), alpha from the cluster, not a disc; colour per particle. |
| SHEET (the rooster tail's body at speed) | SPRITE, ribbon-like stretched cards | The thick fan off the tread at take-off speed reads as a sheet, not as dots: a few large stretched translucent cards per wheel along the release cone, under the clods. |
| PUFF (dust, snow cloud) | V2 six-way atlas (as today) | Kept; snow gets its own brighter bake (finer, sparkly drift stays). |
| DRIFT (snow saltation) | sprite | Kept. |
| SPLAT (landed mud on the ground) | ground-projected quad, its colour from the clod, a baked splat atlas (4 shapes) with a fake normal for the wet light | Lies, glistens, dries lighter and duller, erodes from the thin parts (today's law, better shapes). |

Soft particles: puffs keep the analytic ground fade (no depth read, the phone's cost). Clods need none (they are meshes).
Sprites (drops, sheets) fade against the ground height the same way, and against the AA pass's depth texture on gamer IF the
pipeline already has one bound (question 3), never a new depth pass.

### 1.4 The wheel throw, by its real speed and slip
Today's tread release is right (a point at angle phi round the tyre moves at V(1 - cos phi), V sin phi; it lets go once
V^2/R passes the mud's adhesion). Added: SLIP. An aeroplane wheel is not driven, so its rooster tail is weak at a steady roll
and strong at (a) the touchdown spin-up (the tread slides forward: mud shoved forward and up), (b) braking (locked: a bow
of mud ahead, no tread throw), (c) a deep rut (sinkage: the bow wave and the side walls). The emission cone (axis, half-angle,
speed) comes from those four and the tyre's width; the prop wash strips drops from wet ground aft along its footprint. Skis
plough (as today), floats belong to spray.js.

## 3. Mud on the plane, computed from the geometry

### 3.1 The airframe grid (once per build)
When a build is made (and in the garage, and in node), voxelise the drawn aeroplane in the CRAFT FRAME (the same frame
aeroweather's `vCraftPos` is in) into an occupancy grid: cell 6 cm (gamer) / 10 cm (phone), only the box from the ground to
1.8 m above the wheels' contacts (where thrown mud can reach), the whole length and span. Per cell: occupied, the part id
(leg, fairing, wheel, fuselage, wing, flap, stab, fin, float), the mean outward normal. A Cub: ~120 x 30 x 180 cells, ~650 k
cells as one bit each + a sparse normal list: under 0.5 MB, built in ~30-60 ms off the geometry the build already has
(triangles rasterised into the grid; no new geometry, no hand placement). Any garage-built aeroplane gets it the same way.

### 3.2 The hits (each frame, CPU)
Every clod and drop in flight near the aeroplane moves in the craft frame (its world velocity minus the aeroplane's, rotated
into the craft frame); its segment this frame is marched through the grid (3-D DDA). On the first occupied cell: a HIT. The
clod dies there (or splits in two smaller drops on a glancing hit). So the far side of a leg, a fairing's lee, the stab behind
the flap are shadowed by what is in front of them, because the clods stop there.

### 3.3 The deposit (accumulated, per surface)
A second grid, the DEPOSIT (same cells, 4 channels = the mud arriving from 4 direction bins: up, aft, outboard-left,
outboard-right, in the craft frame), takes each hit: mass = clod volume x wetness x clamp(impact speed / 6 m/s), spread over
the hit cell and its 26 neighbours with a small kernel. It is uploaded as a 3-D texture (WebGL2 `Data3DTexture`, R8x4) at
most 4 times a second, only the dirty slab.

### 3.4 The draw
aeroweather's fragment samples the deposit at `vCraftPos` (trilinear), weights each channel by how much the fragment's normal
faces that arrival direction (so a wing's top over a muddy underside stays clean, the inner face of a leg takes what came from
the inner wheel), breaks it with the existing blotch noise and the same mud colour rule (the ground's colour, wet to dry), and
draws it through the existing mud layer. Then the mud sits where it really would: behind and above each wheel, the inner leg,
the fairing's front and bottom, the flaps' leading edges and undersides, the stab's underside if the throw reaches it, the
tail wheel's own spray on the rudder's foot.

### 3.5 Wash and dry
- Rain: each cell loses a share a second by rain x how much its surfaces face up and out (the undersides keep it longest).
- Water landing / taxi: the cells below the waterline (the float's or the hull's) wash fast; the spray above it slower.
- Drying: wet fresh mud is darker and glossy, it dries lighter and matte over minutes (the same law as the splats).
- The scalar `aeroMudStep` m stays (its law, the gate MUDCOAT, the career's link): it becomes the grid's total, so nothing
  that reads m changes.

### 3.6 The BUSH macro (the build's long-term wear)
The macro's "wheel mud" also uses a computed deposit: a canned 30 s mud run simulated in node (the same sources, the same
grid) once per build, stored with the build's look. The hand cone goes away for every plane (question 5).

### 3.7 Gates (node, no GPU)
- GATE GROUNDFX: the pool budget per tier, the LOD, the colour per particle, the slip cases (spin-up, braking, steady roll),
  no allocation in the frame loop.
- GATE MUDDEPOSIT: on the stock Cub and the Cessna 172 (list:50), a 20 s soft-mud roll in node: the deposit lands on the
  gear legs' inner/aft faces, the wheel fairings (if any), the belly aft of the mains and the flaps' undersides; it is ZERO on
  the wing tops, the windscreen and the cowl's top; a leg's lee side holds under a third of its front's. quickshot.js renders
  the mask over the build (side, front, under) for the evidence.
