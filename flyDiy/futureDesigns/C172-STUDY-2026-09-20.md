The same exercise as the Cub's (PERF-STUDY-2026-09-15.md §2-4), on ONE build
the user made in the garage rather than on a recreation card, against the
aeroplane every pilot knows the numbers of — then the build CORRECTED to the
type sheet through the repo's own chain, and measured again.

Files:

- `builds/cessna172_2026-09-20_corrected.json` — THE DELIVERABLE: the same
  airframe with the edits of §4, re-joined headless, flown. Also the STOCK

- `futureDesigns/C172-STUDY-2026-09-20.run.txt` / `.corrected.txt` — the
  instrument's raw output for both.

The reference is the 172R row of `tools/perf_study.js`'s REAL table (POH
172R, IO-360-L2A 160 hp, MTOW 1111 kg), extended today with the TYPE SHEET a

## 0. The instrument

    node tools/perf_study.js --build=builds/cessna172_2026-09-20.json --vs=c172 --engine=io360_mccauley --fly --md=out.md
    node tools/perf_study.js --build=builds/cessna172_2026-09-20_corrected.json --vs=c172 --fly

`--build` flies a saved build as it is (the export already carries the join's
measurements: the profile, the cabin, the gear stations, the tail's areas — no
re-bake). `--vs` names the reference row (guessed from the build's name when
it says 172/cessna/cub). `--engine` swaps the registry row on every engine —
the build as saved carried an O-540 (235 hp, 176 kg) the user had boosted it
with; the user's second save (`bugReports/cessna (2).json`, an OLDER state of
the airframe) has the IO-360, so the honest as-found run is the newer
airframe on the older engine. `--fly` adds the pilot's own take-off, climb
and landing. Everything under "ours" is MEASURED off the built aeroplane by
the same probes the shakedown and GATE DRAG use; nothing is read off a rule.
The build is loaded to the type's MTOW with freight through the cargo door
before the power-bound numbers are read.

Three sections print: the PERF table (the PERF STUDY's columns), the
DIMENSIONS (ours / type sheet / delta) and the BUILD CHECK — the bench's three
cards headless (stance and balance off the shakedown, the sandbags ticked to
their verdict, the hot-and-high sheet) plus the ledger, the systems rows and
the drag build-up.

THE DATUM (found in §4, and it changes how §1.2 reads): every station the
join writes — `gear.x`, `wings[].xLE`, `cabin.seatsX`, `tail.hX`, and the
frame's `engX` — is metres aft of the `wsFront` ring, the WINDSCREEN BASE.
`_cage_join.js` names that ring "the firewall" (`zFw`), but it is measured at
`wsFront`; the cage's `noseLen` section (the cowl deck) runs forward of it to
the real firewall. A 172's firewall is ~0.5 m ahead of its windscreen base
(FS 0 vs ~FS 20), so a type sheet's fuselage stations must be re-based before
they are compared. The instrument's comment says so now.

## 3. The discrepancies AS FOUND, ranked, and what each one was

### 3.1 The engine: 235 hp as saved, and the 160 hp row that is a 180

The build had been boosted to an O-540. The registry's `io360_mccauley`
(00_registry.js) is named "Lycoming IO-360-L2A" and rated `powerW: 134000`
= 180 hp — the RV-7's engine under the 172's name (the RV's REAL row says 134
kW, so the RV is served right). The L2A is the same IO-360 rated to 2400
rpm: 119/134 kW = 2400/2700. The 172 CARD in PERF STUDY read its ROC +7 % /
TO −14 % on the wrong engine too. RULING OWED: rename the row (IO-360-A1A
180 hp) and add an `io360_l2a` 160 hp / 2400 rpm row, or re-rate. §4.1 is
the honest workaround.

### 3.2 The balance: CG 47 % MAC, static margin 3 %, and where the engine is weighed

Not "the nose is short" — the first reading of this section was taken on
the wrong datum (§0). Re-based to the windscreen base the nose is ~right;
what was wrong is WHERE THE ENGINE'S MASS SITS. The frame places a nose
engine by a prop rule, `engX = −(0.18 + 0.32·propR)` (60_gen_spec.js
~4536): flange 0.49 m ahead of the windscreen base, mass node at −0.24 —
INSIDE the cowl deck, the block behind the firewall. The cowl page draws the
same engine with its flange 1.17 m ahead (`CAGE_ENG.units[0].at`, measured
through the headless join) and its centre 0.9 m ahead — exactly where a
172's is (FS −16). The join measures that station (`M.engUnits`) and writes
`x` for pusher/wing mounts only; 61_gen_frame.js:531 says "the join measures
a drawn engine's flange" and for a nose mount it does not. 144 kg weighed
0.67 m aft of where it is drawn = the CG 0.1 m (7 % MAC) aft on THIS build,
and something like it on every nose-engine build in the game (§6.1).

The rest of the 47 % was the wing 0.19 m forward of the type's station
relative to the cabin and the tail volume: Vh 0.37 (Sh 2.65 × arm 4.37 /
(18.4 × 1.68)) against the 172's ~0.63; the stab trim of 4.6° said the
tail was working hard.

### 3.3 The wing: 14 % too big and rigged like a Cub

Root chord 1.95 m (the 172's 1.63, constant to ~45 % semispan then tapering
to 1.12); incidence 4° (1.5°), washout 1.5° (3°), dihedral 3° (1.7°).

### 3.4 Drag CdS 0.65 vs 0.47 (+37 %): the cabin 18 % too wide, the legs bare

Frontal area 1.80 m² (the 172's ~1.4); the gear row 0.146 m² with the leg
fairings off (the 172R has them, on the mains and the nose strut).

### 3.5 Fuel: 28 L in the nose against 212 L in the wings

The tool had to ballast 259 kg of freight aft to reach MTOW; a 172 carries
150 kg of fuel at the CG.

### 3.6 Empty weight −14 % (632 vs 736)

PERF STUDY §4.1, unchanged: the mass model does not scale with the aeroplane
(the c172 CARD read −44 %; this build, drawn full-size, −14 %; corrected
−10 %). Not a dial on the build.

### 3.7 The runs, and the landing

The analytic roll read −25 % where the flown run read −8 %; the flown
landing roll +129 % was the pilot's approach and braking (§5.4).

### 4.1 The 160 hp engine without a registry change

The editor's identity rule (2026-09-01, "never call an engine wrongly"): a
physics dial that deviates from the applied preset flips the row to CUSTOM
ENGINE (`engCatalogueAudit` → `engPreset` = the custom index, 41 here), the
join writes `engines[0].custom` = the engine model's own resolve of the
dials, and that is what flies. The IO-360 dials at 2450 rpm resolve to 118.6
kW / 144 kg — the 172R's 160 hp engine to 1 %, 6 kg over. The registry key
falls back to `a65_sensenich74` (the prop-diameter default only; the prop is
set explicitly and its thrust is synthesised from D and power). The name on
the plaque is "custom Flat (boxer) 4-cyl 5.9 L". Honest, and it needs no row.

### 4.2 The engine station — G445.1, the join writes the nose flange

The first delivery carried `engines[0].place.dx = −0.67` as a stopgap. It
could not survive: a garage LOAD runs the spec back out through the join
(`BUILD_SYNC`), the merge REPLACES the engines array whole, and the join
wrote `place: {dx: 0}` for a nose mount — so the offset was gone the moment
the file was opened in the game. G445.1 does what §6.1 asked: the join
writes the station the engine layer drew (`M.engUnits[0]`, kind 'nose') as
`engines[0].x` — as it always did for the other mounts — and 60_gen_spec
takes a measured nose `x` over the prop rule (`engX = e.x + place.dx`; the
rule stays for a bake with no drawn engine). On this build the flange lands
at −1.10 m and the mass node at −0.76 m from the windscreen base, where the
drawn engine's centre is. Checked in the browser after a stock load: the
loaded spec carries `x: −1.10`.

### 4.3 The crank carries the strut (G140): 0.55 broke the wing

The taper was first put at 0.55 of the semispan (the 172's) — and the
sandbags read 124 % of yield (HELD — over yield, no certificate). The strut
attaches at the crank, so that moved the strut outboard; 0.45 (the 172's
actual strut station) reads 73 %. Between them the reading is
DISCONTINUOUS: 0.42 → 67 %, 0.45 → 73 %, 0.48 → 129 %, 0.55 → 124 % — the
strut's outboard end snapping to a different spar bay. Worth a GATE LOAD
case: a strut station sweep should be monotone.

### 4.4 The two dials that do not do what their names say

- `wgDx` is CAGE z (forward positive): −0.3 moved the LE 0.24 m AFT, i.e.
  by ~0.8 of its value after the planeScale. Sign and gain worth a label.
- `noseLen` grows the cowl deck FORWARD of the windscreen base and moves no
  measured station (all of them are aft of that ring); it changes the drawn
  length only. Two passes were spent learning that; the datum note in §0 is
  the lesson.

### 4.6 v2 — the user's eye in the shed (2026-09-20, after the stock load)

"Is the wing well positioned for sure? I would have seen it a tad more
fore." It was not: v1 had put the LE 0.58 m aft of the windscreen base on
a rule of thumb (pilot 0.3 m behind the LE) where the 172's LE is 0.15 m
aft of it (FS 26 vs FS 20) and the pilot's hips 0.35 m behind THAT. `wgDx`
+0.13 puts the LE at 0.154. The balance then reads NP 65 %, CG 50 %,
STATIC MARGIN 15 % — the 172's — and the stab trim 0.5° (neutral; v1's
31 % margin had the tail working at −2°). The CG PERCENTAGE reads 50
because the model's airframe mass sits ~0.8 m further aft than a real
172's (§5.5), not because the wing is wrong; the margin and the trim are
the type's. The take-off run reads 286 m flown (POH 288), lift-off 28.3
m/s, the circuit complete. With the CG forward the mains at 1.47 m carried
32 % of the weight on the nose (a 172: ~20 %): `s1Z` 1.15 puts them at
1.27 m, nose load 23 %, nose-over 17°.

"It also tends to shake quite violently on take off." Measured on the
trace: from 21 m/s the heading error swings ±1.9° at a ~1 s period with
the nosewheel (`dr`) hunting it and the bank following at ±0.4° — the
trike steering loop (43_pilot.js `kP = 3.2·kS/kM`, `kS` floored at 0.30
above ~25 m/s), not the airframe. It was not the nosewheel LOAD: v2
carries more of it and weaves less (±0.8°). Bank jitter per 0.1 s: as
found 0.036, v1 0.23, v2 0.12. A pilot-track item (A8/P2): the loop's
floor is a fixed number where the nosewheel's authority is not.

"The seats are clipping through the cabin": the side-by-side rule places
each seat centre at min(seatGap, halfW − 0.25); with the cabin at 0.98 m
that is the rule's own maximum and the lower seat corner meets the
rounded belly. `seatGap` 0.18 pulls the pair in. "Delete the option for
the hanging safety belts": done in G445.1 (row, parts list, design tiles,
drawing).

"It needs an aft bulkhead": the cage BUILDS one — 28 faces, full cabin
height, 1.79 m aft of the windscreen base, at the aft passenger pillar
just behind the rear seats (cageInterior, `intBulk` 1). What the user saw
is a drawing question (where it is looked at from), owed a look with the
user's eye on the exact view.

### 4.5 The take-off the pilot rejected, and the elevator

The first corrected build (everything above but the elevator) FLEW A
CIRCUIT on the bench and was REJECTED by the pilot: lift-off at 29.9 m/s and
10.8° of pitch, then 4 s at 0.5 m with the nose easing to 8° and vs 0 —
"cannot climb out, putting it back down". Not a tail strike (the tail post
touches at 11.3°). The mechanism, from 43_pilot.js: the rotation phase
allows the attitude servo 0.30 of integrator stick (`rotateIMax`), LIFTOFF
drops it to 0.15, and a nose-heavy aeroplane with a 31 % static margin and a
26 % elevator cannot hold its lift-off attitude on 0.15 — it sinks back to
8°, where this wing (1.5° of incidence now, not 4°) does not lift 1017 kg at
33 m/s. The 172's elevator is 44 % of its tail (15.7 of 35.7 ft²); at
`stHinge` 0.40 (chord fraction 0.44) it rotates, lifts off at 29.6 m/s and
flies the circuit. A real correction to the type, found by a real symptom —
and a note for the pilot track: the LIFTOFF integrator cap is a fixed
number where a real pilot holds what the aeroplane needs (§6.3).

## 5. What is still off on the corrected build, and whose it is

### 5.1 Static margin 31 % (NP 62 % MAC) — the model's neutral point

With the type's tail volume (Vh 0.57 vs the 172's 0.63) the model's NP sits
at 62 % MAC; a 172's is ~48 %, its margin ~15 % at this CG. The model has
no fuselage destabilising moment to speak of (a 172's fuselage moves the NP
forward by ~10 % MAC) and its d(eps)/d(a) reads 0.34. A 172-dimensioned
aeroplane is therefore over-stable in this model — which is what made §4.5
happen, and what the as-found build's small stab had been compensating.
Not a dial; a model item for the aero chantier.

### 5.2 Drag CdS 0.56 vs 0.47 (+18 %) — the family's number

Body 0.163, gear 0.093 (faired), screen 0.022, junctions 0.017, cooling
