# SKIS: one ski landing gear for every aeroplane (13 Nov 2026)

Chantier SKIS (cloud session). The brief, the user (13 Nov): a GENERIC ski gear, "one main type and a few options, not a
list of 10"; dimensions adjustable; PHYSICS STOPS ABERRATIONS (the ski's area sets the contact pressure, small skis on
a heavy plane sink); no wheel brakes on straight skis; mass, drag and price paid; drawn by the gear generator, WYSIWYG.
The user's reference (13 Nov, photographs and the maker's sheet): the **Summit Aircraft SS2300** - "the Pilatus uses
similar skis". A0's research: `futureDesigns/SKI-REFERENCES-2026-11-13.md` ([SRC]/[EST] labels kept below).

## 1. One ski, three mountings, two options

The ski is ONE shape (core `29c_ski.js` `skiPlan`, the drawing and the physics read the same function):

- the shell, `L` x `W` (the SS2300: 18 x 79 in), on white UHMW **bottom skins** grown by a margin (22 x 81 in);
- the **wheel's cutout**: the aft **beam** keeps `beam` (0.57) of the width on the inboard side, the cutout starts
  `notchAt` (0.49) of the length from the toe, outboard - the tyre sits in it beside the ski; `beam` 1 is a straight
  plank (no cutout);
- the toe a half-round of the full width, upturned `tip` over the last 16 %; the heel square with small radii;
- the **pedestal** on the beam's spine over the axle (0.585 of the length from the toe).

Measured off the user's top-view photograph against its 22 x 81 in skins.

**THE CUTOUT IS THE TYRE'S** (the user, 13 Nov: "the wheel has to be able to go down through the little opening ...
so it can be put up and the wheel touches, or pulled down and it touches instead of the wheel"). The wheel-ski moves
the ski past its tyre, so the opening must take the tyre at its LOWEST through the ski (skis up: the tyre `prot` under
the sole): `skiPlan` moves the cutout's start forward of the axle by the tyre's chord at the deck's top (and at the
skins' top, past their margin), a 3 cm clearance and the fillet - the row's start holds only when it is further forward
already - and stands the tyre's inner wall 15 mm outboard of the beam's skins. The tyre's width is the DRAWN carcass's
(`skiTyreW`: the gear layer's standard / tundra / slim profile). GATE SKIS 4 walks the tyre through every height of the
skins and the shell against the outline beside it (the Carbon Cub's 31" tundra: the cutout from 39 % of the length).

The mounting (`gear.ski.kind`, the row `gearSki`):

| kind | what | tyre | brakes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `plain` | straight ski: the wheel comes off, the pedestal on the axle ("option to run as straight ski") | none | **none** |
| `wheel` | the wheel beside the ski in its cutout, `prot` (3 cm) below the sole (the SS2300's own) | carries on a hard surface, sinks till the ski bears in snow | the wheel's |
| `retract` | the hydraulic wheel-ski (the PC-6's): the ski slides from `prot` above the tyre's bottom (up) to `drop` (6 cm) below it (down) in `time` (15 s) | up: as `wheel`; down: clear | the wheel's, skis up |

Options: the **tail / nose ski** (`third`: a straight plank, its wheel through a slot, steered with it; plain when the
mains are plain) and the **heel wheel** (the SS2300's 4 in wheel, drawing). Rows (the gear layer's "skis" group, every

`skiL`/`skiW` 0 = sized: the SS2300 scaled to bear its share of the weight at `pDesign` 8 kPa (written back as rows).

## 2. The physics (30_solver `skiGround`, per ski node, per substep)

Each main's and the third unit's node carries up to two ELEMENTS below it: the ski's sole (`hS`) and the tyre (`hT`).
On HARD ground each meets the ground on the node's own spring (`KGn`); the tyre's share goes back to the wheel pass
unchanged (rolling, brakes, `twLine` - a tyre element is its wheel, bit for bit); the ski's share slides on its
sole's mu (`SKI_HARD_MU` by surface: wet grass 0.22, pavement 0.35), with a breakout under 0.3 m/s, **no brake**, and a
side force that builds with the slip angle (`cn` 4/rad, the runners as a keel) to its Coulomb limit.

On SNOW-MUD's soft layer (section 3) each element sinks in its RUT by SNOW-MUD's Bekker law p = k z^n over its own area
(the ski's bearing area from the planform; the tyre's footprint): so **the area sets the pressure** and the sinkage.
The rut remembers (a lifted ski leaves it; it never springs back); running forward relaxes it over one bearing length
(fresh snow). The motion resistance is SNOW-MUD's compaction w k z^(n+1)/(n+1) plus the bulldozing (`plough`); the layer
stiffens with speed as (1 + (v/vP)^2): past the hump the ski planes ("keep enough power to stay on top"). Measured (GATE
SKIS 5, SNOW-MUD's powder with the fresh top): the Carbon Cub's sinkage 28.3 / 18.2 / 11.1 cm on 0.6 x 0.12 / 1.1 x 0.2 /
1.7 x 0.3 m skis; on its SS2300s it lifts off in 91 m; on 0.6 x 0.12 m skis it bogs and the pilot aborts. (SNOW-MUD's
settled powder alone gave 4.0 / 2.2 / 1.4 cm and the small skis flew off first - the aberration the fresh top ends.)

The ski's pitch about its axle (`out.skiPitch`, what the drawing shows; the contact acts through the pivot): on the
ground along the surface, in the air the rigged incidence (`inc`, the check cable), never past the safety cable's
`droop` (-20 deg). The wheel-ski's selector `ctl.ski` (1 down, 0 up); unset, it follows the surface under the mains
(down over a soft layer) and a reset snaps to it.

## 3. THE SOFT GROUND IS SNOW-MUD'S - one version (agreed 15 Nov, A0: "agree the contact API with them, not two versions")

The first cut (13 Nov) had its own `world.soft(x, z)` record and presets. SNOW-MUD built the soft ground the same day as
SURFACE codes, and the skis now read THEIRS - `world.soft` and `SOFT_GROUND` are gone (GATE SKIS 9 checks it):

- the world's SURFACE code under the contact: 8 packed snow, 9 powder, 10 wet snow, 11 firm mud, 12 soft mud;
- the LAYER `GROUND_SOFT[code] = { k, n, zMax }`: Bekker's p = k z^n, the layer's depth over the firm base;
- a ski bottom's friction `GROUND_SKI_MU[code] = [kinetic, breakout]` on every code (water: none - the water's pass);
- the laws `groundSinkPlate(S, N, A, w)` (a ski's sole) and `groundSinkWheel(S, N, b, D, A)` (a tyre), the scratch
  `GROUND_SINK`, and the readouts `sim.out.softZ` (the deepest sinkage) / `softF` (the compaction drag), which the skis
  write too (the dust's plough reads them).

THE RUT - THE MOVING DATUM (SNOW-MUD's named next step, built here for the ski elements). Their laws are quasi-static; a
contact in the solver is a spring, and the snow's k (10^6-10^7 Pa/m^n over a square metre) would be a stiff loop. So each
ski element (the sole; a wheel-ski's tyre) keeps its rut's depth zc: its ground is the snow's top lowered by zc, met on
the node's own spring (the gear's stiffness, unchanged); zc deepens - filtered, 0.06 s - toward SNOW-MUD's sinkage of the
load it carries (groundSinkPlate over the sole's area; groundSinkWheel for the tyre), plastically (it never springs back
while the element stands), never past zMax (then the firm base), and relaxes as the element runs into fresh snow (over one
bearing length). The motion resistance is SNOW-MUD's compaction w k z^(n+1)/(n+1) at the rut's depth, + the plough.
A wheel-ski's tyre in its rut pays its drag there: the wheel pass adds no second sink drag for that node.

What only a ski needs are OPTIONAL FIELDS ON SNOW-MUD'S OWN ROWS (their ask: no second table; set from 29c, SNOW-MUD's k,
n, zMax and ski untouched; no fields: SNOW-MUD's law exactly - the mud, the hard ground): `skis` 1 (a ski's ground), `vP` the planing speed (the layer stiffens as 1 + (v/vP)^2: the hump), `lat` the edges' side grip, `plough`
the bulldozing, and `fresh` - THE TWO-REGIME SNOW, SNOW-MUD's named cut ("a row field, absent = this law"): the top z of
the layer is virgin snow { k ~2e5 Pa/m^n (the published fresh snowpack), n, z } over SNOW-MUD's settled law. A sole at
5-15 kPa compacts it first and sinks decimetres (the references' "a well-sized light ski sinks ~8 cm in fresh powder"; a
ski too small for its aeroplane sinks through it); a TYRE does not read it (at 50-300 kPa it collapses at once, which
SNOW-MUD's fit at the tyres holds), so a wheel - and a wheel-ski's tyre - is SNOW-MUD's law exactly.

| code | the ski's fields on GROUND_SOFT | fresh top | GROUND_SKI_MU (SNOW-MUD's) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 packed | vP 12, lat 0.35, plough 0.6 | none (rolled) | 0.04 / 0.08 |
| 9 powder | vP 7, lat 0.30, plough 1.0 | k 2e5, n 1.5, 25 cm | 0.12 / 0.24 |
| 10 wet | vP 6, lat 0.35, plough 1.4 | k 4e5, n 1.2, 12 cm | 0.10 / 0.20 |
| 11, 12 mud | - (no ski belongs there; the selector stays up) | - | 0.25-0.30 (SNOW-MUD's estimates) |

The wheel-ski's selector unset lowers the skis where the row under the mains carries `skis` (the snow), raises them
elsewhere. A bare aluminium bottom on the snow slides at 0.35 / 0.40 (`SKI_ALU_MU`).

THE PLUME AND THE VISUAL SINKING ARE SNOW-MUD'S (their ask: one hand edits dust.js and app.js syncDustFx - a ski source
kind: no tread release, the plough / bow wave across the ski's width x its own sinkage, a touchdown burst, powder off the
toe and sides). What this session hands them is DATA:
- `def.refs.skis` = one record a ski, in the solver's order (the mains, then the tail / nose ski):
  `{ nodes: [node], w, L, fwd, aft, main, kind, posed }` - the node the contact acts at (the pedestal on the axle), the
  width and length over the skins, the toe `fwd` m ahead of the node and the heel `aft` behind (along the body), main or
  not, the mounting; `posed: true` - THE PHYSICS ALREADY SINKS THE NODE INTO ITS RUT (the moving datum), so a visual drop
  of the airframe must not sink a ski a second time. The key only with skis;
- `out.skiSink[k]` (m, the unit's rut: the deeper of its sole and its tyre) and `out.skiLoad[k]` (N) in the same order,
  and SNOW-MUD's `out.softZ` / `softF` (soft frames only, the same `softLive` flag).
The drawn ski is posed off its axle node, which rides down into the rut: the ski sits sunk by zc below the snow's top.

## 4. Mass, price, drag (29c `skiResolve`, frame 61, aero 62)

Mass a ski = rigging + mounting x (k0 + kA x the skins' area), by the shell's material: aluminium (the Aero line),
glassfibre (the Airglas LW3600), carbon (solved so the SS2300 weighs its 27 lb with rigging: 12.25 kg); mounting x1
plain, x1.5 the wheel beside, x2 the wheel-ski (+ its power pack). A plain ski REPLACES its wheel (mass, price, drag of
the wheel off). Price [EST] catalogue orders of magnitude. Drag a ski: the nose (the upturn and the deck over the
skins' width) at 0.5, the sole's friction, the rigging, the pedestal; x1.15 beside, x1.6 the wheel-ski [EST, the
C185's 130 -> 115 kt]. Measured: the PC-6's aluminium wheel-ski kit 149 kg (the references: 100-150); the Carbon Cub's
power to cruise 0.565 on its tundras, lower on plain skis (the 31" tyres' drag off), higher on wheel-skis.

## 5. The drawing (tools/_gear_gen.js `ski`, `skiRigRun`; tools/_cage_gear.js; app.js ski rigs)

Lofted on the core's planform: the bottom skins; the monocoque shell (a screwed flange, the walk-on deck, the beam's
faceted spine rising to the pedestal); the non-skid walk; the screws at a pitch; the pedestal (base plate, two
gussets, the axle's socket; the axle running on from the hub; the wheel-ski's slide and jack); the lugs and the cable
along the top; the heel wheel in its fork; the finish (white, red, yellow, black, carbon weave). The rigging runs - the
front spring and cable to the fuselage's lower flank ahead of the leg, the aft check cable - are two-end members: the
flown rig stretches them from the airframe's fitting to the ski's lug as the ski pitches and slides.

## 6. Honest cuts and open points

- The contact acts at the axle node; the SS2300's centre of pressure stands ~0.1 m inboard of the wheel (the wheel in
  the cutout): the small roll couple is not modelled.
- No freeze-in (the parked bond), no ski-drag pick, no overflow (the references' suggestions, next).
- The drawn ski lies level in the garage; in flight it is posed by the solver's pitch; a slope steeper than the
  safety cable's stop clamps the drawing, not the contact.
- A wheel on a soft layer (no ski) still reads the grass rows: SNOW-MUD's.
- Twin booms: no tail ski (two tail contacts).
