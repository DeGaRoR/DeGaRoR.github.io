# SKIS: one ski landing gear for every aeroplane (13 Nov 2026)

Chantier SKIS (cloud session). The brief, the user (13 Nov): a GENERIC ski gear, "one main type and a few options, not a
list of 10"; dimensions adjustable; PHYSICS STOPS ABERRATIONS (the ski's area sets the contact pressure, small skis on
a heavy plane sink); no wheel brakes on straight skis; mass, drag and price paid; drawn by the gear generator, WYSIWYG.
The user's reference (13 Nov, photographs and the maker's sheet): the **Summit Aircraft SS2300** - "the Pilatus uses
similar skis". A0's research: `futureDesigns/SKI-REFERENCES-2026-11-13.md` ([SRC]/[EST] labels kept below).

## 1. One ski, three mountings, two options

The ski is ONE shape (core `29c_ski.js` `skiPlan`, the drawing and the physics read the same function):

- the shell, `L` x `W` (the SS2300: 18 x 79 in), on white UHMW **bottom skins** grown by a margin (22 x 81 in);
- the **wheel's cutout**: the aft **beam** keeps `beam` (0.57) of the width on the inboard side, the cutout starts
  `notchAt` (0.49) of the length from the toe, outboard - the tyre sits in it beside the ski; `beam` 1 is a straight
  plank (no cutout);
- the toe a half-round of the full width, upturned `tip` over the last 16 %; the heel square with small radii;
- the **pedestal** on the beam's spine over the axle (0.585 of the length from the toe).

Measured off the user's top-view photograph against its 22 x 81 in skins. The mounting (`gear.ski.kind`, the row
`gearSki`):

| kind | what | tyre | brakes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `plain` | straight ski: the wheel comes off, the pedestal on the axle ("option to run as straight ski") | none | **none** |
| `wheel` | the wheel beside the ski in its cutout, `prot` (3 cm) below the sole (the SS2300's own) | carries on a hard surface, sinks till the ski bears in snow | the wheel's |
| `retract` | the hydraulic wheel-ski (the PC-6's): the ski slides from `prot` above the tyre's bottom (up) to `drop` (6 cm) below it (down) in `time` (15 s) | up: as `wheel`; down: clear | the wheel's, skis up |

Options: the **tail / nose ski** (`third`: a straight plank, its wheel through a slot, steered with it; plain when the
mains are plain) and the **heel wheel** (the SS2300's 4 in wheel, drawing). Rows (the gear layer's "skis" group, every
one absent at its default - `GEN_SKI_ROW_OFF`, `CAGE_DEFAULT_ABSENT`): `gearSki skiL skiW skiTip skiAt skiInc skiT skiTL
skiTW skiSkin skiBeam skiNotchAt skiProt skiMat skiColor skiHeelW` and the wheel-ski preview `skiPos` (never saved).
`skiL`/`skiW` 0 = sized: the SS2300 scaled to bear its share of the weight at `pDesign` 8 kPa (written back as rows).

The drawn ski IS the flown ski: the join writes `spec.gear.ski` from what the gear layer drew (`CAGE_GEAR.skis`).

## 2. The physics (30_solver `skiGround`, per ski node, per substep)

Each main's and the third unit's node carries up to two ELEMENTS below it: the ski's sole (`hS`) and the tyre (`hT`).
On HARD ground each meets the ground on the node's own spring (`KGn`); the tyre's share goes back to the wheel pass
unchanged (rolling, brakes, `twLine` - a tyre element is its wheel, bit for bit); the ski's share slides on its
sole's mu (`SKI_HARD_MU` by surface: wet grass 0.22, pavement 0.35), with a breakout under 0.3 m/s, **no brake**, and a
side force that builds with the slip angle (`cn` 4/rad, the runners as a keel) to its Coulomb limit.

On a SOFT layer (section 3) each element sinks by the **plastic Bekker law** p = k z over its own area (the ski's
bearing area from the planform; the tyre's width x its chord at that depth): so **the area sets the pressure** and the
sinkage. The rut remembers (a lifted ski leaves its rut; it springs back only `kEl`); running forward relaxes the memory
over one bearing length (fresh snow). The motion resistance is the compaction plus the bulldozing, b k z^2 (1/2 +
plough); the layer stiffens with speed as (1 + (v/vP)^2): past the hump the ski planes ("keep enough power to stay on
top"). Measured (GATE SKIS 5): on powder the Carbon Cub's sinkage 33.8 / 15.3 / 8.0 cm on 0.6 x 0.12 / 1.1 x 0.2 / 1.7
x 0.3 m skis; on SS2300s it lifts off in ~87 m; on 0.6 x 0.12 m skis it sinks to the bed and the pilot aborts.

The ski's pitch about its axle (`out.skiPitch`, what the drawing shows; the contact acts through the pivot): on the
ground along the surface, in the air the rigged incidence (`inc`, the check cable), never past the safety cable's
`droop` (-20 deg). The wheel-ski's selector `ctl.ski` (1 down, 0 up); unset, it follows the surface under the mains
(down over a soft layer) and a reset snaps to it.

## 3. THE SOFT GROUND - the interface with SNOW-MUD

A world MAY answer `world.soft(x, z)`: `null` for hard ground (every world today), else a SOFT RECORD - the layer over
the hard bed, `terrainH` being its TOP:

```
{ kind,          'snow' | 'ice' | 'mud' (a label)
  depth,         m, the layer over the bed (0: a hard surface, only the friction rows count)
  k,             Pa/m, the pressure per metre of sinkage on loading (p = k z); 0 = no sinkage
  vP,            m/s, the planing speed (the layer stiffens as (1 + (v/vP)^2)); 0 = none
  kEl,           Pa/m, the elastic rebound on unloading (the layer is plastic)
  plough,        the bulldozing term over the compaction's b k z^2 / 2
  muSki, muSki0, muAlu,   a UHMW sole's kinetic and breakout mu, a bare aluminium sole's
  latSki,        a ski's side grip
  roll, muBrake, muLat }  a TYRE's three rows on the layer (GROUND_SURF's)
```

`SOFT_GROUND` (29c) holds presets from the references (powder, packed, wet, ice); SNOW-MUD owns the snow and may
replace them. The per-element law (`SKI_SOFT.press / resist / tyreA / kAt / relax`) is pure and generic - a wheel on
snow or mud reads the same functions (the wheel pass does not yet; that is SNOW-MUD's to wire, behind its own gate).
GATE SKIS hands a preset to a world shim over HOME (no snow surface exists on master yet).

## 4. Mass, price, drag (29c `skiResolve`, frame 61, aero 62)

Mass a ski = rigging + mounting x (k0 + kA x the skins' area), by the shell's material: aluminium (the Aero line),
glassfibre (the Airglas LW3600), carbon (solved so the SS2300 weighs its 27 lb with rigging: 12.25 kg); mounting x1
plain, x1.5 the wheel beside, x2 the wheel-ski (+ its power pack). A plain ski REPLACES its wheel (mass, price, drag of
the wheel off). Price [EST] catalogue orders of magnitude. Drag a ski: the nose (the upturn and the deck over the
skins' width) at 0.5, the sole's friction, the rigging, the pedestal; x1.15 beside, x1.6 the wheel-ski [EST, the
C185's 130 -> 115 kt]. Measured: the PC-6's aluminium wheel-ski kit 149 kg (the references: 100-150); the Carbon Cub's
power to cruise 0.565 on its tundras, lower on plain skis (the 31" tyres' drag off), higher on wheel-skis.

## 5. The drawing (tools/_gear_gen.js `ski`, `skiRigRun`; tools/_cage_gear.js; app.js ski rigs)

Lofted on the core's planform: the bottom skins; the monocoque shell (a screwed flange, the walk-on deck, the beam's
faceted spine rising to the pedestal); the non-skid walk; the screws at a pitch; the pedestal (base plate, two
gussets, the axle's socket; the axle running on from the hub; the wheel-ski's slide and jack); the lugs and the cable
along the top; the heel wheel in its fork; the finish (white, red, yellow, black, carbon weave). The rigging runs - the
front spring and cable to the fuselage's lower flank ahead of the leg, the aft check cable - are two-end members: the
flown rig stretches them from the airframe's fitting to the ski's lug as the ski pitches and slides.

## 6. Honest cuts and open points

- The contact acts at the axle node; the SS2300's centre of pressure stands ~0.1 m inboard of the wheel (the wheel in
  the cutout): the small roll couple is not modelled.
- No freeze-in (the parked bond), no ski-drag pick, no overflow (the references' suggestions, next).
- The drawn ski lies level in the garage; in flight it is posed by the solver's pitch; a slope steeper than the
  safety cable's stop clamps the drawing, not the contact.
- A wheel on a soft layer (no ski) still reads the grass rows: SNOW-MUD's.
- Twin booms: no tail ski (two tail contacts).
