## 1. THE PHYSICS — LANDED, G268 (it was NOT applied; now it is)

TAIL CHANTIER 2 (G214-G219, G233, G235) gave the CONVENTIONAL tail a two-spar
truss per surface (HF/HR/HB stations on the stab's trapezoid, VF/VR/VX up the
fin), strips riding those bays with the deformed chord, the drawn control
chords as the rear spars, and a place in GATE LOAD's rig (`GEN_LOAD_SURFACES`
finds the stab by HF/HR, the fin by VF/VR). The twin boom's tail is still cut
2's (G181): the stab is FOUR NODES (the two boom tails' tops and bottoms) with
two strips a side hung on them, each fin ONE NODE (`FIN`, `FIN2`) with one
strip, no spars, no control chord, and the rig cannot load either (no HF/HR,
no VF/VR). G266 made the booms hold their tail; the tail itself is the old
approximation.

What "applying the chantier" means here, in order:

1. **The stab between the booms as the P4 truss.** Its root is not a fuselage
   post but the two boom tails: the trapezoid spans boom to boom (`hSpan` =
   2·boomX + the panel's own overhang), stations at the wing's pitch, front
   spar at `tailSparFront`, rear spar at the elevator's chord, the HB chord
   under each station, the root pair of each side tied to ITS boom's tail
   triangle (T, I, O) instead of TPB/TPT + the last ring. The strips then
   ride the bays as the conventional's do (62_gen_aero's `P.TAIL` branch) —
   the `P.BOOMS && P.FIN2` branch retires.
2. **Each fin as the P4 truss up from its boom tail** (VF/VR/VX per side,
   the apex at the drawn fin's top), the rudder chord as the rear spar; the
   fin strips a bay up each post.
3. **The load rig** then finds HF/HR and VF/VR on a twin boom by itself; the
   tail-arm instrument (GATE FLEX, G266) stays as the boom's own gauge.
4. **The ventral fin's area** (G267 draws it, measures nothing): the fin
   layer's `finMeasure` is the sheet above the boom; the ventral is a scaled
   mirror the join does not see. Either the layer measures it into `Sv` and
   `vHeight` (below-the-boom height is a new row the frame would need — a VX
   pair under the boom tail) or it is declared a fairing. Ruling owed.

G268 built exactly this (see HANDOVER): the stab as ONE continuous truss
boom to boom (the two-halves-plus-carry-through idiom is a hinge between
booms — it folded), each fin up from its boom tail, both in GATE LOAD, the
ventral fins counted into Sv by the join (the user's ruling).

## 3. THE POD'S AFT PROFILE ON A TWIN BOOM IS STALE

The join writes `fuse.profile` / `tailBot` / `tailTop` off the pod's tail
post (`zPost`); a twin-boom pod ends at the bulkhead and has none, so the
fragment carries no profile and the garage's merge keeps THE LAST ONE — on
the user's build the earlier conventional taper (0.63 / 0.92 at 2.8 m), not
the drawn pod. G266.2 made the snapshot calibrate on the merged spec so the
visual and the frame at least agree; the frame's aft body is still not the
drawn one. The join should measure the pod's own aft rings (the aero aft's
cone, `aeroAftLen`) into the profile when there is no post, and write the
rows EMPTY when there is nothing to measure rather than let the merge fill
them. Half a session.

## 4. `place.dx` IS COUNTED TWICE ON A CAGE BUILD

The design bake writes `wings[0].place.dx = −wgDx` for the pre-join spec
(G185.11, so a headless archetype flies its nudge); the join's `M.wingXLE`
already contains the drawn nudge (it is measured off the drawn wing); the
resolver adds `place.dx` on top. On the user's build the frame's wing sits
0.3 m aft of the drawn one. Fix: the join zeroes `place.dx` when it writes a
measured `xLE` (one line, plus GATE JOIN's assertion), and the archetype
gate's headless path keeps the nudge because no join runs there. Do it with
the tail-gap gauge open: the 65 mm x in G266's number is mostly this.

## 5. THE WING'S CENTRE SECTION (the user's last paragraph) — LANDED, G274

(Rulings taken as recommended: (a) physics — the strip and the cover take
the skin factor, `removed` drops both; (b) one row, all positions; (c) the
cabin's width at the spar station. Owed: `topFuselage` does not re-close
the cabin over the wing; the plaque's S is still the planform's.)

Today: `wgCentre` (solid / glass / open / cutout) shows for a high or parasol
wing only, and a mid wing runs straight through the cabin. Asked for:

- **the WIDTH of the centre section** as a row (`wgCentreW`, metres,
  default = the cabin's own width at the spar station, `cab.halfW·2`);
- **its construction**, one discriminator: `plain` (the wing's skin through),
  `glass` (the section's upper AND lower skin glazed — a Blériot's view),
  `top glass` (the upper skin glazed, the lower the wing's), `top fuselage`
  (the upper skin is the FUSELAGE's — the wing passes under the cabin floor
  and the cabin's own belly/side closes over it: the low-wing-through-the-
  belly and the mid-wing-through-the-cabin cases), `removed` (the centre
  section is cut out: two half-wings on a carry-through), and the two
  half cuts the user added (2026-09-12: "we also need to keep the current
  cut half, cutting the aft half, and let's add an option cutting the fore
  half instead"): `aft cut` — today's `cutout`, the trailing half of the
  centre section cut back over the cockpit — and `fore cut`, the leading
  half cut instead (the view down past the leading edge, a Storch's);
- **a LONGERON drawn in the cut** for the last three: the carry-through the
  frame already builds (61_gen_frame ties the two spar roots across the cabin
  — WF/WR root pairs and the box caps) drawn as a member: the tube or the
  box beam the construction says (steel tube on a tube fuselage, a spruce
  box on a wooden one), its section the spar's, from one cut face to the
  other — so what is drawn is the load path the physics already flies.

Where it lands: the wing layer's skin (`_cage_wing.js` emits the wing's skin
from the frame def; the centre section is the root bay between ±zRoot, so
the cut is a face filter by |x| < wgCentreW/2 and a material swap per half:
`skyWindows` for glass, the fuselage's `body` for `top fuselage`); the
fuselage cage is untouched for `plain`/`glass`/`removed`; `top fuselage`
needs the cabin's belly (low) or side band (mid) to CLOSE across the wing's
root bay — that is a cage-topology change (the fuselage's rings between the
front and rear spar stations are cut by the wing today) and the reason this
is its own session. The longeron is a fitting-class member (`_fit_gen`'s
placer, the cabane's steel row for a tube, a box for wood), rooted on the
frame's own carry-through nodes.

Rulings owed before building:
- (a) does `removed` change the PHYSICS (the frame's root bay loses its
  skin — cover mass and the strips' root area — but keeps the carry-through
  members), or is it drawing only? Recommendation: physics — the strips'
  root area comes off `Sw`, the cover off the mass, and the plaque says so.
- (b) `top fuselage` on a HIGH wing is the deck; is the row shown there
  (today's `wgCentre` = solid/glass/open/cutout has the deck cases) or do
  the two rows merge into one discriminator across all positions?
  Recommendation: merge — one row, all positions, the options that do not
  apply hidden by the row's own `when`.
- (c) the centre width's default on a mid wing: the cabin's width or the
  root chord's? Recommendation: the cabin's width at the spar station, read
  off the cage (AF.halfWAt), so the cut faces meet the cabin side.
