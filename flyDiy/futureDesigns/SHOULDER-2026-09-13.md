## What it is

An L-section sheet swept along the WINDOW TRANSITION of each flank: the line
where the glass (or the open sky of a cut canopy) meets the skin below it.
The top leg lies at the transition, its outboard edge ON the skin and its
inboard edge W inboard; the vertical leg hangs H down from a real bend. It is
one closed solid per segment (thickness t, bend radius, caps), so it shades
like sheet metal: flat across the bend, smooth along the sill.

Parameters (`DEF` in the generator): W 0.07, H 0.20, t 0.002, bendR 0.004,
drop 0.003 (the top sits this far under the traced transition so the glazing
bead rides on it), inset 0.0004 (outboard edges sit this far inside the
sampled skin), gap 0.004 (the shut line against the dash), run 'pilot' |
'cabin'.

## The three rules that make it clean

1. **Positions, not indices.** Cut doors, cut panes and the canopy duplicate
   the skin's vertices; exploded parts carry `cutOff`. Every edge is keyed by
   its AS-BUILT position, so "glass above / skin below" is read across parts.
   A door's segment is emitted as the door's own part (doorKey + cutOff): it
   explodes with the door (screenshot 15).
2. **The skin is sampled, not assumed.** `makeSkinSampler` answers "where is
   the flank at (y, z)" by intersecting the skin faces in the (z, y)
   projection. The top leg's outboard edge and the underside's edge are placed
   on it; the vertical leg is closed onto the skin wherever the body comes
   inboard of the leg's pocket face within H (a round-bottomed pod, a deep H).
3. **Watertight by construction, checked by position.** A loft of closed
   profiles + two ear-clipped caps, vertices split only at sharp edges (the
   normals are explicit), windings made coherent across position-shared edges
   and the volume made positive. `shoulderCheck` counts open and over-shared
   edges per part; the battery requires 0 / 0 on every configuration.

## Limits

- Forward: the dashboard's aft face — the dash's own minimum z (its outline
  is creased at weight 3, so that plane is exact) — minus the shut-line gap.
  The cap butts the dash the way a trim panel does. Without a dash the
  shoulder runs to the A-pillar.
- Aft: `run 'pilot'` stops at the cabin pillar behind the pilot (pilCabB);
  `run 'cabin'` continues through the passenger bays (their band glass has the
  same transition). On the stock build the door owns the whole pilot sill
  (z 1.93..3.23): the shoulder is one door segment there, with the door's own
  jamb gaps at both ends.
- Recommendation: **default 'pilot'**. The cockpit is where the pilot looks at
  the sill; the pax bays with drawn (knife) windows have no band transition
  anyway, and with band glass the option is one word.

## The throttle

The swept bar (pivot, direction, length, 25° arc, 20 x 8 mm section, the
knob) is intersected with the plane of each leg; the convex hull of the
crossing points, offset by the clearance with rounded corners, is the slot,
punched through the leg as a framed patch (outer rectangle on the loft's own
stations, inner outline, zipped by angle) plus the slot's wall. Whichever leg
the lever crosses gets the slot; a lever that never leaves the pocket is
reported BURIED; a slot that would run off the leg's edge is clipped
(Sutherland-Hodgman) and reported.

Measured on the stock build IN THE BENCH (its own floor estimate, the door
outline's lowest point): the WALL throttle sits 0.59 m under the sill; in the
game, whose crew `floorAt` is the measure, it is **0.30 m under the sill**
(planeScale 0.745), still below a 20 cm leg — no slot, correctly. Raised 0.40 m with
W 0.05 the lever comes out through the FACE (the lever leans inboard only
9.5°, so its section in the vertical plane is long: the slot is ~9 cm); with
W 0.07 the 16 cm lever ends inside the pocket (BURIED); raised 0.55 m it
comes out through the TOP as the user described (screenshots 04, 05, 01c,
01d).

**Recommendation for integration:** when the shoulder is on, mount the wall
throttle's quadrant plate on the shoulder's vertical leg instead of the skin
(`A.wallAt` answering the leg's x), lever entirely in the cabin: no slot, no
buried lever, and it is what real side-wall quadrants do. Keep the slot for
the user's own placements (thrX/thrY/thrZ), where it is exact.

Two traps found and fixed on the day:
- the bubble's released band strip is nearly FLAT at its top row, so "the
  skin face is below the edge" must compare the face's own centroid (with a
  tolerance), not the edge's y — the door's segment lost its middle third
  otherwise;

  duplicate points and an open patch; clipping the convex outline properly
  keeps it a simple polygon.

## The door inner panel (the second option, landed)

`panelBuild` in `tools/_shoulder_gen.js`, off by default (`door panel` rows
under the sill shoulder). The rules that make it clean: the base is the
door's INNERMOST surface sampled (skin, linings, the construction's own
posts — never the dash, which the panel is notched round), the outline is
the door's exact extent along each line y (`extentAt`) inset by the margin,
following raked and stepped edges, convex corners rounded; a closed slab
with a 45° chamfer and a proud map pocket embedded 1.3 mm into it; role
`doorPad` -> `pleatLeather` (a `pleat` bake, normal-mapped rolls with a
stitched groove) and any finish from the paint UI; the crew's wall lookup
mounts the throttle, fuel selector and trim wheel on its face; GATE
SHOULDER's 13 panel rows and CLIP's `trim` skin hold it.
