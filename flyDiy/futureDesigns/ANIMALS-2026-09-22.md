## 1. THE PIPELINE

Fourth table, fourth baker — the rule the props, the chars and the totems already follow:
*the table is the only authority, the baker bakes exactly it, the gate reads the table out of the
python source and asserts the shipped payload against it.*

```
assets/animals/<file>.glb        the delivered asset, byte-for-byte, gitignored
tools/animals_table.py           THE DECLARED TABLE: species rows + clip rows + SOURCES
tools/animal_prep.py             the baker: skin -> media/geo/animals, maps -> media/tex/animals,
                                 one manifest src/animals/<key>_animal.js, clips beside it
src/core/55_animal_codec.js      decodeAnimal / decodeAnimalClip / ANIMAL_REG  (pure JS)
tools/animal_lod.js              poses the skin at the row's `lodPose`, cuts 2-3 levels with
                                 prop_lod.js's quadric decimator, writes src/animals/animals_lods.js
                                 as a PROP PACK (group `animal`) — props.js places it
src/viewer/animals.js            THE ONE FACTORY: the skinned instance, the clip player, the ladder
src/viewer/animal_run.js         THE BEHAVIOURS: herd / pod / flock, from one hotspot record
tools/_animal_check.js           GATE ANIMALS
tools/_animals.html              the bench: shelf / one / clips / ladder
```

### 1.1 Why a fourth codec, and why the LODs are props

`52_char_codec.js` is *nearly* right — a skinned mesh, a joint tree, inverse bind matrices — and
it was read line by line before this one was written. Three things it cannot carry:

- **A clip with translations.** `char_prep.bake_anim` keeps rotation channels only, on purpose:
  the ATD owns a seated pilot's root and a translation would fight the seat. An animal's clip is
  the whole animal — a bear's `Walk` moves its hips, its spine *and* its root, and 34 of its 76
  channels are translations. The animal clip carries **T, R and S per animated joint**.
- **Several clips per subject, picked at runtime.** A character wears one clip at a time from a
  table of two. A bear has eighty-one and chains them.
- **A rigid child.** The elk's antlers (`Chifre`) are not skinned: they are a mesh under a joint.
  The manifest carries the mesh's `node`, the codec hands it back with `jt`/`wt` absent, and the
  factory parents a plain Mesh to that bone instead of binding a SkinnedMesh.

The **levels are prop-format on purpose.** A level is a rigid mesh wearing exactly the maps the
skinned mesh wears — which is the prop payload's shape to the byte, and means `props.js`'s one
material factory, its texture cache, its `THREE.LOD` placement and `propWarm`'s fetch all apply
with no new code. `ANIMALS.place()` builds the ladder itself: level 0 is the skinned instance,
levels 1..n are `propMesh()` of the cut props. The pack's keys are `<key>_l1`… and carry `lodOf:
'<key>'` — a key that is *not* a prop, so `premises_ui`'s palette filter (`!props[k].lodOf`)
already hides them and nothing else ever looks the base up.

### 1.2 The rest pose is a DECLARED pose, not the bind pose

"*similar volume and color*" is a real constraint, and the bind pose is not always a pose the
animal is ever seen in (the bear's is 1.69 m tall — it is up on its hind legs). So a row declares
`lodPose = (clip, t)`: the level is skinned at that instant of that clip, so the silhouette the
ladder swaps to is the silhouette it swaps FROM. `bear` and `elk` stand at `Stand_Idle_01` t=0,
the whales at their swim loop's mid-glide, the bird has no level at all (162 triangles).

### 1.3 Scale is declared as a LENGTH, never as a factor

Every one of these files is in a different unit (cm for the land animals and the orca, ~metres
for the blue whale, ~cm for the bird). A `scale: 0.01` row is a fact about an exporter; a
`length: 2.2` row is a fact about a bear. The baker measures the rest mesh along its own long
axis and scales it to the declared length, and prints both, so a re-export that changes units
changes nothing downstream. The declared lengths (nose to tail, the animal's own literature):
bear 2.2 m, elk 2.5 m, blue whale 25 m, orca **8.5 m** (a mature bull - 7.5 was a female's, and
it was worn by every animal in the pod), bird 1.1 m across the wings. A fifth field, `spread`,
says how much the individuals of one hotspot differ either side of that: a pod is a bull, a
matriarch and calves, never five of one size.

## 2. THE BEHAVIOURS

One record — a **HOTSPOT** — makes a herd, a pod or a flock. It is one `objects` entry of the
premises record (contract v1.17, `kind: 'animal'`), which is what makes them placeable in the
world editor with no new layer and no new save version:

```js
{ id, kind: 'animal', key: 'elk', x, z, n: 6, r: 90, yaw, dy: 0, mood: 'graze' }
```

`n` individuals inside radius `r` of (x, z); `n: 1, r: 0` is one animal placed by hand. Every
individual is seeded off `(id, index)` — the author's hand is never undone by a re-sow, the
contract's rule 5.

### 2.1 Land — the clip machine (bear, elk)

A state machine over the delivered library, not a motion authored anywhere:

    IDLE ──(dwell)──> BROWSE ──> IDLE ──> WALK ──(arrive)──> IDLE ──> LIE ──> IDLE
     └── Stand_Idle_01/02, Stand_Breathing_01     Stand_Eating_01     WalkSlow/Walk

The rules, all of them cheap and none of them risky:

- **The transitions are the asset's own.** `Trans_Stand_to_WalkSlow`, `Trans_Stand_to_Lying`,
  `Trans_Lying_to_Stand`… are played through when the pair exists, and otherwise the two clips
  cross-fade over 0.25 s. A cross-fade is a nlerp per joint between two sampled poses — the same
  arithmetic `char_prep`'s `slerp` already does, at runtime, on 37 joints.
- **Walking follows the terrain.** The individual holds a world position and a heading; a walk
  step advances it at the CLIP'S OWN ground speed (measured at bake time: the root translation
  per second, printed in the report), so the feet do not skate. Its y is `ground(x, z)` and its
  attitude is the ground's normal, low-passed — the same `tiltToGround` the placed props use.
- **Nothing walks into the sea or up a cliff.** A step is refused (and the heading turned) when
  the target is under water, steeper than 28°, or outside the hotspot's radius.
- **Mostly idle, as asked.** The dwell weights are 0.55 idle / 0.25 browse / 0.15 walk / 0.05 lie.

### 2.2 Sea — the loom, the circuit, the blow (blue whale, orca)

The body keeps its delivered swim loop, whose rate follows the animal's speed. Everything else is
the ROOT — a path, a depth and an attitude — which is where "take no risk" puts it:

- **The circuit** is a slow ellipse about the hotspot centre, radius `r`, one revolution in
  2–4 minutes, with the heading tangent to it.
- **The dive cycle** is one scalar `d(t)` ∈ [0, 1] on a period of 40–90 s: the animal rises to the
  surface, runs along it for 15–25 % of the cycle (the back and the dorsal out of the water), then
  arches down to `depth` (12 m for the orca, 30 m for the blue whale) and cruises there. `d`
  drives the y, and its DERIVATIVE drives the pitch, so the arch is the motion, not a keyframe.
- **THE LOOM.** A submerged animal is drawn — that is the ask (*"we should somehow see through the
  water a bit"*). The water writes depth, so a submerged mesh is simply not there; the loom is a
  second draw of the SAME level-1 geometry with a `loom` material: `depthTest: false`,
  `depthWrite: false`, drawn after the water (`renderOrder` above it), its colour the animal's own
  albedo absorbed by the water it is under — `exp(-k·depth)` on the sea's own extinction, alpha
  falling from 0.55 at the surface to 0 at 2.5 × `depth`. Above the surface the loom is off and
  the real mesh draws. Nothing about the water shader changes; a shape rises out of the green and
  becomes an animal, which is exactly the read.
- **THE WAKE AND THE SPLASH** are the aeroplane's own, asked for by name. AS BUILT the field also
  turns on for THEM: it used to run only under a floatplane, so a surfaced animal within 150 m of
  the eye sets `WATER.field.ask` and app.js honours it for half a second (GATE ANIMALS rule 9).
  Within the interaction field's 192 m box:
  a `press` stamp of the animal's beam under the part of it that is wet, a `foam` stamp along the
  back while it runs on the surface, and a `ring` stamp + a `SPRAY` burst when the flukes or the
  head break the surface. Budgeted: at most 4 of the field's 16 stamps a frame go to animals, the
  nearest first.
- **THE BLOW** is the house's chimney smoke, generalised — see §3.

### 2.3 Air — the flocks

*"splattered, and they should probably just go across the map."* Two uses of one payload:

- **AMBIENT**: the world (not a premises) keeps up to four flocks alive within 1.2 km of the eye.
  A flock is born on a bearing outside that ring, crosses on a straight track with a slow sine in
  heading and height, and dies when it is 1.6 km out. `n` birds in a V, each with its own phase on
  the 0.67 s flap loop and its own small offset — one `THREE.InstancedMesh`… no: five skinned
  birds would be five skeletons, so the flock is ONE skinned bird per member up to 9 members, and
  the level-1 prop for the rest (they are 20 px on screen).
- **PLACED**: a hotspot with a bird key is a colony — the flock circles the hotspot instead of
  crossing, at `r` and 40–120 m up.

## 3. THE PLUME — the house's smoke, generalised

`_house_gen.js` builds a chimney's smoke as a bag of camera-facing quads on a rising, spreading
column, with `F.MAT.smoke` and `F.SMOKE_U.uSmokeLit` — the uniform `render_premises` dims by the
day's exposure so the column is a haze lit by the sky, never a grey pillar at night (G449). That
recipe is lifted into `src/viewer/plume.js` unchanged in look and given three parameters the
chimney's own call now passes: the column's **rise**, its **spread** and its **life**. The
chimney is then one preset (`chimney`), the whale's exhale another (`blow`: 8 m of rise in 1.2 s,
a 3 m spread, a 4 s life, one shot on the trigger rather than a standing column), and the same
`uSmokeLit` uniform rides in the same pool, so a blow at dusk is lit exactly like a roof's smoke.
GATE ANIMALS asserts the chimney's own numbers are unchanged by the move — a generalisation that
moves the village's smoke is a regression, not a feature.

## 5. THE GATE

`GATE ANIMALS` (`tools/_animal_check.js`, core tier) holds, reading `animals_table.py`'s own
source:

1. the table = the shipped manifests (keys, order, labels, credits, lengths);
2. every baked skin decodes, its joints resolve, its weights sum to 1 ± 1e-3, and its rest mesh
   measures the DECLARED length ± 1 %;
3. every declared clip exists in its GLB, is sampled on a uniform grid, and its joints are a
   subset of the skin's;
4. the ladder: every level stands in for a real animal key, wears its maps, is inside its
   predecessor's triangle count, and its posed bbox is within 4 % of the skinned rest bbox
   (*"a similar volume"*, asserted rather than eyeballed);
5. the behaviours run headless: 600 steps of a herd, a pod and a flock on a synthetic ground —
   no NaN, nobody under the terrain, nobody outside the hotspot, the clip machine reaches every
   state, the sea cycle surfaces and dives;
6. the premises record round-trips an `animal` object through compose / issues / save / load;
7. the plume's chimney preset is byte-identical in its numbers to the house's own.
