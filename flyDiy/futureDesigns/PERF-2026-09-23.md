# PERFORMANCE STUDY + FIVE TIERS (2026-09-23)

The user's brief: "5 levels in the end: potato computer, was good 5 years ago, current, gamer and ultra. This
computer is considered gamer, and it should hit consistently the 60 fps with the visual settings more or less as
they are now ... the version end of last week, as of Wednesday, was showing an acceptable performance on the
generated map which I would like to see reproduced on the new Jolene island".

Reference machine (= the GAMER tier): RTX 3080, i7-13700KF, 64 GB, Chrome (ANGLE / Direct3D 11), display
5120 x 1440. Branch `claude/performance-profiling-optimization-1095f7`.

## How it was measured

- `tools/frame_perf.js` (the gate-less measurement): a fresh headless Chrome on the real GPU, no vsync, Jolene,
  three places - STAND (the airfield, parked aircraft, village), FOREST (a dense stand) and 300 m over the field
  ("sea" in the tool) - 120 frames each, median + p90. `--size WxH` (new) for the big screens.
- The scratchpad rig (see the memory note): per-draw census, scene-level GPU timer, CPU time of `render()`,
  WebGL call counts, a CPU sampling profile, translated-HLSL scan, pinned-camera A/B shots with a CONTROL pair.

## THE FINDING THAT MATTERS: at 1080p the frame is CPU-bound

At 1080p on this machine the frame is bound by **three.js's JavaScript per draw call** (about 7 us a draw in
`render()`: culling, sorting, uniform uploads - 30 000 WebGL calls a frame), not by the GPU:

- the scene at 50 % render scale costs the same as at 100 %; the terrain at a 3x coarser mesh the same;
- `performance.now()` round `renderer.render(scene)` equals the TIME_ELAPSED "GPU" time of the scene pass: the
  GPU query spans the gaps while JavaScript feeds it;
- the GPU process is idle (0.4 of a core), the page's main thread is 98 % busy.

So at 1080p the levers are the NUMBER OF DRAWS and the scene-graph walk, and a faster GPU buys nothing. At
5120 x 1440 it turns GPU-bound (fill), where the render scale helps. Per-draw GPU timers (a query round every
draw) serialise the GPU and mislead; trust scene-level time and `render()` CPU time.

## What was fixed (none of it changes the picture unless stated)

GPU side (wip 1-3):
- far terrain cut to the eye by screen error (1 px), 8.5 M -> ~3 M triangles; honest bounding spheres on every
  chunked InstancedMesh (r186 culls on the union of the geometry sphere at every instance - nothing culled);
- reversed float depth instead of log depth (log depth writes gl_FragDepth: no early-Z anywhere);
- the AA target's stencil never resolved (ANGLE emulates the depth-stencil resolve: ~12 ms at 8x);
- draw order: occluders (impostors, cards, cover, craft) before the ground, the ground nearest first (r186 sorts
  opaques by material id BEFORE depth: the ground was shaded whole under everything);
- impostors merged per chunk (the sheets as texture-array layers); the lake cut's discard only where a lake is;
  the class layer's blur not computed under the tint that replaces it; the deep sea bed not painted.

This round:
- **the splat read mip 0 everywhere**: ANGLE's D3D back end makes a gradient-free copy (SampleLevel 0) of every
  texture-sampling function called inside a loop that holds a `continue`; the candidate loop did. Now an if-block.
  (Also a quality fix: the far ground shimmered.) The rest of the programs checked: only the mist march has one,
  harmlessly.
- the detail band (150-900 m) samples a set shared by the near and far triplets once (bit-exact);
- the hex-tiling cut of wip 2 REVERTED to hex everywhere: it showed the tiles repeating as a checker, and with
  the mips honoured it saved nothing;
- the premises ground patch in 512 m blocks with a 2/4/8/16 m LOD by measured error (1.5 M -> ~0.5 M triangles,
  culled per block, skirts);
- CPU: the static premises (houses, lots, roads, ground - 14 000 objects) out of the per-frame matrix walk; hidden
  animals too; distant houses (under 16 px) draw their walls and roof only (their smallest bags by area - 8 % of
  the house's surface - hidden; glass, lit panes, smoke, props kept): 1 023 -> ~600 draws at 300 m; the lots'
  fences and yard patches merged per 256 m cell (261 -> 20 draws). Control shots: identical within the animation's
  own noise.
- options: RENDER SCALE (bicubic upsample, 50-100 %), TERRAIN DETAIL (1/2/3 px for the ring and the far cut; the
  ring in 16 x 16 chunks below exact), the FIVE TIERS.

## What costs and what does not (1080p, gamer, one option flipped)

At 1080p almost nothing in the menu moves the frame, because the frame is the draw count: 8x -> 4x MSAA saves
~4 ms of GPU but the CPU bound hides most of it; clouds, mist, water, glare, sway, canopy, reflections: within the
noise (+-1 ms). Shadows off removes ~800 draws (the whole shadow pass). The density options re-stream the forest.

What the frame IS at 300 m (1080p): the ground's shading (the splat ~ half of the GPU time), ~2 000 draws of which
houses ~600, the aeroplane ~500 with its shadows, poles ~260, smoke cards ~180.

## What we cannot afford (yet) and the next chantiers

1. **Draw count** is the ceiling at 1080p. The three big offenders cannot be batched as built, because every
   object carries its OWN materials:
   - houses: ~11 bags each, each its own finish (~20 per-house uniforms: dirt line, weathering, AO...). HLOD:
     a far form per house (or per block) with the finish baked to vertex data or an atlas: est. 2-3 ms.
   - props (poles, fence stretches, cars): one THREE.LOD per prop; instancing per (prop, rung): est. 1-2 ms.
   - the aeroplane (242 meshes, 141 materials, 77 under 5 cm, all casting) and the parked aircraft (~120 materials
     each): merge the static parts per finish; the cabin interior only when the eye can see in: est. 2-4 ms.
2. **The big screen**: 5120 x 1440 at gamer is 36-41 ms at 100 % (GPU-bound, and twice the draws); the render scale is the lever
   (see the tier table), the splat is the dearest pixel.
3. **The garage** (15 ms at 1080p): 6 shadow lights re-rendered every frame (5 836 shadow draws) and photoscans
   of 100-200 k triangles each (compressor ~200 k, bandsaw / panel saw ~100 k, jointer 72 k, a cardboard box 34 k,
   person_koky 332 k at L0). Cache the spot maps (static lamps; re-render on a change), LOD-cut the scans.
4. VRAM: the impostor arrays are ~710 MB.
