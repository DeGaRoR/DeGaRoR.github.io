The user's brief: "5 levels in the end: potato computer, was good 5 years ago, current, gamer and ultra. This
computer is considered gamer, and it should hit consistently the 60 fps with the visual settings more or less as
they are now ... the version end of last week, as of Wednesday, was showing an acceptable performance on the
generated map which I would like to see reproduced on the new Jolene island".

Reference machine (= the GAMER tier): RTX 3080, i7-13700KF, 64 GB, Chrome (ANGLE / Direct3D 11), display
5120 x 1440. Branch `claude/performance-profiling-optimization-1095f7`.

## How it was measured

- `tools/frame_perf.js` (the gate-less measurement): a fresh headless Chrome on the real GPU, no vsync, Jolene,
  three places - STAND (the airfield, parked aircraft, village), FOREST (a dense stand) and 300 m over the field

- The scratchpad rig (see the memory note): per-draw census, scene-level GPU timer, CPU time of `render()`,

## THE FINDING THAT MATTERS: at 1080p the frame is CPU-bound

At 1080p on this machine the frame is bound by **three.js's JavaScript per draw call** (about 7 us a draw in
`render()`: culling, sorting, uniform uploads - 30 000 WebGL calls a frame), not by the GPU:

- the scene at 50 % render scale costs the same as at 100 %; the terrain at a 3x coarser mesh the same;
- `performance.now()` round `renderer.render(scene)` equals the TIME_ELAPSED "GPU" time of the scene pass: the
  GPU query spans the gaps while JavaScript feeds it;
- the GPU process is idle (0.4 of a core), the page's main thread is 98 % busy.

So at 1080p the levers are the NUMBER OF DRAWS and the scene-graph walk, and a faster GPU buys nothing. At
5120 x 1440 it turns GPU-bound (fill), where the render scale helps. Per-draw GPU timers (a query round every
draw) serialise the GPU and mislead; trust scene-level time and `render()` CPU time.

## What costs and what does not (1080p, gamer, one option flipped)

- **Ground textures**: what costs is not how many textures the library holds, it is how many are BLENDED PER
  PIXEL: each terrain type mixes 2-3 sets by a noise mask (each hex-tiled, colour + normal, triplanar on slopes),
  in the 150-900 m band twice. One set per type plus cheap variation (hue/value noise, already in the shader)
  would save ~8-10 ms at 5120 - the user's "variations rather than new textures" is exactly the right lever.
- **Tree models 8 -> 2-3**: ~0 fps. The far forest is one draw per chunk whatever the species (texture-array

- **One grass model**: the 20 tuft models are ~90 draws in a forest view: ~0.5 ms.
- **Debris**: 36 distinct models (the two stick packs are 32 of them) x every visible block, and every one casts a
  shadow: ~600 draws in a forest view. Fewer models (3-5 twigs, 3 sticks) or no shadow from the twigs is the cheap cut.

## Tried and rejected: "what cannot show is not sampled"

A set whose height (<= 1) plus its mask weight cannot reach the heaviest set's has a blend weight of exactly 0, so
in principle it need not be sampled (same pixels, ~1 set per pixel instead of 3). Under ANGLE/D3D11 any branch
whose condition depends on a texture read makes the shader compiler emit far worse code (flattened, derivatives
kept): the candidate-level skip alone made the 300 m frame 8 ms SLOWER, the full version 17 ms slower and a
ten-minute compile. The only way through is explicit-gradient sampling (textureGrad) everywhere - slower per sample
on NVIDIA and a large refactor; not done.

## G533 - the new player kept out of harm's way: the auto render scale and the first launch's tier (2026-09-23)

- **Auto render scale** (aa_resolve.js AUTO, the menu's 'auto' step; every tier but ultra): every 30 frames the median
  frame is read; over 1.08 x the 60 fps budget the scale steps DOWN one of the menu's steps (1 / .85 / .75 / .67 / .5,
  at most every 2 s: a step rebuilds the target) and the next reading must show it PAID - a CPU-bound frame (1080p on
  the reference box) is not faster with fewer pixels, so an unpaid step is taken back and the scale left alone 30 s,
  doubling on each repeat (5 min at most): the picture is never blurred for nothing. It steps UP when the frame holds
  60 and the scene pass's GPU time (a timer query round it) says the next step's pixels fit 80 % of the budget; an up
  step that then misses holds the ups a minute. The decision is a pure function (`autoDecide`) that GATE AA drives with
  simulated frames: CPU-bound 22 ms ends at 100 % (probes at 0.5 s, 31 s, 92 s), GPU-bound 8 + 32 k^2 ms comes down
  85 -> 75 -> 67 -> 50 % in 8 s and holds, a fast machine never leaves 100 %.
- **The first launch picks its tier** (gfx_settings tierProbe): no saved choice -> gamer boots; 4 s after the roll-out
  6 s of frames are read (the auto scale acting) and the UNTOUCHED default steps down if it still misses: current to 30 ms,
  5 years ago to 45, potato beyond. Kept in flydiy.gfx.auto, said in the menu, run once; a player's own pick is never
  overridden. Checked live (a contended box: 39.3 ms -> '5 years ago').
- **Rigs stand down**: a headless or driven browser (HeadlessChrome / webdriver) - the gates, frame_perf, the scratch
  rigs - keeps a fixed 100 % and no probe unless ?autoscale=1 / ?autotier=1.
- **The saved choice migrates once** (pv 2): a pref saved ON a preset (old names mapped: low -> 5 years ago, medium /
  high -> gamer) becomes that preset as it is now (auto scale, soft bloom); a custom mix keeps its options, its 100 %
  (the old default) becomes auto.
- Not measured for its benefit here: the box was saturated by other sessions (GPU 100 %, 9.7 of 10 GB) - the
  controller's numbers above are the gate's simulation and a live correctness run at 1280 x 720.

## G567 - the ground's COLD compile: diagnosed, explained on screen; the fix not landed yet (2026-09-24)

A cold roll-out (fresh Chrome profile, Jolene, gamer box): screen ~232 s - compile step capped at 20 s, then the
'frames' step blocked 196 s. The six ground programs (4 Lambert, 2 Standard: splat_ground.js in the ring, twin,
fine, outer, outer-dry materials) linked in ~216 s each (in parallel); one alone 111 s.

THE CAUSE, measured with Chrome's own d3dcompiler_47 called directly on the dumped "COMPILER INPUT HLSL"
(WEBGL_debug_shaders; ANGLE's flags; `#define ANGLE_ENABLE_LOOP_FLATTEN` as ANGLE passes it): HLSL has no calls, fxc
inlines every call site. sMat's three branches (near triplet, far triplet, the band) = 12 sSet -> 36 sTile (triplanar)
-> 144 sFetch (hex: 3 + the plain tile's 1) = 288 texture reads of straight code inside the candidate loop: 412 KB of
bytecode, 12 453 instructions, 337 samples. Trimmed by hand (4 compiles in parallel): base 177 s; no band 60 s; one
triplet 14 s; no hex 17.5 s; one triplet + no hex 5.4 s; no set sampling at all 2.3 s (the floor of the rest).
The compile time is superlinear in the inlined size.

THE FIX (branch of the jolene-animals-pipeline worktree, WIP commit 7e293eb1 "splat sample chain as loops"): the sets sampled in ONE
loop over deduplicated (layer, scale) slots, the triplanar planes a loop, one fetch site: cold roll-out 232 -> 29 s
(compile 12.5 s, frames 1.2 s), one program 111 -> 4.4 s. The picture: A/B in ONE page (the old block spliced back by
onBeforeCompile under another cache key) - ~1 % of pixels differ, median 5-6 levels, isolated, mean luminance equal
to 0.01, high-frequency energy 1-3 % LOWER (no new sparkle). BUT THE GROUND IS 1.5-2x SLOWER ON THE GPU (scene render,
2560 x 1440, alternated x3 in one page): 300 m 47 vs 24 ms, 100 m 54 vs 28, 30 m 71 vs 54. NOT the cause (each tried,
each as slow): the hex loop (unrolled: 3 fetch sites), indexed arrays (named registers instead), the plane loop,
register pressure (3 slots), a uniform loop bound, sampling in the loop at all (the first set straight - 300 m never
enters the loop - still slow). Old code + the new sTile alone = as fast as old. So the cost is in how sMat gathers
its sets (sSlot + sPut/sAt + sBlend on the gathered slots), not in the fetches. Next: bisect sMat from the old side.

## G568 - the ground's cold compile, FIXED: 208 -> 34 s, the frame and the picture unchanged (2026-09-24)

The G567 bisection's answer: the GPU cost was a LOOP HOLDING THE SETS NESTED IN THE CANDIDATE LOOP - every such
variant drew 1.5-2x slower even where the inner loop ran zero times; the old straight code and "old + the new sTile"
did not. So no nested loop: ONE straight triplet (sTriplet: three sSet) inlined once, and the candidate loop runs
two passes per terrain type (sMatPass: pass 0 the near triplet, pass 1 the far; one of them completes the type -
under the fade only the near, past it only the far, in the band both; a far triplet identical to the near is not
sampled twice). sTile's plain tile shares the first hex vertex's fetch: 27 fetch sites instead of 144.

49/53/56 vs 50/54/54 ms, 100 m 62/58/49 vs 65/61/46, 30 m 55/55/31 vs 53/53/32 - equal. Picture: 100 m mean diff
0.008 (control 0.002), 30 m 0.010 (control 0.008). GATE SPLAT 3 holds the shape: sMatPass and sTriplet one call site
each, sSet / sTile / sFetch at most three, no loop inside the sample chain (each red on a broken copy).
