# POST-FX — where the frame stands, what it can carry, what it costs; the shed and the world; what r186 and the field offer

2026-09-21, on the user's ask: *"where do we stand regarding post FX? Like bloom, colour
grading (on top of the managed colour profile), etc. I know we have anti-aliasing already.
Can you do a complete study of what we have, what we can have, and the associated
performance costs? Also, please study the differences in rendering/lighting conditions
between the shed and the world, there shouldn't be any ultimately. And anything new and
fancy, recent, in our recent three.js packages, or in some github repo, or in the
literature?"* Rulings taken while scoping: measure the cheap candidates on this GPU; fix
the IBL regression now, minimally; and (the one that shaped the landed half) *"all the
effects should have on/off switches in the graphics menu; let's not take too much risk
here, and ensure we can perfectly operate as of today when turning the post fx off."*

Measured on this tree unless marked INFERRED. What landed with the study: G448 (the six
switchable passes, `src/viewer/post_fx.js`, GATE POSTFX, `tools/frame_perf.js`,
`tools/postfx_shot.js`) and G448.1 (the `scene.environmentIntensity` fix). Everything
else here is a finding or a proposal.

## 0. THE STATE — there was no post-processing, by rule

**The frame, pass by pass** (world, tier `full`, clouds `half`; `src/viewer/app.js` loop
at `aa.render(...)`, `aa_resolve.js render()`):

| # | pass | target | where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ATMO sky-view table | `G.rtSky` 192×108 HalfFloat | `atmo.js` update, every frame |
| 2 | ATMO aerial-perspective atlas | `G.rtAP` 2048×546 HalfFloat (scissored rows) | every frame |
| 3 | cloud shadow bake (a tile in the AP atlas) | `G.rtAP` | every other frame as the drift moves |
| 4 | reflection probe (world: dome + cap → PMREM; cabin probe A6) | PMREM cube | when the sun moved 1.5°, `day.version`, the clouds' dirty |
| 5 | far shadow cascade (impostor quads only) | `FAR.rt` 2048² packed depth | every 4th frame / 20 m |
| 6 | canopy cover mask | `COVER.rt` 2048² mipmapped | on 20 m moved / dirty |
| 7 | cloud march (the resolve's PRE hook) | MRT ½ or 1× HalfFloat, reads the previous frame's depth | every frame |
| 8 | three's shadow maps (sun 1024-4096², `sunNear` 512-2048², the shed's 5 spots) | inside `render()` | every frame |
| 9 | **the scene** — with the cloud composite as a quad at its own depth (G436.7) | `S.rt`: HalfFloat, 8× MSAA, 1.25× ss, stencil, depth texture when asked | every frame |
| 10 | the resolve blit (Catmull-Rom 5×5 when ss>1, else one tap) + TPDF dither | the canvas | every frame |
| 11 | the sun flare: seven additive quads, `toneMapped:false`, occlusion by 5 CPU rays | the canvas | every frame |
| 12 | **(G448) the post passes, only while a GRAPHICS row is on** | own targets, the canvas | every frame |

**Where colour happens — the two rules that decided this study.**

1. **The resolve target is display-space.** `aa_resolve.js` sets `isXRRenderTarget = true`
   and `texture.colorSpace = SRGBColorSpace` on its half-float MSAA target, so r186 treats
   it like the canvas: every material tone-maps (Cineon, the §4j ruling) and sRGB-encodes
   *on the way in*, per its own `toneMapped`, and the blend unit composites in display
   space. Why: the first linear-target cut re-composited the 29 transparent materials in
   linear light (the +68-code table in `aa_resolve.js` THE TARGET IS DISPLAY-SPACE); the
   W0.5a r186 port hit the same wall from the other side (every `toneMapped:false`
   material loses its opt-out under a post tone map). Consequence: **there is no linear
   HDR radiance anywhere after the scene pass.** A "physically" correct bloom, an
   exposure-fusion tonemap, an SSR that adds radiance — all of them want it.
2. **The resolve owns no colour** (GATE AA `tools/test_aa.js:113-140`): no tone curve, no
   transfer, no exposure in the blit. One implementation of each exists, in the renderer.

What was tried and taken out: SSAO (G56/G57 built as its own pass — colour target + depth
texture + two ShaderMaterials, half-res then full-res 5-tap normals + bilateral — G60
deleted); a full-screen HDR bloom (G419 S7: *"no full-screen HDR bloom pass. It would
re-architect the resolve ... and cost a frame-wide blur on a 1.25x target"* — the corona
in the dome shader and the flare quads are what reads as the sun's glare). The exposure is
an authored schedule (`light_rig.js` EV_KNOTS, ~20 stops noon→night); the "eye pass" was
owed since G417.

**Tone curves on the GRAPHICS menu:** ACES, AgX, Neutral (Khronos PBR), Reinhard, Cineon
(default), linear — all r186 core, both renderers. AgX and ACES desaturate the liveries'
yellows and greens toward white at the top (the filmic behaviour); PBR Neutral keeps a
base colour's hue and saturation under grey light and only rolls the top — the
e-commerce curve, worth an eye on the liveries; Cineon's cooler shadows are the ruling.

**The bundle:** `tools/vendor_three.js` bundles `export * from 'three'` and nothing else —
no `EffectComposer`, no pass, no TSL display node, in either bundle. Every post effect in
this game is hand-written GLSL over the pass's hooks.

## 1. THE COST TABLE, MEASURED

Instrument: `tools/frame_perf.js` (new, D1's tool). Every `renderer.render()` call is
wrapped in an `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` TIME_ELAPSED query and tagged by the target
it draws into; three's shadow-map pass runs inside `render()` and queries cannot nest, so
it is timed in a second sub-run that wraps `renderer.shadowMap.render` alone. 120 frames
per configuration after tree_perf's settle; median and p90 per tag; the raw (unqueried)
frame beside the queried one so the timer's own cost is visible. Places: the stand (where
the roll-out puts the aeroplane — the shed, the field, the premises), the densest forest
stand at 110 m (tree_perf's), over the sea at 300 m, and the shed (`--garage`).
`--probes "name=js;;..."` runs named configurations (a GRAPHICS row on) at each place;
`--quiet` waits for the GPU to be under 15 % BEFORE Chrome is spawned (the rig's own
Chrome renders flat out and would never let it read quiet afterwards) and every row
records `nvidia-smi`'s utilisation just before it.

**A trap the study fell into, recorded for D1:** the box is shared. Five sessions were on
it, two headless rigs (`imp_audit.js`, `a7_livery.js`, later `water_shot.js`) rendering with
`--disable-gpu-vsync` at 100 % GPU for the whole session and two gate batteries. A
TIME_ELAPSED query measures wall time on the GPU's timeline, so another context's work
lands inside it: the same stand measured 86, 241, 350 and 418 ms across four runs an
hour apart. **A GPU timer number taken while `nvidia-smi` says 100 % is not a number.**
`frame_perf` refuses to hide it (the row prints `GPU WAS n % BUSY BEFORE`) and `--quiet`
waits. Under heavy foreign load the queries also come back DISJOINT and are dropped (two
rows of the effects run carry no post tag at all for that reason). No quiet window came
in this session; the tables carry the contended numbers and say so.

**Two findings that are D1's, not post-FX's, but were in the way:**
- Headless, the roll-out screen's parallel compile idles out (`boot: nothing landed for
  20 s`, the overlay stays ~62 s, `CONTINUE ANYWAY`); `tree_perf.js` (2026-09-14, before
  LOADING S3) rolls out and sleeps 8 s, so its numbers since S3 were taken under the
  overlay's `holdRender` — a table of the shed. `frame_perf` waits for `BOOT.state`.
- The island's far terrain at the stand: 16 Lambert meshes of 0.2-2.2 M triangles each,
  12 M in the scene, 14.5 M in the frustum before the trees, ~4-7 k draw calls at the
  stand (the premises' 1807 props). That is the frame's body; no post pass changes it.

## 2. WHAT WE CAN HAVE — three ways in, and what each buys

**(a) Display-space post, after the resolve — what G448 is.** Reads the tone-mapped
frame, draws onto the canvas or its own targets. Right for anything that is *about the
picture*: a look, a vignette, a fringe, an auto-exposure loop, god-rays (a 2D effect
anyway), a cheap bloom. Wrong for anything that is *about radiance*: a bloom that should
know the sun is 10⁵× the sky gets a threshold on a curve that already clipped it; an AO
that should only touch the ambient term darkens the sunlit paint. Cost: nothing when off
(no hook, no target — GATE POSTFX), ~0.05-3 ms per row when on. Risk: none to today's
frame, by construction.

**(b) A linear-HDR post chain — the S7 objection, argued out. TAKEN AS G448.3 the same day (the `compositing` row, linear by default): the pictures put the re-judging at 0.5-1.5 codes mean and confined it to the canopies, the roof panels and the bulbs - see the HANDOVER entry.** Render the scene into a
LINEAR half-float target with `toneMapping = NoToneMapping`, do bloom / AO / SSR / TAA in
radiance, tone-map once in the blit. This is what every engine does and what three's TSL
`RenderPipeline` does. What it costs here: (1) the 29 transparent materials, the editor
overlays, the x-ray and the glazing re-composite in linear light (the +68-code table) —
every one re-judged, or the transparents drawn in a second pass into the display-space
canvas after the tone map (a two-pass split: opaque HDR → post → tone map → transparents
on top — the usual answer, and it keeps the flare where it is); (2) the 22
`toneMapped:false` sites (the dome, the flare, UI, instrument faces, the impostor bake)
need a "no tone map" carry — a stencil bit or an emissive-only second output; (3) the AP
splice at the head of `tonemapping_fragment` moves into the post pass (it wants linear
radiance, so it belongs there anyway). Estimated: a session for the split, a session for
the re-judging. Buys: a real bloom, an AO on the ambient term (with normals from a
second output), SSR on the water, the door to TAA.

**(c) r186's built-in chain — `new WebGLRenderer({ outputBufferType: HalfFloatType })` +
`renderer.setEffects([...])`** (`WebGLOutput.js`, in `three.min.js` already). Three keeps
an internal 4× MSAA half-float scene target, runs addon-style `Pass` objects over it and
tone-maps + encodes in its own final blit. It IS option (b) written by three: MSAA fixed
at 4 (we run 8 + 1.25×), no supersample, no dither, `toneMapped:false` not honoured, the
gamma-blend trap in full. It would replace `aa_resolve.js`, not compose with it. Named
here so nobody re-discovers it; not recommended while the 8×/1.25× tier and the
display-space transparents are the ruling.

**The candidates, ranked for this game** (what each is *for* here):

1. **The eye** — landed as a row. The one post effect that answers a complaint the
   playtest made (the shed's lamps, the dusk cockpit): a 16×16 mean and a loop, bounded
   so the schedule keeps its authority. Ruling owed: on by default or not.
2. **Look / lens** — landed. The "grading on top of the managed profile" the user asked
   about: three looks and a vignette; an authored 32³ LUT is the next step (a
   `Data3DTexture` slot is in the shader).
3. **Bloom** — landed, display-space, soft by default off. For the sun on the water and
   the lamps at night it reads; on a bright sky `strong` blooms the sky itself. The HDR
   bloom is (b).
4. **Rays** — landed, half res. The owed "god-rays off the same tile" (G425); the cloud
   shadow tile could feed the mask later.
5. **AO** — landed, honest about its limit. The G57 write-up's rule holds (full res,
   5-tap normals, bilateral) for the *material-pass* AO; this one is the switchable kind.
6. **DoF** (cockpit only), **SSR** (water), **TAA/TRAA** (with WebGPU, ruling (an)) —
   not landed.

## 3. THE SHED AND THE WORLD — thirteen differences, and which ones are physics

One renderer, one camera, one loop; `aa.render(inGarage ? garageScene() : scene, camera)`.
Both rooms take the same Hillaire day (`ATMO`), the same `SKY_LIGHT.applyDay`, the same
`aeroMaterial` factory. And yet:

| # | the difference | shed | world | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **the sun's unit** | `hangar.js:3238 unit: 1` | `render_world.js:334 LIGHT_UNIT = π` | **physics — the sun on the aeroplane is π× (1.65 stops) stronger outside at the same hour.** Origin: r128 ran `physicallyCorrectLights` true in the room and false in the world (legacy `irradiance *= PI`); W0.5a froze the gap as `LIGHT_UNIT`; SKY-CHANTIER's `roomGain` is 1 in both callers. |
| 2 | **the sky's radiance** (`ATMO.U.scale`) | K_SUN×1 | K_SUN×π×gain | physics — the shed's sky, its door view, its room probe and its outdoor probe are all π darker |
| 3 | **the hemisphere light** | none (deleted G62.5: it cannot be occluded) | K_HEMI×π×2.0 (island row, G400) on top of the probe | physics — the world's Standard materials get two skies (G94); the shed's get one |
| 4 | the environment's builder | a cube photograph of the room (`app.js bakeHangarEnv`, `fromCubemap`), 256², re-shot on 1.5° / mood / switch | `ATMO.makeProbe` dome + flat cap (`fromScene`), no geometry | legitimate — a room IS its walls; but the two should agree on the sky's level (#2) |
| 5 | **`envMapIntensity` intent** | ×(m.env/0.55) per mood | ×0.5 (WORLD_ENV) | **was a regression — both inert since W0.5a; fixed G448.1** (below) |
| 6 | exposure | schedule, capped at `LAMP_EX_CAP` 1.4 | schedule × `exposureK` (1.0 island, 1.217 sunset row) | the eye — the cap is "the eye adapts to the lamps"; the `eye` row is the general form |
| 7 | fog | `Fog(bg, 40, 120)` — a dark smoothstep on everything incl. the aeroskin, AP off | AP splice + mist, AP on | physics — the shed's air is not 40-120 m of black |
| 8 | shadows | 2048² room map (bias 0 / nb 0.02), 5 spot maps, 2 baked prints | 1024-4096² far map (bias −0.0009 / nb 0.18) + near craft map + far cascade + canopy + cloud shadow | legitimate — a room is 15 m, the world is 1 km; but the craft's bias should be one number |
| 9 | clouds | dome mesh, no cloud shadow, key gain only | volumetric + per-pixel shadow + hemi boost | legitimate (the shed sees the sky through a door) |
| 10 | glare occluders | the whole garage scene | `[craft]` + the terrain profile | legitimate |
| 11 | the interior probe | the room probe stays | `probeIn` (neutral cap) in the cockpit | legitimate |
| 12 | the visible object | the editor's cage `edSit` | `craft` | same factory; fine |
| 13 | ground bounce `gb = 1` | on the floor's albedo during the cube shot | the cap colour + hemi groundColor | one number, three places |

**#5, the regression, verified in the r186 source.** `WebGLRenderer.js:2736`: for every
Standard/Lambert/Phong material with `material.envMap === null` and `scene.environment`
set, the `envMapIntensity` uniform is written from `scene.environmentIntensity` (default
1); `WebGLMaterials.js` uploads `material.envMapIntensity` only when the material carries
its own `envMap`. Nothing in `src/` wrote `scene.environmentIntensity`. So from W0.5a
(2026-09-13) every `aeroSetEnv`, `propSetEnv`, `CAGE_ENERGY.setEnv`, the moods' `ENV0`
loop (for the room's materials) and the shed's env mute were no-ops — the aeroplane flew
at 1.0 under a doubled hemisphere: the G94 "washed out" back, worse. SKY-CHANTIER:40 had
found it and handed it to Session D; D landed the probe and not the number. **G448.1
writes the number where it belongs:** `hangar.js setMood` ends with
`hangarScene.environmentIntensity = muted.env ? 0 : m.env / 0.55`; `app.js` writes
`scene.environmentIntensity = WORLD_ENV` at creation and at the door. The three inert
factory calls are gone from `hangar.js`; the `ENV0` loop stays because the OUTDOOR list
(apron, strip, grass, tufts) carries the sky probe as its own envMap and is still read per
material. GATE LIGHT 4c re-aimed, 4e (the shed writes its scene), 4f (no inert call).

The A/B (`tools/postfx_shot.js`, same boot, same eye, same hour; A = the scene forced to
1 after the mood, B = the fix): shed afternoon crop mean 46 → 62 (×2.2, the factor
authored at G62.5 to replace three deleted fills — every shed judgement since S5/G412 and
G439 was made at 1.0, so **this is the biggest visible change of the fix, and the user's
eye rules it**; the alternative anchor is `m.env / 1.21`); dusk 77 → 73, night 81 → 76
(the lamp-lit probe fades as authored); the env mute 46 → 32 (it did nothing before).
World: fuselage saturation 0.49 → 0.53 (afternoon), 0.57 → 0.61 (golden) with luminance
−10 % — G94's direction; the water −3 %, the leaves and premises within that. The
collateral (0.5 now reaches the water, the leaves, the impostor gain, the premises, the
parked builds, the cabin — all tuned at 1.0) is modest in the wide frame; if any of it is
unwanted the follow-ups are: an own `envMap` on those materials refreshed in the probe's
`onSwap`, or the aeroplane's 0.5 as a uniform in the aeroskin hook. Follow-up F1 recorded:
the shed floor's 1.7, the room glass's 1.4 and the canopy's `1.4 × refl` bases are lost
under a scene number (the canopy `refl` dial was already dead).

**The proposal — ONE LIGHT CONTRACT, so the aeroplane looks the same on the apron and on
the shed floor with the door open:**

1. `unit = π` in both rooms (retire the `unit: 1` in `hangar.js:3238`; `LIGHT_UNIT` is
   the number), the shed's lamps re-expressed in the same unit (candela × π is a one-line
   table change in `hangar_sky.js`), `roomGain` retired. The shed's sky, door view and
   both probes then carry the world's radiance (#2 for free).
2. The hemisphere question decided once: the world's Lambert set opted out of the IBL
   (`LAMBERT_NO_ENV`) with the RULING OWED at `render_world.js:124` — *"the IBL is the
   better ambient; taking it means retiring the hemisphere and re-judging the world by
   eye."* With the fix in, the honest path is: Standard materials take the probe only
   (hemi 0 for them — a `#define` split like `LAMBERT_NO_ENV`'s mirror), Lambert keeps the
   hemisphere until the r184 **LightProbeGrid** (WebGL, L2 SH in a 3D texture, dynamic
   objects lit, ~200 KB for 12³) gives the terrain a baked ambient — that grid is also
   the shed's answer: a probe volume instead of one cube photograph from 3.2 m, so the
   aeroplane under the wing and the aeroplane by the door see different ambients.
3. The shed's `Fog(40, 120)` retired: AP off (as now) plus a room haze term of the same
   shape as the mist (a density × distance, not a colour wall).
4. Exposure: one schedule, the lamp cap expressed as the `eye` row's clamp (the eye
   adapts to the brightest source in the frame — that is what the cap encodes by hand);
   the real answer for "a bright door in a dark shed in one frame" is exposure-fusion
   local tone mapping (Wronski 2022: three synthetic exposures blended through a
   Laplacian pyramid at quarter res, < 1 ms, God of War / Ghost of Tsushima variants) —
   an option-(b) effect, since it wants radiance.
5. The craft's shadow bias one number in both rooms; the shed's spot shadows keep their
   own.

Same-frame A/B shot list for the session that takes this: the aeroplane on the apron
outside the club hangar and on its floor with the door open, same hour (afternoon,
golden, dusk), `postfx_shot.js` from the same eye offset; the metric is the fuselage's
luminance and saturation in both (the G94 numbers), and the floor's.

## 4. NEW — r183-r186, the ecosystem, the literature

**In the vendored r186 (`three.min.js`), usable today:**
- `WebGLRenderer({ outputBufferType })` + `setEffects()` — §2(c).
- **SunLight with cascaded shadow maps** (r186 #34221, moved to addons #34326): a
  target-less directional light, four cascades in 1024² each fitted to the view frustum
  per frame, texel-snapped, blended at the boundaries, faded at the far end; ~1 KB
  gzipped. What it would replace here: the far map + `sunNear`'s black light + the far
  cascade + the canopy cover's manual dance (`shadow_near.js`, `render_world.js
  farRender`). Not free — our craft-only near map is a design, not a workaround — but
  the first thing to try in D2 against the shadow cost in §1a.
- **LightProbeGrid** (r184 #33125, WebGL first; r186 fixes its SunLight integration
  #34370): §3 proposal 2.
- Energy conservation of diffuse + sheen (r186 #33985): the look changed slightly at
  the r186 upgrade; the liveries were judged after it.
- PMREM spiral blur (#32367), `WebGLEnvironments` unifying cubemaps (r183 #32792).
