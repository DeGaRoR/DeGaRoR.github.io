# AFB-0220: Jolene AFB 02/20 gone wild, and the glider winch standing on 13/31 (13 Nov 2026)

The user (13 Nov, via A0): 02/20, the crosswind runway nobody uses (the main one is 13/31), becomes an **unmaintained,
degraded strip "divided into several runways"**:

- a **narrow central band** of the old concrete: clean landings, the Aero Club's spot landing, the plane-vs-car drag race;
- **one side very bumpy** but landable with care (STOL practice);
- **the other side** full of mud, rocks and fallen trees, with proper collision ("crash into them in many different
  ways: wingtip, gear, prop");
- **further out, the forest taking over**, through the tree system only ("never plant a mesh tree").

The glider winch is **installed permanently on 13/31**, with its cable run.

A0 at 17:15 added: exotic things to crash into, few strong pieces (performance), naturally overgrown, gradual
transitions.

## 3. Collision: "as it does to trees and poles"

- **The debris is TREE_HITS.** `20_world.js` setPremises registers the records' cylinders as the trunk set `'wild'`. That
  runs in the page's world, in the physics worker's (which composes the same record) and in every node gate. The hit is
  therefore the same everywhere, cooked or not.
  - A lying trunk is a **chain** of short vertical cylinders, its step the local radius.
  - A boulder group is a **dome**: cylinders on a grid, each as tall as the ellipsoid cap over its centre, less what is
    buried.
  - The solver meets them with every damage path it has for a tree:
    - the beams' collapse load (a wingtip, a strut, a gear leg bends or breaks);
    - the propeller strike, graded by DMG-DRIVE;
    - the nose crush.
  - The cylinders are measured off each subject's bounding box in the pack. They are within 0.5 m of the drawn mesh at the
    rims (the G433 rule is 1 m). 412 cylinders in all.
- **The derelicts are props.** They are solid by their drawn mesh in the page: render_premises `hitAdd`, the obstacles'
  column grid. This is exactly as every placed prop on the island is.
  - **Found:** that path is met by the airframe's NODES, so a 0.3 m pole stands between a wing's nodes. Measured, the
    Cub's wing went through a pole 4.5 m out at 5 m/s with no contact.
  - So a pole with `trunk: true` is also a trunk cylinder (26c_wild `POLES`), and its wing then breaks on it.
  - This session sets that flag on its own 9 poles only. The island's other 40 poles keep the old behaviour; that is a
    decision for the coordinator.
- **The winch** is solid by its drawn mesh, the same as a prop.

## 4. The winch on 13/31

- **The model** is the WINCH session's (`tools/_winch_gen.js` with the club look from `_winch_weather.js`, both
  lazy-loaded). It is PBR: 239 meshes, every one a MeshStandardMaterial out of `MATLIB.shared` in the `winch` scope. It
  reuses the game's own materials: the house library's scan images and the aircraft weathering method. GATE AFB0220 PBR
  checks this.
- **Placement.** render_premises stands it on the composed ground, weathers it and registers it as solid.
- **Why that spot.** The calm day's launches go on 31, toward the NW (G772's calm departure). So the winch stands at
  the end of 31's launch line.
- **The launch line.** The record's `cable` is 1 100 m, so a winch launch toward it moves the lined-up glider up the
  centreline. The glider goes from 31's threshold, 2.3 km away, to the launch point at mid-field.
  - This is done by `winch_mode.js` with app.js's `slide`, which re-places the line-up pose with the motor still off.
  - The cable then runs to the standing winch, and nothing else is built.
  - GATE AFB0220 LINE flies it to the back-release: **308 m**, 114 m short of the winch.
- **The other direction.** A launch the other way (a NW wind) finds no winch ahead. `winch_mode` then sets one up at the
  far end for the launch, as before.
- **The cable run.** The cable is drawn live during the launch (winch_mode). When idle, the runway is clear: a cable
  lying on the main runway would be debris on it. The launch point's camp marks the line.

## 6. Performance

- **Debris.** About 35 k triangles in ~12 instanced draws, plus their shadows. Static: built once per rebuild, nothing per
  frame. The materials are plain MATLIB `std`, as the cover ring's rocks.
- **Hits.** 412 trunk cylinders in one set, binned once. The solver meets them only when the aeroplane's box reaches
  them; on the central band it gathers none.
- **Forest.** The tree system's own fill, with impostors, LOD and chunks. There are no record trees.
- **Winch.** One model, the WINCH session's (239 meshes, which their gate costs).

## 7. Cuts (named)

- **No frost-heaved slabs.** No asset is a tilted concrete slab, and no tree-pack model is one.
- **No runway edge lights.** No prop is one; the old edge-line poles stand in for them.
- **No toppled windsock mast.** Props stand upright, with yaw only.
- **No half-sunk barge-tank.**
- **Trunks are vertical cylinders.** A wheel cannot ride up a rock; a rock edge is a step. That is right for a boulder
  or a log (above the axle) and harsh for the 0.3–0.4 m rock groups.
- **Debris draws at all distances.** It has no distance fade (static, few draws).
- **The derelict props' prop strike.** The obstacles path has no propeller strike; it is the obstacles' own behaviour.
  The poles carry a trunk; the wrecks do not.
