# SWIMMERS: whales, orcas, and later boats, at the surface and under it (ANIMALS, 15 Nov 2026)

The user, 15 Nov: "the orcas ... do not swim realistically, they put themselves on their back, there is no interaction
with the water, so no wake, no interaction with the surface at all, barely any transparency ... the same for the whales
and for everything that swims ... at some point we may want to have animated boats ... generate wakes when they are
crossing the surface and not when they are further down, where we manage transparency correctly ... I really give you a
large mandate so that every animal that we have placed in the game is really looking right and worthy of a AAA game."

## What G498 does, measured (15 Nov)

| Fault the user sees | Cause |
|---|---|
| "on their back" | The body is upright: in 200 s of the pod's run its up axis never drops below 0.77, and the clip and the static cuts are upright too. A submerged animal is drawn by the LOOM: a second, unlit copy drawn after the water with `depthTest: false`, so its triangles land in draw order, and the orca's white belly shows through its black back from above. |
| "barely any transparency" | The water has no depth buffer (water.js header): its opacity is the COLUMN's, from the bed's analytic depth. Over deep sea the column is opaque, so a whale 3 m down is hidden, and the loom is a hack drawn on top. |
| "no wake" | The wakes live in WATER's interaction field: a 192 m box that follows the aircraft, on only while the aircraft is within 60 m of water level. A pod 1 km away never makes one, and a boat would not either. The field has 16 stamps a frame, shared with the aircraft. |
| "do not swim realistically" | A circle around the hotspot at constant speed, one dive cycle (a fifth at the surface, an arch down, a cruise). The fluke beat's rate follows the speed, but nothing else does: no breathing sequence, no fluke-up, no pod formation, no banking that matches the turn. |

## The design

### 1. Under the surface: the water knows where the bodies are
- Submerged swimmers are drawn as ordinary skinned meshes, lit, depth-tested, BEFORE the water: correct
  self-occlusion, their own maps and shading.
- The water shader gets up to 8 SUBMERGED CAPSULES (centre, axis, half-length, radius) as uniforms. Per water pixel,
  the view ray below the surface is tested against them: where it meets a body, the column depth used for the opacity
  and the absorption is the depth to that body, not to the bed. A whale 2 m down then shows through 2 m of sea water,
  tinted by its absorption (G797's coefficients), fading out with depth exactly as the bed does.
- No depth buffer (the reason the water is analytic still holds). The cost is 8 ray-capsule tests per water pixel,
  only when a capsule is live (count 0: skipped by a uniform branch); the 8 nearest submerged bodies to the eye.
- The LOOM goes away.

### 2. At the surface: a wake layer anchored in the world, for anything that crosses it
- `src/viewer/wakes.js`: a body registers a HULL each frame (position, velocity, heading, beam, length, and its
  vertical extent). Its SUBMERGENCE (the part of the body crossing the waterline) and its speed set the wake: no
  crossing, no wake, so a whale cruising at 20 m leaves nothing.
- What it draws: a foam trail ribbon on the surface (world-anchored points, aged and faded, width growing astern), the
  Kelvin arms (two ribbons at +-19.5 deg, amplitude by Froude number), a bow wave for fast hulls, and rings and spray
  on a breach or a fluke slap. One draw for all wakes (a merged dynamic buffer).
- Near the aircraft, it also stamps WATER's interaction field (the real displaced surface the floats feel), as today.
- Boats later are one more caller: the same hull record.

### 3. Swimming
- Paths: a smooth wander inside the hotspot (no circles), banked turns (roll from the turn rate and speed), pitch
  from the vertical speed.
- The breathing sequence: for a blue whale, 3-5 blows 15-20 s apart at the surface with shallow arcs between, then a
  terminal dive (the back arches, the tail stock rises; fluke-up when the clip allows), 2-4 min down; for orcas,
  surfacing every 30-60 s in a pod, an arching roll at each breath.
- The fluke beat: the clip's rate from the speed (the stride rule of the land animals: no speed, no beat).
- Pods: one leader on the path, members on offsets with their own phase, surfacing within a second or two of each
  other.

### 4. Gates (GATE SWIM, node)
- Upright: a swimmer's up axis never below cos(roll limit) except inside a scripted roll.
- No wake from a body that does not cross the surface; a wake from one that does, scaled by speed.
- The capsule list is the nearest submerged bodies; a body under 0.3 m of water is not drawn twice.
- The breathing sequence's timing; the pod surfaces together.
- The cost: the wake layer's CPU time per frame and its draw count, median and p99.
