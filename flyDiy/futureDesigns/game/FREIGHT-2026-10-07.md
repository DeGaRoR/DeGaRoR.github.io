# FREIGHT — weight AND volume, fitted inside the airframe, strapped once accepted
### (2026-10-07, G2206, the GAME COORDINATOR; from the user's ask of 7 Oct)

The user: *"what do we do for freight? We'll need to handle weight + volume, and the volume should be fitted within the
airframe, like the fuel tanks check whether they fit. The player can manually change the location of the load, but an
automatic one will be proposed, and the load will be strapped visually and physically once accepted."*

**Built on what exists.** The fuel / battery path already does the hard half: **bays measured off the airframe's own
mesh** (`GEN_BAYS`, `genBayResolve`, `genBayList`, `_bay_site.js`, `genWingBay`; G97-G99) and **vessels fitted into them**
("every vessel, its bay, and whether it fits", `64_gen_build.js` ~:644), with the walls (`GEN_BAY_WALL`) and the access
doors (`GEN_ACCESS`). Freight reuses that machinery with three differences: the load is many boxes not one tank, it goes
through a DOOR, and it is placed per flight, not per design.

## 1. The model

- **A load = items.** `{ id, kind: 'crate'|'box'|'bag'|'drum'|'long'|'bulk', kg, dims: [L, W, H] m, rigid, stack, fragile? }`.
  A contract's load is a list of items, generated with the job (CONTRACT-MODEL's goods gain dims: "120 kg of crated parts"
  = 2 crates 0.8 × 0.6 × 0.5 m, 60 kg each; mail = bags; fuel drums; a canoe = one long item).
- **Cargo spaces = the airframe's own volumes**, measured like the bays: the cabin floor behind the crew, **an empty
  seat's space** (a seat with nobody in it is floor), the baggage bay, later a belly pod or float lockers. Each space has
  its box, a floor limit (kg/m²) and its station (for the CG). **The door it is reached through** (GEN_ACCESS's openings):
  an item must pass the door in some orientation. That is the rule the fuel tank never needed.
- **The fit**: an item fits if it passes a door that opens onto a space AND packs in that space beside the others and the
  occupants. Pure, deterministic, gated like the tanks.

## 2. The proposal and the player's hand

- **Automatic placement, always proposed**: a small 3-D packer (first fit, biggest and heaviest first, low and near the
  CG), within the floor limits, the door, **the certified CG range and the MTOW** (the plaque's numbers). It shows the
  resulting CG and the margins.
- **The player may move it**: a LOADING view (the aeroplane on the stand, cut away; items as boxes) where items are dragged
  between spaces and positions. The CG and limits update live. A placement out of the CG range or over the floor limit is
  shown red but **allowed** (the spine: the aeroplane flies what you loaded, and you find out).
- **Accept = strapped**: the items become part of the aeroplane: point masses at their positions in the sim (mass,
  inertia, CG; the same way crew and fuel are), and **drawn** as props with straps (the props already exist:
  `crate_wood_*`, `box_cardboard`, `bags_stack`, `barrel_plastic`, `jerrycan`, `pallet_*`, `cement_bags`).

## 3. In the game

- **A cargo job's ✗ (the hard no-no rule)**: an item that fits through no door of any of your planes. Everything else is
  the player's call.
- **The delivery** (CAREER-WIRE's stop record): the load aboard is the strapped items, not a typed number.
- **Passengers** stay seats (they board, they don't fit).
