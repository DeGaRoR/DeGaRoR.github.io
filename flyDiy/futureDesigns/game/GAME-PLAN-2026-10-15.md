## 1. CRITICAL REVIEW — what is strong, what will hurt

**Strong**
1. **The tutorial teaches by owning.** The ultralight, then a client's Cub you end up keeping, then losing it to a
   route, then *choosing* your next plane. Each beat introduces one system (flying, the AP, the editor, certification,
   floats, routes, procurement) at the moment it matters. This is the best part of the plan.
2. **Landmarks as stage punctuation.** The STAGES machinery (tracks, construction look, finished look) already
   exists. Tying stages to it makes progress visible on the island itself.
3. **"Routes absorb aircraft"** (the Cub beat) is a systemic driver, not just a story beat: every route you sign
   takes an aeroplane out of your hands. **That is the engine of fleet demand** (§6.2). It should be the rule, not a
   one-off.

**Will hurt (and the fix)**
1. **The tutorial depends on aircraft we have not validated.** Only the Cub, Jodel and Cessna (+ floats) are
   validated. A *setter ultralight* and a *Ruckus-style ultralight* need design, an autopilot pass, certification and
   every strip they visit (cabin strip → WWII field → the client's strip) cleared by the AP. → **Prerequisite P1**:
   one validated ultralight archetype, plus a CAREER-FIT gate row for the tutorial's strips.
2. **Losing the Cub to a route can feel like a punishment.** The player has just been given their first real plane
   and loses it. The fix is in the framing and the pay: the route **pays up front** (a large commitment fee), the Cub
   stays **visible** (it flies its route on the map and you can see it at the dock), and the same mission **unlocks**
   the three occasions immediately. Lose a plane, gain a choice, in one beat.
3. **"Routes" do not exist yet as an income system**, and time only runs in flight (ruling ax). A route needs a clock to
   pay against. The SIM-CLOCK is now that clock: **a route pays per sim-day elapsed** (flying and waiting both advance
   it), credited on your next landing. Without this, routes are a promise with no mechanism (§5.3).
4. **Cheap planes plus no running costs make money meaningless by mid-stage 1** (the user agrees: §12 simulates it). Gran Turismo works because the
   *entry rules* are the constraint, not the money. Here the natural constraint is **space**: every plane is physical
   and must stand somewhere (§7). So **storage is the late-game sink**: hangars, door sizes, the derelict big hangar.
   Long-term storage is unlimited and undrawn, so it should cost **time**, not money: a recall takes sim hours, which
   keeps the no-running-costs ruling (§7.3).
5. **Era names invite a calendar.** "1950s tech" on an island with 2020s cars and a VOR reads as a time-travel bug. Name
   the stages by *materials* (§4). → RULED (§R): no eras; the segments are named by material.
6. **Checking used and manufacturer planes against the tech stage** looks hard but is not: the tech stage is only a
   mask over editor options, so a design's stage is **the highest stage of any option it uses** (§4.3). One table
   answers the editor, the market and the makers.
7. **Turboprops in stage 2** compete with the exotic shapes for attention, and a turbine needs engine work we have not
   scoped. Put turbines in stage 3. Stage 2 is metal, twins, retractables, bubble canopies, pushers and booms (§4.1).
8. **The "remote community" landmark** touches the real-place rule (GAME §10.2: Annette Island is the Metlakatla
   community's reserve). It must be **fictional newcomers** (a fishing cooperative, a research outpost), never a
   depiction of the real community.
9. **The demo boundary needs to be a feature cut, not a time cut.** See §8.

## 2. THE SHAPE OF THE GAME

```
TUTORIAL (on rails)          STAGE 1 Wood and fabric        STAGE 2 Sheets and bolts        STAGE 3 Composite and electronics
ultralight → Cub → route  →  open map, small planes   →   twins, booms, pushers   →   turbines, glass, STOL, large
→ the three occasions        keystone: the observatory    keystone: the hydro plant    keystone: the long runway
                             (DEMO ENDS HERE)
```

- **A stage = a set of missions + one keystone landmark + one machine-shop rung.** Completing the keystone opens the
  next rung. The other landmarks of a stage are optional and may be finished later, so full completion is never
  required.
- **Missions are attached to stages** (a stage's story missions and the generated work it offers). Generated work keeps
  flowing from every provider at every stage. Rank per provider (reputation, §12.5) still scales its size.
- **The sandbox is unchanged**: everything open, every track at max.

## 4. STAGES AND THE TECH MASK

### 4.1 The three segments (tech by material; no eras, §R)

| segment | character | construction | engines & systems | size | the designs it favours (all existing editor features) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Wood and fabric** | bush flying | steel tube, wood, fabric | small flat-fours, fixed pitch; steam gauges; basic lights | 1-4 seats (6 max) | taildraggers, high wings, biplanes, floats, the Cub / Jodel families |
| **2 Sheets and bolts** | the exotic shapes | aluminium monocoque, bubble canopy | bigger pistons, constant speed, **twins**, retractable gear, better panels | up to 6 pax + 2 crew | **twin booms, pod-and-rod pushers, V-tails, tricycles**, twins: the "exotic 70s" |
| **3 Composite and electronics** | modern utility and records | composite shells | **turbines**, glass cockpit + GPS, economical engines, **electric**, the STOL kit (slats, VGs, fences, G2820) | up to Caravan class (DC-3 at most) | large utility, niche, records, STOL competition builds |

Open checks before stage 3 is promised: turbine powerplants and large airframes (perf at a stable 30 on the laptop
target, rigs, hangar doors). Their state is to be verified, not assumed.

### 4.2 Unlocks: missions open the rung, money buys within it

- **The keystone landmark opens the rung** (the story decides *when*).
- **Machine-shop packs within the rung are bought** (money decides *which first*). This is R.4's machine shop, kept:
  a pack adds hangar assets we already have and unlocks its options.
- **The segmented tree (§R):** v1 = three segment unlocks. Later, a segment may split into **individual entries**
  (e.g. "Sheets and bolts" → aluminium skin / retractable gear / constant-speed prop / twins), each bought or earned on
  its own. The mask (§4.3) already supports this: an entry is just a finer key in the same table.

### 4.3 The mask: one table answers the editor, the market and the makers

- **One table: option → stage.** Each material, engine, avionics item, gear type, canopy and kit carries the stage that
  unlocks it. The editor greys the rows above the player's stage, with the reason in place, and nothing hover-only
  ("Machine shop: Metal").
- **A design's stage = the highest stage of any option it uses.** It is computed, never stored.
- **The market and the makers filter by it**: used listings and catalogue models at or below the player's stage.
- **Tutorial mode** is the same mask with a narrower allowance (finish-only at T0, Mission 2's checklist at T2).
- **The sandbox** has the full mask.

### 4.4 Above-stage occasions (the gifts)

A landmark reward or a rare used listing may be **one stage above** you: you may own it and fly it, but not modify
the parts above your stage. This is GT's gift car. It makes rewards exciting without breaking the ladder.

### 5.2 Mission kinds (the user's list, sorted by role)

- **The base, every stage:** passengers and freight. Freight may name *goods* rather than a place ("deliver 200 kg of
  timber"), so the player picks the source. This breaks the linearity.
- **The constraint missions** (they drive design variety, §6): competitions (STOL, race, straight speed, consumption,
  gates); client orders (a new plane or a modification); surveys (altitude + heading).
- **The story texture:** medical and emergency (the night medevac exists in R.3); leisure, camping and friends'
  gatherings; tourist tours and wildlife sightseeing; your own cabin's improvement (a cabin track: the starting shed
  grows); grey night jobs (§0).
- **Later:** aerial spraying, banner towing, coast-guard assistance.

### 5.3 Routes: the mechanism

- A route is **a plane + a hired pilot + an A↔B schedule**. **Assigning it locks the plane** (it flies the route, shown
  on the map and standing at either end).
- **Pay (§R):** a **down payment on signing** (large: the T6 beat), then **per completed round trip**. A round trip takes
  the route's distance x2 at the assigned plane's cruise, plus a turnaround, in sim time. Trips complete as the sim clock
  advances (flying and waiting both advance it, SIM-CLOCK). The pay is credited on your next landing, so there is no idle
  exploit. A faster or bigger plane on the same route earns more per day: the route rewards the design.
- **Release:** you may end a route at any time, and the plane returns to its home field. There is no penalty, but the
  per-day income stops.
- This is the side track's "hired pilots + route missions" (R.4), made concrete.

## 6. WHY BUILD SEVERAL DIFFERENT DESIGNS — the user's open question

Not one mechanism, but five that compound:

1. **Entry classes in competitions** (GT's race rules): "taildraggers under 450 kg", "twins only", "pushers", "biplanes",
   "amphibians", "electric", "under 2 000 credits to build". The class *is* the design brief.
2. **Physics-honest work that favours different shapes**: short high strips (STOL), water (floats, amphibians), bulky
   cargo (door size, the freight grid), long legs (efficiency), night (lights, panel), a small shed's door (span). Write
   the generated jobs so each stage has all of these, so one plane cannot be optimal for everything.
3. **Routes absorb aircraft** (§5.3). Every route you sign removes a plane, so you need *another* one, and that
   one is usually built for the *next* job, not the last one.
4. **Client taste**: build contracts with one flavour spec ("a pusher", "twin tails like the old ones"), allowed under
   §0. A happy client's follow-up changes one criterion (GQ26), and that pushes a variant.
5. **A collection log** (the GT car list / logbook): a page of archetypes you have *built and flown* (taildragger, biplane,
   floatplane, pusher, twin boom, V-tail, twin, canard if we have it...). Milestones give gifts (an odd airframe, a
   pack discount). It costs nothing at runtime: it reads the fleet and the logbook.

Each stage's story missions should *showcase* its signature shapes (§4.1). Stage 2's exotic shapes are carried by a
competition series whose classes are booms, pushers and V-tails.

## 7. THE FLEET (the user's principles, matched to what exists)

### 7.1 Principles (the user's; ✓ = already built by HANGAR-STORAGE / FLEET-PROPS)

- ✓ Every plane is physical and stands somewhere: the hangar (inside slots), an owned site's apron (outside), or long-term
  storage (undrawn, unlimited).
- ✓ A plane stays at the airport it lands at, in the parking area.
- ✓ "Bring it home" is free at the WWII field.
- **New: summon to another owned site, within its hangar's size and door.** It is the same recall, checked against
  that site's building (the slot caps already sit on top of geometry).
- ✓ A dedicated fleet UI (the STORAGE panel), and planes on the map (MAP-MENU's fleet layer).

### 7.2 Sites you own

The cabin strip (the smallest shed, the start) and the WWII field (the medium hangar). Later, the side hangars
(cap 2, GQ4) and the derelict big hangar (restored = stage 3, R.4).

### 7.3 Storage: room is the only rule (§R)

- **Long-term recall is free and instant.** The only condition is room: a free slot (inside, or the apron) at the field
  it lands at.
- Consequence for the economy: long-term storage is unlimited, so **space limits how many planes stand ready, not how many
  you own**. Space alone is a weak money sink, so §12 must find the sink (candidates there).

### 7.4 Generated names

- Every new airframe gets a memorable generated name. The player may rename it.
- **Default style**: a word pair from the plane's own traits plus a touch of humour, e.g. "Rusty Heron" (a high wing on
  floats, weathered), "Short Fuse" (a short-strip racer), "Mrs. Patience" (slow, reliable). No "Plane 1", no "test".
- The generator reads the design (gear, wing, engine count, finish), so the name hints at the plane. It is seeded, so
  names are stable.
- The tail registration stays separate (the marking kit).

### 7.5 Portraits and thumbnails: one pipeline, three looks

- **First, the clean side shot**: an orthographic profile, captured at the bake that already happens on save (FLEET-PROPS
  bakes on save; the storage cards have a `thumb` slot waiting). It is cheap, consistent and readable at card size.
- **Second, the blueprint**: the *same* orthographic capture through an edge-and-line shader on blueprint blue, plus
  three views (side / top / front). There is no new geometry.
- **Third, the hero portrait** (MSFS-style but dirtier): a 3/4 render in the hangar or on the apron, with the
  weathering. It is shot on demand, never at boot, under the strict garage gate.

## 12. THE ECONOMY SIMULATION (the user: "maybe we should simulate the economics a little further")

**Why:**
- planes are cheap (the GT model);
- there are no running costs (G-COST);
- long-term storage is unlimited and recall is free (§R).

So none of the usual money sinks exists. The numbers must come from a simulation, not from intuition.

**What it is.**
- A node script (planning only; to run in an A0 slot). It plays careers headless on the game's own core:
  - the real job generator (`careerOffers` / `contractJobs`);
  - the real pay (`contractPay`);
  - the validated designs' measured cruise and payload;
  - routes paid per round trip.
- A **policy** stands in for the player. Four policies:
