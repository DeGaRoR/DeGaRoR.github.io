The economy simulation

G3250-G3259 ECON-SIM · 2026-10-22 16:34 UTC · 20 seeds × 4 policies × live / proposed, on the game's own core (tools/flight_core.js 31f1b9ebff). GAME-PLAN §12.

The recommendation, in plain words

  • Routes are the economy's leak today. A route pays its down payment up front (8 round trips): a metal Cessna on HOME - strip 11 brings 103 920 on signing, more than the aeroplane costs, and a wait earns round trips by the hour. The router ends with route income at 66 % of everything (target 40 %). Proposed: perKm 30, a down payment of 6 trips, and a timetable of 6 round trips a sim day: the router lands at 13 %.
  • Money stops meaning anything early. On master the 60 000 grant + T6's down payment put the wallet at 8.4× the next purchase at T7, and every policy is above 3× through segments 2 and 3 (frugal 23.3× / 47.0×): nothing is left to want.
  • The planes are already cheap enough. The pace to a Cub-class build is 3.0 contracts and to the 4-seater 8.0 (with its rung): keep the airframe price as the ledger × labour. What is missing is the reason to spend after the fleet is bought.
  • The sink that holds is landmark co-funding (the leading candidate, confirmed: without it money at rest goes back to 15-45× in segments 2-3, see the sinks table): the keystones at 2.5 / 6 / 12 Cubs, the optional landmarks beside them, plus a machine-shop rung per segment (0.25 / 2.5 Cubs) and the side hangars re-priced (a field shed 3.6 Cubs, ~10 contracts). Drop the story career's 60 000 grant and pay a 20 000 commitment fee at T6 instead (the route's own down payment beside it): the first real money, enough for any one of the three occasions. Competitions, occasions and buildings are taste, not sinks: removing any one of them moves money at rest by a few hundredths.
  • Time is the real constraint, not money. A contract takes ~25 min of real play at 1× (the island's legs, the positioning, the circuits) and the tutorial alone ~2.6 h (T5's survey flies 63 km), so the demo (tutorial + segment 1 at 14 contracts) is ~8.5 h, not 2-3. At 1× the tutorial nearly fills the 3 h by itself; with the cruise at 4× segment 1 could hold ~4 contracts. The economy's ratios hold whatever the length (they are per contract): the demo length is the user's call (a cruise time compression, a shorter T5, or a longer demo).

How it was measured

  • Every career starts with careerNew + storyNewCareer (the cabin found by name: the Cabin Strip, w1) and plays T0-T7 through storyStep / storyApply: the Rufus flies to HOME, the client's Cub is finished to the checklist (the client pays the work: no wallet line, as the page does), delivered to strip 11, given, flies T5's three authored jobs through contractOnStop, goes on floats (an edit: free today) and lands at the seaplane base, where T6 signs the real route with Kit (routeOffer + routeSign: the down payment).
  • Open play: each contract is the offer (careerOfferIds: the real generator, arcs, build contracts, night work) with the best pay per real minute that one free plane of the fleet can fly (contractDoers over the measured designs); flown leg by leg (positioning unpaid), each stop handed to contractOnStop. A windowed job is left for later when its window opens more than 3 h away; outside its window the player waits on the ground; a day leg is not flown between dusk and dawn (the night is waited out). Routes settle at every landing and wait (routeSettle), as the page does.
  • Build contracts: the cheapest design of the open segments that meets every criterion (read off its certificate numbers) is built (paid, the ledger x labour), flown to the client and delivered (it leaves the fleet). xwindKt / batteryKWh criteria are not attempted.
  • Segments are the sim's (TECH-MASK is not in the game): a design's segment is what it wants of a hangar (tube / wood 1, metal 2, composite 3); a segment opens its designs (and, in the proposal, after its machine-shop rung is bought); its keystone stands after {"1":14,"2":20,"3":26} contracts flown in it, and in the proposal once its landmark is co-funded.
  • Real time: every leg at 1× (cruise + 300 s), plus contract 2 min, wait 0 min, buy 2 min, build 12 min, buildContract 20 min, route 2 min, comp 4 min, T0 10 min, T2 25 min, T6 5 min, T7 4 min. These are estimates: the page measures nothing of a player.
  • The pace ("contracts to X") is the contracts flown from the start of the segment that sells X until their pay covers its price: the earning rate, not when a policy happened to buy.
  • Money at rest = the wallet / the cheapest thing the stage still offers that is not owned yet (an airframe type, an occasion, a side hangar, the segment's landmarks and the next rung); nothing left: the next segment's cheapest airframe.
  • Competitions are NOT in the game: the competitor in the live config buys the class designs (a fast one, a short-field one) and favours the challenge contracts; in the proposal it builds a dedicated design per class and enters every round (35 / 30 / 20 % gold / silver / bronze).
  • Not modelled: selling, damage (repair is free, a crash resets: no money), the used market beyond the occasions, the makers' catalogue (a maker costs more than building), reputation's effect beyond what the generator already reads, the player's own exploration time.
