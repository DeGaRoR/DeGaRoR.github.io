# THE TWIN BOOM, THE AERO AFT, AND THE ENGINE BLOCK — a spec (2026-09-04)

The user, in their own words: "I'm tempted by the dual boom implementation. We
should then add an 'aero aft' to the cabin, encompassing the bulkhead (not so
different from a cowl fitted at the back really, maybe we can start there?)
and finishing rounded or as a teardrop (slider wanted). It would be possible to
select the pusher config from this boom setting (in parallel with the rod
setting), and the engine would simply come on the aft bulkhead, like the
current pushers. In this case, no aero aft, the engine cowl will do the job.
... For the twin boom, the Cessna 337 should be the main inspiration, but also
others. It could be pullers, 2 engines on wings (like the WW2 style fighters
and the nice P38), nose mounted like the Cessna, possibly one in front, one at
the back in pusher config. ... I think we can assume that the elevator is
always inside the twin booms."

This document is the spec asked for. It states what exists that the arc can
stand on, the decisions, the rows, the physics, the gates, and the questions
that change the work materially. Nothing here is built yet except where a
G-number says so.

## 0. What already exists

- **The rod boom** (G26, `boomStyle 1`): the fuselage table stops at the aft
  bulkhead, the aft bulkhead is a CAPPED aperture (`rearAperture`, G174 reads
  it as the pusher face), one bare tube (`rodY`, `rodD`) carries the tail; the
  fin/stab layers clamp on the rod (`finCentreline` sweeps it, the stab seats
  on its keel/deck, G173).
- **The mounts** (G174): `engines[i].mount` nose | pusher | wingTop | wing,
  `S.engAt[]`, `engineFaces()` as the one face description, one prop part per
  engine, the wash per mount, the derived wing station following the engine's
  lever.
- **The aero nose** (`noseFinish 'aero'`): rings continue through the screen
  zone into a shrinking, drooping cone to a front grid cap — the exact shape
  an "aero aft" is the mirror of.
- **The cowl bench's nacelle mode** (`aftMode 1`, `tailLen`, `tailDrop`): a
  pod that closes on its own tail cone — bench-only today, not in the cage's
  cowl SHOWN list.
- **The mirrored pod** (S2, `mirror 1`): the aft half is the front half
  reflected — the user's earlier "aero aft" for a pod, with no bulkhead face.
- **The join's tail classifier** (`tailSurfBounds`): stab = outboard of the
  boom half-width, fin = centreline above the deck. Two fins at ±boom would
  land in the STAB pass today.
- **The frame's tail** (61_gen_frame §4): one tail post (TPB/TPT) on the
  fuselage centreline, HT nodes braced to it, one FIN node.

## 1. Decisions

### 1.1 `boomStyle` grows to three values, and the pusher rides it
    0  lofted skin (as now)
    1  rod & pod (as now) — one bare tube
    2  twin booms — two booms off the wing, a tailplane between them
The design tile **Fuselage style** shows the three. The **Engine mount** tile
keeps its own values (the user: "it would be possible to select the pusher
config from this boom setting, in parallel with the rod setting") — so the
Rod & pod tile and the Twin-boom tile each get a *note* and the pusher tile
stays the writer of `engMount 1`; picking Pusher on a lofted body writes
`boomStyle 1` as it does today (G176). A Twin-boom body with a pusher is the
337's rear engine: `engMount 1` + `boomStyle 2` is legal and is the aft face.

### 1.2 The aero aft — the pod's tail when nothing sits on the bulkhead
`aftFinish` (cage row, default 0 = the capped bulkhead as now; 1 = aero aft):
the aft bulkhead ring continues, exactly as the aero nose does, into a
shrinking cone: `aftLen` (0.3–2.0 m), `aftDroop` (−0.3..0.3, the axis
falling or rising), `aftTip` 0..1 — **the slider wanted**: 0 a rounded dome
(the aero nose's ellipse-quadrant taper), 1 a teardrop (a point). Built by
the same `ringAt` machinery as the aero nose, run the other way from the
`pilPaxA` ring. Rule: **the aero aft exists only when nothing is bolted to
the bulkhead** — `engMount 1` forces it off (the cowl does the job), and the
row says so.
Yes to the user's "start from a cowl fitted at the back": the FIRST cut can
be the cowl bench's nacelle tail cone (`aftMode 1`) placed on the bulkhead
face turned round — it exists, it is round and it closes. The reason not to
stop there: it is the cowl's material and part, not the fuselage's, and the
livery, the zones (G139) and the skin grammar would all see a cowl where the
user sees fuselage. So: cut 1 = the cowl cone on the aft face (one session,
proves the look); cut 2 = the fuselage's own aero aft rings (the real one).

### 1.3 The twin booms
- **Where they root.** On the wing — always. Two booms at `boomX` (half-track,
  0.6..3.0 m; default = the nacelle station when there are wing engines, else
  0.35 × semispan) leave the wing's trailing edge (or the nacelle's aft end
  when an engine sits there — the P-38) and run to the tail at `boomLen`.
  Section: a round or a shallow-oval tube, `boomD` (0.10–0.40 m), tapering
  to `boomTaper` at the tail (0.5–1.0). Height at the root = the wing's
  chord plane at that station (`CAGE_WING.leAt` / `underAt` give it); at the
  tail = the stab's seat.
- **The fins.** One fin per boom, on its tail end, built by the fin layer
  TWICE (`finToStab`-style: the same fin model, offset ±boomX). The dorsal
  and the keel tab go off (the rod rule). `finSweep`/height rows are the
  existing ones.
- **The stab.** Between the booms, always (the user's rule) — the stab layer
  with `stMount 3 = between the booms`: root half-track = boomX − boomD/2 ...
  no: the stab spans boom to boom, so it is ONE panel from −boomX to +boomX
  with no centre gap (`stX = 0`, span forced to 2·boomX, the tips are the
  boom faces). The elevator is the full span. `stY` = the offset from the
  boom's centre at the tail.
- **The join.** `tailSurfBounds` learns the twin: fins are the two surfaces
  above the boom lines at ±boomX (measured in the stab pass's own band), the
  stab is what lies between; `tail.type = 'twinBoom'`, `tail.boomX`,
  `tail.boomLen`. Areas: Sv = 2 × one fin; Sh = the span between the booms
  × chord.
- **The physics.** 61_gen_frame: no tail post on the centreline; two BOOM
  node chains (root on the wing's rear spar at ±boomX, three bays each,
  `fus` members, tagged 'BOOM'), HT nodes at the boom tails, two FIN nodes
  (FINL/FINR) — `refs.fin` becomes a list. 62_gen_aero: two `fin` strips at
  ±boomX (the rudder channel drives both), stab strips as now between the HT
  nodes. The wash: a 337's rear pusher blows the stab directly (`stabWash`
  1.0); a P-38's props blow the booms' fins (`finWash` at the nacelle
  station). Drag: the booms are two cylinders — `genFusCdA` gains a boom term
  (Cd 0.3 on their frontal, wetted-area skin friction on their length).
- **The gear.** A twin-boom aeroplane is a trike (the 337, the P-38, the
  Skymaster); the tailwheel option is not refused, but the Twin-boom tile
  writes `gearLayout trike`.

### 1.4 The engine on the wing is a BLOCK, pusher or puller
The user: "a wing-mounted engine can be configured as pusher or puller ...
the same for the dual wing mounted engines ... we should be able to move the
whole block holding the engine, not just the engine — in/out (multiple),
fore/aft, up/down."
- `engAim` (row, 0 puller / 1 pusher) — read by `engineFaces` for the
  wingTop and wing mounts (`aft` becomes the row's, not the mount's). The
  design tile **Over the wing** keeps pushing by default; **Twin (wings)**
  pulling by default; a second tile row **Wing engines face** (Puller /
  Pusher) writes `engAim`.
- The BLOCK rows (mount-local, metres): `engBlockZ` fore/aft (−1.0..1.0),
  `engBlockY` up/down (−0.6..0.8), `engNacAt` in/out (exists, 0.15–0.70 of
  the semispan) — applied to the synthetic FACE in `engineFaces`, so the
  pylon, the nacelle cowl, the engine and the prop move together, and the
  join's `M.engUnits` carries the moved station into `engines[].x/y/z`. The
  engine part's `place` slots: fore `engBlockZ`, out `engNacAt`, up
  `engBlockY` (the common trunk).
- With `engAim` pusher on a wing pair, the nacelle's cowl turns round and the
  propeller runs behind the trailing edge — the P-38 is pullers, a Do-335 or
  a 337 is one of each: **one engine in front, one at the back** is
  `engMount 0` (nose) + a second entry `engMount 1` (pusher) — the spec's
  `engines[]` already allows a list; the frame builds each entry's mount
  (G174's block 2b handles one mount kind per list today; a MIXED list is the
  one new case: a loop over entries with their own mount). The design tile
  **Engines** gains "Push-pull (nose + aft)".

### 1.5 The structure is paintable, and the boom truss has its top tubes
- Paint: the truss members (`tube`, `woodFrame`, `aluminium`, `boomTube`) are
  `struct` role sections claimed by Structure & skin and finish rows exist
  for them (verify in the FINISH tab; if a section lacks a row, add it to
  the part's `sections`).
- The rod boom's tightening truss (boomStyle 1) runs its longerons on the
  keel and the flanks; the user: "the tubular structure needs a couple of
  tubes on the top side of the boom, they currently float, and maybe one in
  the middle on top as well, so the dorsal elements have something to attach
  onto." Add two TOP longerons (at the roof corners) and one crown tube
  along the top centreline to the rod-mode truss — the fin's foot lands on
  the crown tube.

### 1.6 Glazing off
`glazeOn` (cabin row, default 1): 0 drops every glass face (windscreen,
windows, skylight, canopy) from the drawn mesh — an open cockpit — and the
join writes `cabin.glazing 'none'` so the frame's glass mass is not billed.
The tree: cabin → **Glazing** (windscreen, pilot window, skylight, window
joints, the bubble rows) with `glazeOn` as its gate; the pilot door moves
under Cabin fit.

### 1.7 The legs on an open frame
When `skinOn` is 0 (G172), `fitOn` roots a leg on the FUSELAGE CONTRACT's
surface — the covering that is not there. Rule: on an open frame the leg's
root is the nearest STRUCTURAL MEMBER of the interior pass (the truss
longerons and diagonals the interior pass draws — `cageInterior` publishes
their node positions), the root is a PIVOT (a lug pair on the tube, no
doubler pad, `padOn` skipped), and the strut runs straight from the pivot to
the axle. `wingMount` (G133) is the precedent: a frame provider handed to
the builders; the open-frame provider returns the tube's own point and the
tube's normal.

## 3. Gates
- GATE FIN: the fin built twice at ±boomX is the same mesh translated (bit
  test); the stab between the booms spans exactly 2·boomX.
- GATE JOIN: twin-boom fixture → tail.type twinBoom, Sv = 2 fins, stab between.
- GATE GEN: configuration `twin boom` (rigid, stands, flies a leg); a
  mixed-mount `push-pull` configuration; `engAim` pusher on a wing pair.
- GATE GEAR: an open-frame provider roots on a tube point and draws no pad.
- GATE ARCHETYPES: a **337-alike** (push-pull, twin boom), a **P-38-alike**
  (two pullers on the booms, one seat), a **Skymaster-alike bush** variant.
- GATE PARTS/SAVE/DESIGN as always.

## 4. Order of work (each a session)
1. **The engine block + aim** (§1.4, no twin boom needed; unblocks the P-38's
   nacelles and the 337's rear engine as it is) — with the glazing (§1.6) and
   the boom's top tubes + paint check (§1.5): small, all measurable.
2. **The aero aft, cut 1** (§1.2: the cowl cone on the aft face) then the
   pusher-forces-it-off rule.
3. **The twin booms** (§1.3): cage (booms + fins ×2 + stab between), join,
   frame, aero, drag, the three archetypes. The largest; it is where the
   wing's `leAt` and the fin's `finToStab` pay off.
4. **The legs on the open frame** (§1.7) — its own session; it touches the
   gear contract (G148/G133) and needs the interior pass to publish members.
5. **The aero aft, cut 2** (the fuselage's own rings) when the look of cut 1
   has been judged.

## 5. Questions that change the work (answer before session 3)
1. **Boom root station.** At the wing's trailing edge on the wing itself (the
   337, most twin booms), or behind a nacelle when there is one (the P-38)?
   Proposed: on the wing at `boomX`; when a wing engine sits at the same
   station the boom starts from the nacelle's aft end. Say if the boom
   should be able to sit AWAY from the nacelle station.
2. **Boom section.** Round tube (cheap, the rod's own grammar) or a lofted
   oval that can taper (the 337's booms are slender ovals)? Proposed: round
   with taper first, oval later as a row.
3. **The stab between the booms**: one full-span panel (elevator boom to
   boom) or two half-panels with a gap? The user said "always inside": one
   panel proposed.
4. **The 337's rear engine** sits on the pod's aft bulkhead with its own cowl
   (as the current pusher). Should the pod's aft face be allowed to sit
   BELOW the wing (the 337's pod passes under the wing) — i.e. is the pod
   always the high-wing cabin, or may the twin boom go with a mid/low wing
   (the P-38 is mid)? Proposed: any wing position; the booms root on the wing
   wherever it is.
5. **Push-pull**: worth building in session 1 as the mixed-mount list, or
   only with the twin boom? Proposed: session 1 (it is the frame loop only).
