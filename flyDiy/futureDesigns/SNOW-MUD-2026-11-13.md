# SNOW-MUD: snow and mud under the wheels (13 Nov 2026)

The user (13 Nov): "if we develop snow, maybe we just develop mud; it's pretty much the same thing in the end."
Uses: the observatory's altiport on Mount Ardent (snow, skis), and the community airport whose first stages are short and
muddy, later graded to another surface. Built by SNOW-MUD (cloud session); the skis' gear is the SKIS session's.

## 1. What a world can now say

Five new SURFACE codes, after the eight hard ones (20_world.js / 27_premises.js `SURFACE`, the names in `SURFACE_NAMES`):

| code | name        | premises look | what it is                                                       |
|-----:|-------------|---------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 8    | SNOW_PACKED | `snow`        | a rolled / groomed snow strip, cold (the altiport's)             |
| 9    | SNOW_POWDER | `powder`      | dry fresh snow over a packed base - the layer is deep            |
| 10   | SNOW_WET    | `slush`       | near 0 C, heavy and sticky ("worse than mud or even beach sand") |
| 11   | MUD_FIRM    | `mud`         | a drying clay strip - the community field's first stage          |
| 12   | MUD_SOFT    | `softmud`     | wet clay after rain - the wheels go in                           |

They are placed ONLY by a record: a premises strip's look (the editor's strip SURFACE pills), an area's DRAW surface (the
area inspector's select), or an old-style surface polygon. Both lists are built from `RUNWAY_LOOKS`, so the five rows
appear there with nothing else to wire. Nothing in either world carries one by default. A snow or mud area keeps trees
and plots out as the hard surfaces do (`FEEL_HARD`).

The strip vocabulary (25_airfield.js): the three snow looks read `snow` (the gear rule: skis may use it), the two mud
looks read the new `mud` (cls `mud`: wheels yes, skis no, floats no). `STRIP_ENUM_SURF` covers the codes too.

## 2. The physics (00_registry.js, 30_solver.js)

`GROUND_SURF[8..12]` are `[rolling, brake mu, lateral mu]` of the FIRM BASE under the layer. Sources: Raymer table 17.1
(firm dirt 0.04 / 0.3, soft turf 0.07 / 0.3, wet grass 0.08 / 0.2, icy 0.02 / 0.06-0.10), ICAO GRF / RCAM braking bands
(compacted snow ~0.30-0.35, dry snow > 3 mm ~0.30, wet snow / slush ~0.26-0.29). The eight hard rows are unchanged.

`GROUND_SOFT[code] = { k, n, zMax, ski: [kinetic, breakout] }` is the LAYER: Bekker's pressure-sinkage law p = k z^n
(Bekker 1956 / 1969; Wong, Theory of Ground Vehicles, ch. 2), the layer's depth zMax (the firm base under it). The
exponents are Bekker's families. k is FIT so the stock wheel (D 0.4 m, standard carcass, 3 kN) reads the operational
rules (FAA-H-8083-23 skiplane chapter, mountainflying.com rule of thumb, AvWeb): soft ground +20-50 % take-off, wet or
powder snow 2-3 x or no-go, wheels limited to about 4 in of dry / 1 in of wet snow. The published fresh-snow k is a
virgin snowpack; a strip is settled, and these k are 10-30 x stiffer.

A WHEEL (`groundSinkWheel(S, N, b, D, A)`): the rigid wheel sinks z = [3N / ((3-n) b k sqrt D)]^(2/(2n+1)); a pneumatic
tyre presses no harder than its LOAD OVER ITS FOOTPRINT (A, `tyreFootprint`: the carcass's width and rated deflection on
the wheel's diameter) - where the rigid wheel would press harder it flattens and sinks to (N / (A k))^(1/n); never deeper
than zMax. The trench it compacts resists F = b k z^(n+1) / (n+1). The solver adds F / N to the wheel's rolling
coefficient. In the rigid regime F / N ~ sqrt(z / D): more load, a narrower or smaller wheel, a softer layer - all drag
more. Read at the reference wheels (extra rolling coefficient F/N, sinkage):

|             | stock 3 kN  | C172 5 kN  | tundra 3 kN | slim 3 kN  |
|-------------|-------------|------------|-------------|------------|
| packed snow | 0.015 5 mm  | 0.019 7 mm | 0.001 1 mm  | 0.023 7 mm |
| powder      | 0.25 10 cm  | 0.28 12 cm | 0.04 3.5 cm | 0.34 13 cm |
| wet snow    | 0.20 7 cm   | 0.24 9 cm  | 0.025 2 cm  | 0.29 10 cm |
| firm mud    | 0.07 2 cm   | 0.095 3 cm | 0.005 3 mm  | 0.12 3 cm  |
| soft mud    | 0.22 6.5 cm | 0.30 10 cm | 0.014 9 mm  | 0.29 9 cm  |

The runs this gives (GATE SOFTGROUND --show; full power from rest to 0.9 Vs, heading held; then idle + full brakes):

| build             | grass | packed | powder | wet snow | firm mud | soft mud |
|-------------------|-------|--------|--------|----------|----------|----------|
| stock taildragger | 71 m  | x0.95  | x1.87  | x1.49    | x1.10    | x1.55    |
| on tundra tyres   | 71 m  | x0.94  | x1.32  | x1.20    | x1.05    | x1.22    |
| stock tricycle    | 67 m  | x0.94  | STUCK  | STUCK    | x1.41    | STUCK    |

The tricycle's small nose wheel bogs in powder, wet snow and soft mud with the stick neutral. With full back stick it
creeps out of soft mud (5.5 m/s after 60 s) - the soft-field technique matters. Packed snow brakes worse (0.30 against
grass's 0.45): the braked roll is 29 m against 21 m.

THE COST: the wheel branch asks `world.surface` once, as before, and keeps the code; on a hard code the only new work is
one integer compare. The legacy rolls are bit-identical (GATE SOFTGROUND A, 16 rolls against master 23ce68c1). The
readouts `sim.out.softZ` (the deepest sinkage this frame, m) and `sim.out.softF` (the compaction drag summed, N) are
written only on a frame that met a layer.

THE CUTS (named): the contact does not move down. The aeroplane stands at its hard-ground pose, and the sinkage is a drag
and a readout (a moving datum fed back by the ground spring is a stiff loop: z(N) against KGn needs a filtered, plastic
sinkage - a later step, if the eye asks for wheels sunk in). There is no speed effect: no tyre planing on slush, no
ski lift. Lateral ploughing is not modelled (the side grip is the row's mu). Freeze-in is the SKIS session's.

## 3. The skis' interface (for SKIS)

- `GROUND_SKI_MU[code]` = `[kinetic, static breakout]` of a UHMW / PTFE bottom on every code. The snow rows come from
  SKI-REFERENCES-2026-11-13 sections 4.1 and 4.6: packed (ideal, -3 C) 0.04 / 0.08, powder (cold, dry) 0.12 / 0.24, wet 0.10 / 0.20. The
  others are ESTIMATES: grass 0.25 / 0.40, mud 0.25-0.30, hard ground 0.40-0.50. Water is `null`: the water's own pass
  carries a ski that is in it.
- `groundSinkPlate(S, N, A, w)` -> F, the PLOUGH (N): a plate of bearing area A (m^2), width w, load N, on the layer S =
  `GROUND_SOFT[code]` (undefined on a hard code: no sink). It sinks to z = (N / A / k)^(1/n), capped at zMax, and the
  trench its front compacts resists w k z^(n+1) / (n+1). `GROUND_SINK` then holds `{ z, p, F, cap }`.
  The sliding friction is on top: mu x N along the ski's motion, `GROUND_SKI_MU[code][0]` sliding and `[1]` to break out.
- Read at a 1.2 psi ski (0.85 m^2, 0.4 m wide, 7 kN): powder 1.5 cm, plough 0.3 % of the load. A 4 psi ski
  (0.25 m^2): 3.3 cm, 1.3 %. A NAMED CUT: one power law per layer, fit at a tyre's 30-200 kPa. It lets a 7-10 kPa ski sink
  only cm into powder. Real fresh snow takes a ski deeper, then stiffens as it compacts. If its gates need that, a
  two-regime snow is the SKIS session's to add, as a row field (absent = this law).
- The tail skid (twMode 4) on snow and mud reads `TW_SKID_MU[8..12]` (steel on snow ~0.10-0.15, an ESTIMATE).

## 4. The look

- THE STRIPS AND AREAS (src/viewer/pavement.js): two pavement classes, `snow` (index 6) and `mud` (7), carrying the soft
  classes' machinery: the compacted wheel band, the ruts, the tread pressed in (full lug imprint, as dirt and sand), the
  coarse patches. Both sit in the runway look's `sf` group, so its knobs reach them.
  - There is no snow set in the pavement library, so SNOW RECOLOURS fine grain (`CLASS_DEF.snow.recolor`): each slot's
    set is graded to a target linear albedo. The fresh surface is ~0.72, the wheel tracks greyer and bluer (~0.52), the
    banks ~0.82, at saturation 0.06. The texture's relief stays; only its colour becomes snow's.
  - It goes through the existing per-slot grade uniforms (no new uniform, no new program). `gradedMean` reads it too, so
    the far tier's `uMean`, `surfaceColor` (the dust's colour) and `groundColor` agree.
  - MUD wears the library's own mud sets (`mudAir`, the aerial mud with tyre tracks; `tracksM`, the muddy ruts).
    `wetMin` 0.45 keeps a wet film on it whatever the day's soil says.
  - The shader's class tests are re-cut: `grassy` is 5 alone (it was "> 4.5"), and the lug imprint takes 6 and 7.
- THE DUST (src/viewer/dust.js): two classes, `mud` (always wet: clods thrown off the tread, never dust) and `snow` (its own
  dry powder: the tyres and the prop's wall jet lift it white). Their water is their own, not the season's soil (`wet`
  on the class row). app.js maps the codes and the pavement classes to them.
- THE BOUNCE LIGHT (render_world.js `GROUND_ALBEDO`): snow 0.55-0.86, mud 0.04-0.10.
- THE SOUND (audio/airframe_worklet.js `AF_SURF`, airframe_model.js `AF_ROUGH`): five rows. Snow is a soft hiss and a
  squeak-crunch; mud is a heavy low rumble with no grains. The worklet's row bound is the table's length (it was a
  literal 8).
- Elsewhere, the vehicle (`VEH_SURF`), the scar (`SCAR_SURF`: bare), the prop-strike allowance (`turfS`: soft) and the
  scuff (`SCUFF_SOFT`: soft) all carry the five codes.

No new per-frame cost in flight: the surface path is the one it was. The pavement's snow is a grade, mud is sets it
already ships, and the phone loads the same code.
