# ASSETS 2026-09-27 — THE ASSET CENSUS: WHAT SHIPS, WHAT LOADS, WHAT IS SHARED, WHERE THE DESIGN WENT WRONG

A study plus a measuring tool, with no game change. It answers the user:

> "Asset rationalization: assets have been developed with no concern for reusability of textures or materials. There
> is a wide variety of those in the houses, roads, runways, the plane, etc. Maybe it's time to revise that and merge
> materials, or have single materials that get PBR settings rather than per-part? I thought that the buildings, being
> procedural in nature, would share a lot of DNA and be able to get optimized easily... So point out where I have made
> mistakes in my asset design. I also have a lot of different meshes in a wide variety of optimization states, and
> some may be redundant, or could be substituted, or merged? Maybe some assets are loaded while not used? Like the
> grass or some trees and ecosystems? Maybe pruning would help?"

**Scope.** Paths are relative to `flyDiy/`. Line numbers are as of `claude/batch-a-base` 4f68545. It is read together
with `ARCH-2026-09-27.md` (the architecture study: §1.4 draws, §3 cooked premises, §4 town kit, §7 aeroplane bake,
§9 cut list) and `QUEUE-C-2026-09-27.md` (the chantiers C0-C4 already validated). This document does not re-plan
anything those already cover. It points at them and adds what they do not cover.

**How the numbers are marked.**
- **[census]**: measured by `tools/asset_census.js` in this session (node, the cloud container), reproducible with
  `node tools/asset_census.js --decode`.
- **[node]**: measured in node here by some other means (named).
- **[G###] / [§x]**: quoted from HANDOVER, ARCH or PLAYTEST.
- **[est]**: arithmetic on measured inputs, with the inputs named.
- **Owed [runtime]**: needs the GPU box. The tool's `--runtime` mode is written for it but was not run here (§1.3).

**Units.** The census's sizes are MiB (2^20 bytes), whether a table writes "MB" or "MiB". Decimal MB appear only in
quotes from other documents.

**"GPU MB".** What three.js uploads today: every image texture as RGBA8, 4 bytes a texel, plus the mip chain
(×4/3). It is an upper bound per file, reached only if that file is resident. What is ACTUALLY resident in a Jolene
session is the runtime census's job (§1.3).

## 0. THE SHORT ANSWER

1. **The user's intuition about the houses is half right, and the half that is wrong is the expensive half.**
   - What the houses share: textures (31 sets, one `THREE.Texture` per set and map, `_house_gen.js:640-657`) and
     shader programs (~7 program shapes, by material type and the hooks each material carries [census]).
   - What they do not share: materials. Every house builds a fresh 27-material finish (`makeFinish`,
     `_house_gen.js:1222`), and so does every outbuilding and every site item (`render_premises.js:1734, 1809,
     1932`). The reason is structural. The per-house dials (paint, dirt line, age, sag, weather, AO gain, wander)
     are 159 UNIFORM values per house [census], held on the materials, and a uniform value can only differ between
     two meshes if they carry two materials.
   - So the DNA is shared at the texture and shader level and cloned at the material level. That clone is what cost
     7 805 materials and ~2 300 draws over Jolene [G566/G574].
   - Running the generator in node over its 46 house presets: 1 242 materials are made. Read as data, they are 520
     distinct value sets on 7 program shapes [census].
2. **One mistake, repeated across the code: variation is bound to MATERIALS instead of DATA.** The same pattern
   shows up in:
   - per-house finishes;
   - one pavement material per strip, with its markings as uniform arrays;
   - aircraft tint and dials in the material pool's key (142 materials a build, 0 shared [G576]);
   - a full material copy per parked placement just to carry a matrix;
   - one material per (prop, part, LOD rung), where 653 records hold ~271 distinct;
   - tree tint as per-material uniforms.

   The repo has no shared material library: there are 6+ local caches, keyed by identity, name or value, each used
   its own way. The one module that does it right is `scenery_life.js`: one material per batch, with the per-instance
   tint in an instance attribute.
3. **No texture is GPU-compressed.** 1 206 textures ship: 157 MB on the wire, ~3.2 GB of GPU memory if all were
   resident, and 0 KTX2/Basis [census].
   - Encoding hygiene is poor:
     - 186 of 208 normal maps are JPEG;
     - 136 single-channel data maps (roughness/AO/gloss/specular) are separate RGB files;
     - 48 maps are a CONSTANT shipped as a texture, 239 MB of GPU if resident (for example seven 2048² "Specular"
       maps that are pure white);
     - 35 grey images are stored in three channels [census].
   - The pavement library's packing (colour+height, normal+roughness, two texture arrays, `pavement.js:450-485`) is
     already the right format. It just is not used anywhere else.
4. **The same ground textures ship in four libraries.**
   - `lot`, `splat`, `pavement` and `site` each carry their own copy of dirt / dry / grass / lush / pebble (and
     `site`+`pavement` share five more sets).
   - 32 byte-identical groups (5.8 MB) plus re-encoded near-copies [census]. Each library decodes and packs its own
     copy on the CPU.
5. **Meshes: mostly in a sound state, and the as-is rule is respected.**
   - Every geometry bin is quantized (int16 positions, int8 normals, u16 UVs and indices) except chars and animals,
     which are float32.
   - The pier kit has LODs beside its as-is meshes; the trees have rungs, impostors and instancing.
   - What is wrong:
     - **no geometry bin is compressed on the wire**: 103 MB, of which gzip alone saves 26 % [census];
     - **the hangar's 45 props have no LODs** (up to 137 k triangles each);
     - **the assets/ bake sources carry 54.6 MB of OBJ variants that no baker reads** [census].
6. **Loaded-but-unused: the downloads are mostly fine; the MEMORY is not** (§3).
   - What is fetched but unused on Jolene without Metlakatla is ~12-20 MB. It is mostly vegetation: four tree
     species and two grasses that no biome reachable on the map plants, fetched in the garage and built at the
     roll-out.
   - The real waste is copies:
     - the 3095×3920 albedo expanded to an RGBA texture the ground shader then paints over;
     - two class-weight textures sampled only in a debug mode (~140 MiB GPU together);
     - every island texture's CPU copy kept after upload (~277 MiB);
     - the terrain quadtrees decoded to Float64 at every node (**238 MiB** [census]);
     - every fetched bin kept by the fetch cache.
   - That is ~0.5-0.8 GiB of JS heap no pixel needs [est], paid before the garage even paints. The physics worker
     (C1b) would pay it twice.
   - The boot fetch is 35.6 MiB / 181 MiB decoded [census]. The ARCH figure of 48.7/238 MB counted the authoring
     DEM, which is never fetched.
7. **What is already being fixed:**
   - TARR (G574): the house finish becomes a vertex slot plus texture arrays;
   - C3's town kit: role × finish-set, one material pair;
   - C4's aeroplane bake: 142 materials → ~4;
   - C2c: TARR arrays offline, KTX2 "if the loader allows";
   - A2-RUNWAYS: pavement bounds and culling.

   **Not covered by any chantier:** texture compression as a pipeline, the four ground libraries, props / pier /
   trees / rocks material dedupe, encoding hygiene, mesh transport compression, lazy families, and repo/site weight.
   §5 slices these into ten sessions in seven groups (AS0-AS6), ordered against QUEUE-C so nothing is done twice.

## 1. THE TOOL — `tools/asset_census.js`

### 1.1 Static mode (any box; ~1.5 s, ~15 s with `--decode`)

```
node tools/asset_census.js                    # summary tables
node tools/asset_census.js --decode           # + near-duplicates, flat / grey maps, decode ms (python + PIL)
node tools/asset_census.js --md               # the same tables as markdown
node tools/asset_census.js --json out.json    # one row per file, for diffing later
```

What it reads, and what it reports:

| input | what the census reports |
|---|---|
| every file under `media/` and `assets/` | image headers (no decode): size, format, channels, alpha, power of two; the texture's ROLE from the bakers' suffixes; GPU bytes; sha256 (exact duplicates) |
| `--decode` | one python/PIL pass: a 32×32 RGB thumbnail (near-duplicates at any size), per-channel std (flat maps), equal channels (grey stored as RGB), alpha actually used, decode ms |
| every `media/` path named in `src/**.js|json` and `tools/_*.js` | who references each file; which of those manifests the SHIPPED page loads (`tools/build.js` MANIFEST + `world_packs.json`) |
| every mesh payload manifest, evaluated with stub registrars (no THREE) | triangles, vertices, parts, LOD levels; each bin's gzip ratio |
| `trees_pack.json` biomes | which vegetation collections a shipped island's terrain types can plant |
| `new THREE.*Material` / `onBeforeCompile` / `customProgramCacheKey` / `InstancedMesh` / `BatchedMesh` / Canvas / Data texture sites per file | the code sites, not instance counts |
| the house generator, run in node on `_house_check.js`'s stub THREE (as the ARCH probe does) | materials a finish makes, per-house uniform values, distinct value sets, program shapes |
| `world_packs.json` | every boot payload gunzipped in node (shipped bytes, decoded bytes, ms); the two terrain quadtrees decoded by `19_terrain_codec.js` (bytes, ms); the terrain-type grid's histogram (which biome codes the map holds) |

It writes nothing under `media/` or `src/` and never re-encodes a file ([[import-models-as-is]]).

### 1.2 Limits of the static mode

- **A texture's role comes from its name.** The pier and prop kits name their maps by hash (`pier/4438f44cf0f6.jpg`),
  so their 468 maps all count as "colour". The runtime census reads the real slot (`map`, `normalMap`, ...).
- **"GPU MB" is per file, if resident.** The ~3.2 GB total is not a session's footprint; it is what the store would
  cost if everything loaded.
- **Near-duplicates skip low-structure maps.** Two maps with little structure at 32×32 (most normal and roughness
  maps) "match" whether they are the same picture or not, so the census leaves them out (thumbnail std < 10). Byte
  duplicates still catch those.

### 1.3 Runtime mode (the LOCAL GPU box; written here, NOT run)

```
node tools/asset_census.js --runtime --cold --secs 60                 # a first visit, Jolene, defaults
node tools/asset_census.js --runtime --secs 60 --variant nomet        # warm, Metlakatla filtered
node tools/asset_census.js --runtime --secs 60 --q town=1             # Metlakatla on (?town=1)
```

It writes the page-side expression to `tools/perf/asset_census_page.js` and runs `tools/rollout_perf.js --eval @...`
(the `rollout_census.js` contract: read, never write). It then diffs the page's answer against the static census.
The page side reports:
- `renderer.info`: programs (each with its name, cache-key length and users), geometries, textures.
- The scene:
  - meshes, InstancedMeshes and their instance counts, BatchedMeshes;
  - DISTINCT materials, by uuid and by a parameter SIGNATURE. **uuid count − signature count = the materials that
    should have been shared**;
  - materials with `onBeforeCompile`, material types;
  - per top-level owner: meshes, materials, geometries, triangles.
- Textures:
  - by kind (image / canvas / data / array / compressed / render target);
  - estimated GPU MB by owner;
  - the 30 heaviest.
- The network:
  - the service worker's media cache keys: every `/media/` URL the profile fetched (run `--cold` for "this session
    only");
  - `performance.getEntriesByType('resource')`: sizes and durations, with a flag when the 250-entry buffer
    overflowed.
- **Fetched vs in the scene:** media fetched that no live material carries (loaded, not used), and families never
  fetched.

Output: `tools/perf/rollout_assets_<cold|warm>_<variant>_census.json` plus tables. Rules: one GPU benchmark on the box
at a time (`tools/perf/GPU_BENCH.lock`); a `--cold` run for the first-visit truth.

**What the runtime run must answer before R2-R4 start (§5):** resident texture MB by owner on Jolene at the stand,
on the taxi and over the village; the materials-by-signature gap; which fetched media are never bound.

**Findings.**
1. **No geometry bin is compressed on the wire.** GitHub Pages compresses text types only, and a `.bin` is served
   as `application/octet-stream`, raw. That is from Pages' documented behaviour, not measured here: the cloud
   container cannot reach Pages (403). The runtime census confirms it, since its resource log carries
   `encodedBodySize` against `decodedBodySize` per file. The world pack already solved this: every payload is one gzip stream,
   decoded with `DecompressionStream` (`build.js` ISLAND_LOADER). The mesh bins would save **26 % (103 → 77 MB)**
   with the same one-line wrapper [census].
   - Quantized data compresses poorly under gzip; meshopt's lossless vertex/index codec typically does 2-3× better
     [est, not measured here].
   - Both are lossless and byte-exact after decode, so the as-is rule holds (it forbids decimating and re-encoding
     the GEOMETRY, not transport compression).
2. **The hangar's props have no LODs.** 45 props, ~1 M triangles, 0 levels, while the pier kit's 92 LOD'd props
   prove the tool (`prop_lod.js`) and the rule's allowance (an LOD beside the as-is mesh, HANDOVER :48803). The shed
   is seen at the stand, the most-looked-at place in the game.
3. **Heavy meshes, with their LODs:**
   - The pier's scanned people and boat: `boat_grady` 370 k triangles, `person_andrew` 355 k, `person_koky` 332 k,
     `person_john` 310 k. Their LODs already cut to 24 k at 8 m (people) and 92 k at 15 m (the boat).
   - The runtime census should confirm that the L0 of a scanned person is never drawn from the air.
4. **Redundancy.**
   - No byte-identical bins (md5 across `media/geo`); no prop key in two packs.
   - The only unpublished geometry is draco (0.8 MB).
   - The 12 published reference planes (1.13 M triangles, 15 MB) are fetched only when the garage shows a
     reference.
   - The trees manifest exists twice, `src/core/trees_pack.json` (221 KB, node/gates) and `src/viewer/trees_pack.js`
     (127 KB, shipped), by design and generated together.
5. **Substitutable:**
   - The 42 `auto_*` cars are one pack (Orchids) with per-car materials. They are the natural candidate for
     "one car material + a texture array layer per livery".
   - The 7 pier modules and the crate/bin variants share maps.

   This is material work (§2.3), not mesh work.

**Programs** are not the explosion; materials are. The ATMO accessor wraps every hook (`atmo.js:624-633`), so

- the define/side/transparent variants;
- the per-feature hooks (house: shadeHouse, cloudWeather, steelMix, shadeGlass, smoke, skirt: 6 variants);
- the two uniform-in-key leaks above;
- three's own permutations (map present or not, normalMap present or not, vertexColors, instancing, shadow depth

A shared library with FEW material shapes and textures always bound (a 1×1 neutral layer rather than "no map")
collapses the permutations too.

## 3. THE LOAD CENSUS

The question is what a Jolene session with Metlakatla off (the default since G590) fetches, decodes and keeps,
against what it uses. The file:line reads below come from a full read of the loader, the payloads and their
consumers; the byte counts are [census] unless marked.

### 3.1 How the page loads

- **index.html** (9.5 MB, 3.0 MiB gzipped) inlines three.js, the core, the viewer and the editor.
- **The island loader** runs first (`build.js:638-706`):
  - it fetches `world_packs.json` and every `files` entry: **15 gzip payloads, 35.6 MiB shipped, 180.9 MiB
    decoded, gunzip 0.41 s in node** [census];

- **Payload scripts** (models, props, panelhw, chars, animals, the world list) are ~98 `<script src>` refs.

  (`storage.js:22-31`).

## 4. THE DIAGNOSIS — WHERE THE DESIGN WENT WRONG, AND WHY

**The pattern: variation was bound to MATERIALS instead of DATA.** Every family grew the same way:
1. A visual feature is wanted: a dirtier house, a runway's markings, a greyer engine casting, a tint on a tree
   species.
2. The quickest place to put the dial is a material property or a uniform.
3. The feature ships with one material per variant.

Each step was local and correct for the feature. Summed over a world, it makes thousands of materials. In
three.js on WebGL, each distinct material:
- is a separate draw (three cannot batch across materials);
- is a separate uniform upload per draw;
- is a separate entry in the shadow passes;
- is a separate sort key in the transparent list;
- and, whenever a define or a map slot differs, a separate program to compile.

The web makes this ~3-5× more expensive per draw than a native engine [ARCH §0 item 2]. It is not a web problem.
MSFS, Unreal and Unity content pipelines forbid it by construction: a "material instance" there is a set of scalar
parameters over a shared shader, and the per-object variation rides in per-instance data or custom primitive data.

**Why it happened here, candidly.**
- **The generators were built for looks, one object at a time, in benches.** The house bench, the aeroskin editor,
  the pavement recorder and the tree bench each show ONE subject. There, a material per variant costs nothing and
  a uniform is the fastest dial to wire to a slider. The benches never measured a world of them. The world was
  assembled from bench outputs, and the first time anyone counted materials was G566 (7 805).
- **Each chantier built its own texture pipeline.** There are:
  - four ground libraries (lot, splat, pavement, site);
  - four texture-array builders (pavement `library`, splat, TARR, the impostor IMPA), each packing on the CPU with
    canvas `getImageData`, each with its own channel layout;
  - five kinds of per-family cache.

  Nothing was wrong within any one of them, but nothing is shared between them. The textures were deduplicated
  within each family (one `THREE.Texture` per URL, per family), never across families.
- **No texture budget.** The bakers pick a size and a format per family (`media_lib.py` roles), and GATE MEDIA
  budgets the page's CODE (4 MiB wire), not the store. Nobody owns "how much VRAM does Jolene cost". Hence:
  - 2048² character maps for pilots seen through a windscreen;
  - white 2048² specular maps;
  - 8K skies;
  - JPEG normals;
  - no compression at all.
- **Imports were treated as sacred, which was right, but the rule was stretched from GEOMETRY to MATERIALS.**
  [[import-models-as-is]] protects the mesh. The import's material RECORDS (653 in the pier kit, 271 distinct) and
  its texture ENCODING (hash-named JPEGs with separate roughness maps) were carried through with the same
  reverence. Material records and texture encoding are packaging, and packaging is ours to rationalize.

**What is already being fixed** (and is NOT re-planned here):

| problem | who fixes it | state |
|---|---|---|
| house finishes → a vertex slot + texture arrays | **TARR G574** | landed; runs after the build queue empties; excludes steel/flags/glass/emissive |
| houses as instances, finish = role × finish-set, one material pair | **C3a/C3b** (ARCH §4) | queued |
| TARR arrays built offline, KTX2 "if the loader allows" | **C2c** (ARCH §3.2) | queued; needs a KTX2 loader nobody has scheduled (→ R2) |

| pavement: ~90 uncullable transparent draws | **A2-RUNWAYS** (bounds, culling) | batch A |

**What NO planned chantier covers** (the gap this study fills):
1. **Texture compression as a pipeline.** KTX2/Basis loader, baker role, per-family budget. C2c assumes it; nobody
   builds it.
2. **The four ground libraries**, and the four array builders packing on the CPU.
3. **Prop, pier, animal, tree, rock and cliff materials:** dedupe by content, texture arrays, instance tint. TARR
   covers houses only; C4 covers aircraft only.
4. **Pavement's per-strip materials and the per-runway canvas.** A2-RUNWAYS culls them, it does not merge them.

7. **Lazy families** (the load census, §3) and the unmapped vegetation.
8. **Repo and Pages weight:** 54.6 MB of unread OBJ variants, the authoring DEM, screenshots and bug reports.

## 5. THE PLAN

### 5.1 The target: a shared material library (MATLIB)

**The answer to "single materials that get PBR settings rather than per-part": yes.** The library has a handful of
material SHAPES. Everything that varies between objects is DATA the shape reads, never a new material.
**The house path already proves it: TARR (G574) is exactly this design for one family.**

| layer | what | where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **shapes** (one material per shape × side × casts; one program each + its depth/distance variants) | `std` (opaque PBR), `cut` (alpha-tested: foliage, fences, cards), `glass`, `glow` (emissive / lamps), plus the special shaders that stay their own: `ground` (splat + pavement), `water`, `sky` / `clouds` / `atmo`, `house` (TARR's, becoming C3b's role × finish-set), `aero` (C4's baked material) | `src/viewer/matlib.js` (new): `MATLIB.shape(name, {side, cast})`; the ONLY place a shared material is constructed |
| **texture arrays** by size class (512, 1024) | layer = one texture SET. Two arrays per class, packed the way `pavement.js` `library()` packs them: **A = albedo RGB + alpha/height; N = normal XY(Z) + roughness** (AO / metalness in a third array only where a family needs them, else scalars) | built OFFLINE by a cook (AS2's tool, shared with C2c's TARR arrays), shipped as KTX2 arrays after AS3 |
| **a params table** | one row per (set, tint, roughness scale, metalness, wear, dirt, UV scale): TARR's slot table generalised (RGBA32F texture, `house_tarr.js:265`) | built by the cook (static content) or appended at runtime (the editor, live builds) |
| **per-vertex / per-instance data** | the row index (a `slot` vertex attribute for merged meshes, an instance attribute for InstancedMesh / BatchedMesh), plus a per-instance tint when an instance differs from its row | the bakers write the slot into the geometry; `scenery_life.js`'s instance tint (`:802`) is the per-instance pattern |
| **a material RECORD** (what the bakers write instead of a material) | `{ set: 'plank_07', tint: [r,g,b], rough: 0.8, metal: 0, wear: 0.3 }`, deduplicated by content at bake time | the manifests (props, pier, trees, rocks, house finishes): the record is data, the shape is code |

**Rules the library enforces:**
- No material is made outside `matlib.js`. GATE ASSETS (AS0a) greps for `new THREE.Mesh*Material` outside an
  allowlist that shrinks session by session.
- No uniform VALUE inside a program key (`site-macro-<m>-<amount>`, `site-fade-<soft>`).
- Every shape binds every slot. A 1×1 neutral layer stands in for "no normal map", so three.js makes one program
  per shape rather than one per map-presence permutation.
- A per-object dial is a params-table column, never a uniform.

**What stays out:** the flown aeroplane in the garage (the live aeroskin shader the livery editor needs, ARCH §7.2),
the sky, clouds, water and atmosphere shaders, and post-FX.

### 5.2 The prune list

## 6. THE TEN FINDINGS, RANKED

1. **The materials are cloned, not the DNA.**
   - The houses share textures (31 sets) and programs (7 shapes), but every house, outbuilding and site item makes
     its own 27-material finish.
   - 159 per-house uniform values force it [census]: 7 805 materials over Jolene [G566], and in node 1 242 materials
     for 46 presets, which are 520 distinct value sets [census].
   - TARR (G574) and C3 fix the houses. **The same mistake, not covered by any chantier, is in:** props / pier (653
     records, ~271 distinct), trees (tint per material), rocks / cliffs (a material per part), pavement (a material
     per strip, markings as uniform arrays), the per-runway 2048² canvases, and parked per-placement copies.
2. **No texture compression anywhere.** There are 1 206 textures, 0 KTX2. Every map is decoded to RGBA8 and
   uploaded on the main thread, with mips generated on the GPU; that is up to ~3.2 GB if everything were resident.
   KTX2 is ÷4 to ÷8 in VRAM, and C2c already assumes a loader nobody builds (AS3).
3. **Memory waste dwarfs download waste on Jolene.**
   - An albedo texture painted over by the shader (46 MiB GPU + CPU).
   - Class-weight textures sampled only in a debug mode (93 MiB GPU + CPU).
   - CPU copies of every island texture (~277 MiB).
   - The quadtrees as Float64 at every node (238 MiB [census]).
   - Every fetched bin kept by the fetch cache.

   That is ~0.5-0.8 GiB of heap and ~0.14-0.2 GiB of VRAM for no pixel [est], and the physics worker (C1b) would
   hold a second copy (AS1).
4. **Four ground libraries ship the same surfaces.** lot, splat, pavement and site each carry dirt / dry / grass /
   lush / pebble (and asphalt ×5 in site + pavement): 32 byte-identical groups (5.8 MiB) plus re-encoded copies,
   each decoded and packed on the CPU separately. Pavement's library (two packed arrays, demand-driven) is the
   right design for all four (AS2).
5. **48 textures are constants.** They include seven pure-white 2048² character "Specular" maps and a flat 2048²
   normal: 239 MiB of VRAM if bound, 0.7 MiB on the wire. Each should be a number in the material record (AS0b).
6. **Encoding.** 186 of 208 normal maps are JPEG; 136 single-channel data maps are separate RGB files (none packed
   ORM-style except pavement's); 35 grey images in 3 channels; an 8192×4096 sky pair past the 4 096 many GPUs stop at
   (unused under ATMO) [census].
7. **Unused vegetation: the user's guess is right, for part of it.**
   - Four tree species and two grasses (birch_autumn, maple, ash, pine_lampi, grass_plates, grass_scan) are in no
     biome reachable on Jolene without Metlakatla.
   - They are fetched in the GARAGE and built at the roll-out: 1.55 MiB of bins, ~7-8.5 MiB of maps, up to 62 MiB of
     GPU [census].
   - Three of the 19 splat sets are unused on Jolene.
   - The grass that IS planted is used.
8. **Geometry ships uncompressed.** 103 MiB of mesh bins go raw over the wire (Pages does not compress `.bin`). The
   world pack's own gzip-stream pattern saves 26 % [census], and meshopt's lossless codec saves more [est]. Both keep
   the bytes the as-is import wrote (AS5a).
9. **The meshes themselves are mostly sound, and the as-is rule is respected.** Everything is quantized except the
   skinned chars and animals, the pier kit has LODs beside the as-is, and the trees have rungs, impostors and
   instances. The gaps:
   - **the 45 hangar props have no LODs** (~1 M triangles; bandsaw 137 k) and stand at the most-looked-at place in
     the game (AS5b);
   - the characters carry 4 × 2048² maps each (85 MiB GPU per seated pilot) (AS6).
10. **The repo carries dead weight that Pages publishes.**
    - 54.6 MiB of Jodel OBJ variants no baker reads (the user's own models: the user decides).
    - The 10.9 MiB authoring DEM (by ruling).
    - The unpublished draco.
    - 327 MiB of screenshots and bug reports.
    - A media cache that never evicts superseded textures.

**THE ONE SENTENCE:** flyDiy's assets were designed one bench at a time, so variation went where a bench's slider
could reach it, into materials and uniforms. The fix is a small material library fed by texture arrays, a params
table and per-instance data (TARR's design, generalised), plus a compressed texture pipeline and a memory diet on the
island. QUEUE-C already does it for the houses and the aeroplane; AS0-AS6 do it for everything else.
