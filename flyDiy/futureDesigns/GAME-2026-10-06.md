## 0. THE GAME IN TWELVE LINES

1. **You are the manufacturer and the operator** (GAME-LAYER §2). You design aeroplanes, buy them or find them, put
   pilots in them, and fly the island's work with them.
2. **The loop.** Pick contracts on the map → choose or get an aeroplane that can do the job (customise, build, buy
   used) → choose a pilot → fly (the pilot flies; you may take the stick) → get paid, or learn why not → the island
   changes.
3. **Contracts come from providers**: the mine, the dock, the resort, the survey office, the field trust, private
   clients. Each provider has a tab in a SnowRunner-style list beside a map of the island.
4. **Build contracts ask for performance, never for a spec.** For example "a fast plane for four", "under 300 kg empty",
   "an hour on a ridiculous tank", "electric". They are verified by the certificate where it can tell, and by a flown
   acceptance leg where it cannot.
5. **Three concepts of property** (the user's words): **the main hangar**, **two side hangars**, and **every
   materialised aeroplane with its costs**. An aeroplane stands in a hangar, or is stationed at any runway without one.
6. **Planes are physical.** They stay where you leave them, and each one is visible as a prop at its last base. "Bring
   it home" puts it back in a hangar (free in the sandbox, a recovery fee in a career).
7. **Three ways to get an aeroplane**: customise a manufacturer's model (the validated archetypes), build one from
   scratch in the garage, or buy a used one where it stands and fly it home.
8. **Pilots are people.** Each recruitable pilot has a personality (a PILOT-PERSONA profile), a Mixamo body, a
   portrait, a wage and a story. They get better with logged landings.
9. **Time runs only in flight** (ruling ax). Hangar rent, wages and running costs accrue per flown hour, and the fuel
   cost is the fuel the simulation actually burned.
10. **Buildings move with the missions.** Delivering the mine's contracts stands its headframe up, through a
    construction stage and then the finished building.
11. **The welcome screen** offers Continue / New career / **Sandbox** (today's game, unchanged) / Garage only (the
    phone's mode). The rigs and gates never see it.
12. **Free choice is the rule.** No contract names an aeroplane or a route. No pilot is forced. No progression gate
    hides a part of the island. Rank only unlocks the size of the work offered.

## 2. THE LOOP

```
        ┌──────────── the MAP: providers' contracts, your fleet where it stands, your pilots ─────────────┐
        │                                                                                                  │
   pick a contract ──► have an aeroplane that can do it? ──no──► CUSTOMISE a maker's model / BUILD / BUY USED
        │                        │yes                                         (the garage, the bench, the market)
        ▼                        ▼
   choose the pilot ──► fly it (the pilot flies; you watch, or take the stick) ──► arrive / divert / break
        │                                                                                │
        ▼                                                                                ▼
   paid: the fee − fuel burned − wages − rent        the aeroplane stays where it stopped (a prop there)
        │                                                                                │
        ▼                                                                                ▼
   provider reputation ↑ → bigger work offered,      damaged → grounded until Repaired (dm10); lost → the story
   the provider's buildings go up (§11)
```

**THE SPINE, kept from GAME-LAYER §1.** A mismatched aeroplane fails for physical reasons. If you send an overloaded
Cub to the 250 m mine street, the pilot cannot get it off the ground, and that is the game telling you. The certificate
is the instrument you consult before risking an asset. The map's detail card does part of this for you (§8.3: "your
fleet against this job"), without ever deciding for you.

**What v1 resolves, and what it does not.** v1 is **one flight at a time, the one you are in**. Your other aeroplanes
stand where they are. GAME-LAYER §6 (background flights resolved by the real solver in a worker) is **P-later** (§15
row 14). It is the observatory's half of the game and a large chantier, and the loop above is complete without it.
This is a change from GAME-LAYER §10, where assignment was the spine's L. Here the spine comes first through the
flight you are in.

**Time.** Ruling ax stands: the garage, the map and the menus are timeless, and the clock runs in flight. Contracts
have **no wall-clock deadlines** in v1. A "window" is a condition of the flight itself, such as "before dark" or "with
the weather as offered".

## 3. THE THREE CONCEPTS — the main hangar, two side hangars, the materialised aeroplanes

The user: *"Just work with 3 real concepts: the main hangar, 2 side hangars (ability to reset planes to hangars, but
also to station them on runways without hangars), all materialized planes, with their costs."*

GAME-PREMISES modelled any number of hangars on plots at seven aerodromes. **This study keeps its model and caps it to
the user's three concepts.** The rules (capacity as geometry, nothing moves while it flies, the lift refuses nothing,
sandbox records and career charges) all carry over unchanged.

### 3.1 The main hangar — the workshop

- One, **at HOME** (Jolene AFB). It is today's garage room, the club shell with today's kits (`sheds.HOME`).
- **It is the only place where an aeroplane is built or structurally modified.** Repair, paint and small work happen
  in the side hangars too (§3.2).
- Its shell, size and kits can be upgraded (`playerUpgrade`, GAME-PREMISES §2.4). The kits set the labour factor.
- The garage opens here by default (`player.here = 'HOME'`).

### 3.2 Two side hangars — where your fleet lives away from home

- **At most two**, each on any **plot** the world offers (`BASE_OFFERS`: HOME.2, w3, tw_ski, mn_strip, SEA, mk_sea).
  The player chooses where they go. That is the free choice: a seaplane shed at the dock, or a field shed at Tamgas
  Hill.
- A side hangar is a field or club shell (works only at HOME.2), bought or rented, and moved by releasing it and taking
  another plot.
- In a side hangar you can: **park**, **repair** (the labour factor of its kits), **paint**, **refuel**, and swap
  pilots. You **cannot build** there. (Default; the alternative is a side hangar with the woodshop kit allowing wooden
  builds, §16 GQ3.)
- The garage can open in a side hangar (`playerGoTo`, instant: the Kerbal vessel switch, GAME-PREMISES Q7). It shows
  the aeroplanes standing at that base. The editor offers repair/paint/finish only.

**Why two and not N.** It is the user's number. It also makes WHERE to put them a real decision instead of a
collection. It caps the world-side cost, because at most three player hangars ever stand in the world (§4.4). The
plot list stays declared, so a later "third hangar" is one number.

### 3.3 Stationed on a runway — no hangar

- Any aerodrome is a **station**: the aeroplane stands tied down at that field's stand / apron ring (premises v1.21
  `stands`, DEST-TO `flightWhere` kind `stand|apron`). This is GAME-PREMISES's `out` / `away`, under the user's word.
- A stationed aeroplane can be **flown** from there, refuelled (if the field sells fuel, a field property), and seen.
  It cannot be repaired except by "field repair" at ×1.25 labour (GAME-PREMISES §4.4).
- Stationing is free in v1. Weathering outside and a parking fee come with the wear system (GAME-PREMISES Q10, §16
  GQ7).

### 3.4 "Reset to hangar" — bring it home

- **Bring it home** puts any aeroplane, wherever it stands (or wherever it stopped: a field landing, a crash site),
  back into the hangar it last left, or, if that is full, the main hangar. Room is checked by `hangarPark`; if
  nothing fits, it is stationed at HOME.
- **Sandbox: free and instant.** **Career: a recovery fee** (`playerRecover`: 200 + 25/km, GAME-PREMISES) and the
  aeroplane is unavailable for one flown hour of the clock (§16 GQ5; default **no delay**, the fee only).
- This replaces GAME-PREMISES Q8 ("no ferry"). The user asked for the reset.

### 3.5 The materialised aeroplanes — and their costs

- **A materialised aeroplane = one airframe** (GAME-PREMISES Q1, kept): a saved build in the fleet ledger
  (`player.fleet[slot] = {hangar|null, aero, foot}`). It has a purchase price (or build price), a running cost per
  flown hour (§12.2), a hull value, a logbook, a pilot it last flew with, and a state: ready / damaged-grounded (dm10) /
  written-off.
- **A design is not an aeroplane.** Designs live in **the drawing board** (§5.4): free, unlimited, shared between the
  sandbox and careers, and exportable. Materialising a design costs its ledger price. This is the clean answer to "slot
  vs design" and it keeps the sandbox's forty builds harmless (§13.3).
- **The fleet screen** (one of the map's tabs, §8) lists every airframe: where it stands, its state, its value, its
  cost per hour, its last pilot, and actions (Fly from there · Bring it home · Repair · Sell · Show on the map).

## 4. THE FLEET YOU CAN SEE — a prop copy at its last base

The user: *"ideally a prop copy of each plane is shown at its last base, on the apron, so the player's full fleet can be
seen. Of course, with almost no impact on performance."*

### 4.1 What is drawn

- Every materialised aeroplane that is **not the one being flown** stands as a parked prop where the ledger says it is:
  - on the apron ring / stands of its station;
  - inside a side hangar behind an open door, at `hangarPark`'s place (only when the door is open and the camera is
    near; default: residents are not drawn inside closed sheds, §4.4);
  - at the crash / field-landing spot while the aeroplane awaits "bring it home" (a wreck still; damage ON only).
- When you roll out an aeroplane, its prop **hides** at once (the live aeroplane replaces it at the same stand). When
  the flight stops, the ledger moves it (`playerArrive`) and its prop appears there at the next roll-in or stop.
- The **flown** aeroplane itself is never a prop (the live model, flown_bake).

### 4.2 How — reuse the parked pipeline, add one door

`parked.js` already turns any build into a one-draw-per-level atlas-baked prop with `mine:<slot>` keys. What is missing:

1. **Bake on save, never on roll-out.** When an airframe is saved or materialised in the garage, queue its `mine:` bake
   on the garage's idle path (`whenIdle`, the way the cook loads). Sign it by the spec's fingerprint (the bench's: spec
   minus cosmetics + PHYSICS_V) **plus** the finish, and cache it in IndexedDB `flydiy.parked`. A roll-out then decodes
   it (62-85 ms for three cooked keys today), and a capture never runs in the world.
2. **Fix the editor leak first.** HANDOVER G809 finding 2: a live `mine:` capture depends on the player's *current*
   build (241k vs 250k tris). For a fleet that is a correctness bug: the wrong aeroplane could stand on the apron. The
   capture must start from a clean editor state, gated byte-for-byte (the same spec captured under two different
   current builds gives identical bytes).
3. **Placement.** Each aerodrome gets an ordered list of tie-down spots: the record's `stands` (v1.21) plus an apron
   ring computed off the runway's `site` (MILL-TAXI's clearance rules: no spot on a taxi route or a building). The
   fleet takes them in airframe order. This is deterministic, so the same fleet always stands the same way.

### 4.3 The perf budget — "almost no impact"

| rule | why |
|---|---|
| **Only L2/L3 beyond 30 m; L1 only within 30 m of the camera** (the existing ladder) | L1 is ~160-200k tris; L2 ~20k; L3 ~4-5k |
| **At most 6 fleet props drawn per frame** (the nearest), others culled; L3 beyond 450 m, gone at 2.5 km (existing) | a full HOME apron of 6 at L2 = ~120k tris, 6 draws |
| **potato / laptop / pocket: L3 only, max 4** (~20k tris, 4 draws, ~0 MB extra GPU beyond the atlas); FRIENDLY-WELCOME-BUDGETS says "parked bakes not built" on the light presets, so this adds a **L3-only** path for the fleet (the user's ask outranks the blanket rule; §16 GQ9) | the fleet stays visible on the weak machines |
| memory: a baked key is ~3.5-3.8 MB of atlas + geometry; **max 8 keys resident**, LRU over IndexedDB | a 40-build sandbox must not hold 150 MB |
| **no capture, bake or decode during flight**; the fleet set changes only at roll-out / roll-in | no hitch in the air (ROLLOUT / A5 lessons) |

**The proof (LOCAL-GPU, FLEET-PROPS slice B):** FRAMECOST at HOME taxi and cruise with 0 / 3 / 6 fleet props, on
gamer and retro and potato under `boxlock take gpu`. **Acceptance: within the strict-gate noise of 0 props**, ≤ +0.3 ms
GPU at taxi on retro, and no new long task in the roll-out. Plus a same-frame still pair (GPU timing needs a still).

### 4.4 The hangars in the world (with GAME-PREMISES S3)

- At most **three player hangars** stand in the world (main + two side), each `genHangarBuild` exterior at its
  plot/preset/dims (GAME-PREMISES §5). Plots you do not hold show nothing new (default; GAME-PREMISES Q19's "to let"
  shell is §16 GQ8, default **off**: no extra buildings).
- Residents inside: drawn only when the door is open and the camera is within 60 m. Otherwise a closed shed costs
  nothing extra.

## 5. PROCUREMENT — three routes

The user: *"customize one from the manufacturers (the archetypes), build one from scratch, or buy a used one."*

### 5.1 Customise a manufacturer's model

- **The manufacturers** are fictional companies, one per family, each with a catalogue of models built from the
  ARCHETYPES / stock designs (`tools/_cage_design.js`). **Only validated builds are sold at launch**: the Cub, the
  Jodel, the C172, the C172 on floats and the twin ultralight on floats (the user's ruling, 1 Oct). Each new
  validation adds a model. The other ~22 archetypes stay hidden until validated. Example makers (names for the
  narrative pack to finalise):
  - **Tamgas Light Aircraft**: the Cub family (tube-and-fabric, bush).
  - **Ateliers Jodel-alike**: wood, the Jodel family.
  - **Northstar**: the C172 family, metal; floats as an option.
  - **Driftwood Ultralights**: the twin on floats.
- **What "customise" means: the options a maker offers, priced.** Each model has a short options sheet: engine
  (within the maker's list), tanks, seats, gear (wheels / tundra / floats / skis), avionics tier, the finish (any
  livery, free of structure), and the registration. These map onto existing spec rows (`designBake(sel, over)`), so a
  customised model is an ordinary spec.
- **Factory-certified:** a maker's model arrives with its certificate and bench results already awarded (they are the
  validated numbers), and **delivered to HOME** (or any side hangar, for a delivery fee).
- **Opening it in the full editor** is allowed (free choice). The aeroplane becomes "modified": the maker's
  certificate is withdrawn (the bench fingerprint already does this), and the price of the change is billed at build
  price (dm11's rule).

### 5.2 Build from scratch

- The garage, as today: the birth flow → the editor → the bench. **Materialising** (the first "Build" of a design) pays
  the ledger (`genShakedown.cost` = parts + materials + engine) **× the main hangar's labour factor** (0.8 with every
  verb the design wants, 1.0 if short; GAME-PREMISES §4.4 applied to building).
- No build time (the garage is timeless, ax). The constraint is the money and the main hangar's kits.
- **Scratch builds are cheaper than a maker's equivalent** (no maker's margin, default ×0.75 of the catalogue price
  for a like build) but arrive **uncertified**: the bench is yours to run. The value of a scratch build is the freedom
  to do what no catalogue sells.

### 5.3 Buy used — where it stands

- **The used market** is a tab on the map. Each listing is a **materialised airframe standing at an aerodrome**: a
  validated design with seeded variations (engine hours, a past repair, an older avionics tier, the paint, the
  registration, a few kg of extra empty mass), a price (catalogue × condition × 0.4-0.8), and a seller with a line of
  text.
- **You buy it where it stands and fly it home**, or pay "bring it home". This is the physical fantasy SnowRunner's
  abandoned trucks give: *"there's a Jodel for sale at Metlakatla."*
- Listings refresh after every N completed contracts (time only runs in flight; default N = 3), at most 4 at once.
- **Condition is honest but limited in v1**: hours and a damage history are text and price only, because there is no
  wear system yet (DEFORM's reliability layer). Extra empty mass and an older avionics tier are real spec differences.
- **Found aeroplanes** (ROADMAP P6 "found aircraft"): a later variant where a survey contract reveals a derelict
  airframe at a strip. §16 GQ12, default later.

### 5.4 The drawing board — designs vs airframes

- **Designs**: every spec you have ever made, every maker's model you have customised, and imported files. They are
  free, shared between the sandbox and careers, and are today's `flydiy.build.*` slots lifted into a library.
- **Airframes**: materialised designs owned in a career (or in the sandbox's fleet). They cost money, stand somewhere
  and carry a logbook.
- "Build this design" makes an airframe from a design. "Save as design" files an airframe's spec. Editing an airframe
  bills the change (dm11).

## 6. BUILD CONTRACTS — performance to reach, written as user stories

The user: *"should concentrate on performance to reach, not a spec on the plane … The player needs to think VS the user
stories."*

### 6.1 What a build contract is

A client wants an aeroplane, for themselves or for their business. The contract states **what the aeroplane must DO**,
in the client's words, with **measurable acceptance criteria** underneath. **It never names a configuration** (no "high
wing", no "tricycle", no "Rotax"). You design or adapt something, materialise it (paying for it), prove it, and
**deliver it by flying it to the client**. The client pays the agreed price, plus a bonus for margins beyond the
criteria. The design stays on your drawing board, and **the client may re-order**: a good design becomes repeat income.
That is the manufacturer's half of the game.

### 6.2 How each criterion is verified — the certificate where it can tell, the flight where it cannot

| criterion kind | verified by | today's source | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| seats / occupants | spec | `cabin.seats`, `cabOcc`, `paxOcc1-6` | "for 4" = 4 occupied seats at their station masses |
| empty mass ≤ X | ledger | `genShakedown` mass with nobody aboard and no fuel | exact |
| powertrain kind (electric, diesel…) | spec | the energy module (`60c_gen_energy.js`) | exact |
| tank / battery ≤ X | spec | tank litres / kWh | exact |
| span ≤ X (fits a door) | spec + `hangarDoor` | GAME-PREMISES' door rule | exact |
| structural (aerobatic +6 g) | **structural certificate** | `66_gen_cert.js` limit/ultimate | exact, already cached (DMG-CERTCOST) |
| crosswind, density altitude | **bench** | BENCH_TESTS xwind / dalt | awarded results |
| water operations | **bench** hydro + the delivery itself | — | the delivery lands on water |
| **cruise speed ≥ X at load L** | **flown acceptance leg** | — | **must be flown**: VCruise is solved and clamped to 1.55-2.2 Vs (`64_gen_build.js:116`); a fast design reads as capped |
| **endurance ≥ T on tank V** | **flown**: burn measured in a 10-min stabilised cruise × tank, with a reserve | — | the shakedown's endurance is full-throttle / 0.67, a rule of thumb |
| **range ≥ D** | flown: groundspeed × measured endurance, calm air | — | — |
| take-off / landing distance at a strip | **flown at that strip**: the delivery or a demonstration | the logbook's `run` | "gets out of Jumbo Mine Street with 2 aboard" is proved by doing it |
| cost ≤ X | ledger | `genShakedown.cost` | exact |

**The acceptance leg** is a recorded segment of the delivery flight. The pilot (the AP with the contract's profile, or
you) holds a stabilised cruise for N minutes at the declared power, and the game measures TAS, fuel or energy flow and
altitude. That needs a small recorder, **ACCEPT** (§15 row 7), built on FLIGHT_REC's frames but tied to the build and
signed into the logbook. The plaque gets a "**proved in flight**" row beside the bench stickers. Those are honest
numbers that will also improve the plaque for the sandbox.

**The client's pilot flies the acceptance** at a stated personality (a careful club pilot by default). If your design
only works for an expert, a story can say so ("the doctor is not a bush pilot").

| *"A trainer for the club, cheap and forgiving."* (the club) | cost ≤ 25 000; crosswind limit ≥ 12 kt; flown by a **student** profile, safe circuit (PERSONA_BANDS) | ledger + bench + flown with the student persona | handling for a weak pilot |
| *"Something that will take the aerobatic box."* (a showman) | structural ultimate ≥ +6 g; climb ≥ 5 m/s | structural cert + shakedown | structure mass |
| *"A plane for East Point clearing, 150 m."* (the survey office) | take-off and landing at nv_strip with 1 aboard and 1 h of fuel | flown at the strip | STOL |

**Bonuses** run on margins, like SnowRunner's bronze / silver / gold contests: +10 % per criterion beaten by a stated
margin (faster by 10 %, lighter by 10 %). There is no penalty for exceeding a criterion.

### 6.4 Failing it

A delivery that misses a criterion is **refused, not punished**. The aeroplane stays yours (it is an airframe in your
fleet; sell it, keep it, adapt it), the contract stays open, and you may try again. Crashing during delivery follows
the damage rules (§12.4). That is the spine: you learn why.

## 7. THE CONTRACT PROVIDERS, AND WHAT THEY OFFER

### 7.1 Contract kinds (SnowRunner's three, adapted; cited in §8.1)

| kind | what | repeatable | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contracts** (a provider's arc) | multi-stage jobs: carry X from A to B, a build contract, a survey; stages in order, sub-stages in any order (SnowRunner's model, [5][10]) | no | "Reopen the mine: survey the road, fly the engineers in, deliver the pump" |

| **Survey** | land at an unproven site, prove it, and it becomes a station (GAME-LAYER §4.2; WORLD-V2 §7 nominations) | no | "is the gravel bar at Walker Creek landable?" |

### 7.2 The providers (fictional organisations on the real map; names finalised by the narrative pack)

| provider (tab) | home field | work | its arc builds (§11) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Field Trust** (your landlord at the old Army field) | HOME | tutorial jobs, the field's restoration | the WWII hangars restored, the tower relit |
| **Jumbo Mine Co.** | mn_strip | crews, parts, ore samples, the short street strip | the headframe, the ore shed, the tram (exists) |
| **The Dock & Cannery** | SEA / mk_sea | fish spotting, floats freight, mail | the dock extension, the cold store, a slipway |
| **Skyline Resort** | tw_ski | guests, sightseeing, VIP, the electric build | the lodge, the lift, the snow strip widened |

| **Private clients & the Club** | anywhere | build contracts (§6), challenges | the club house, a museum hangar for your best design |

Six tabs plus **All**. Rank (§12.5) is per provider, as **reputation**. Reputation only changes *how big* the offered
work is. Every tab is open from day one (free choice).

### 7.3 The contract record (data, in the game's own record; GAME-LAYER (bb))

```
contract = {
  id, provider, kind: 'contract'|'job'|'challenge'|'build'|'survey',
  title, brief,                         // text keys into the narrative pack (no prose in code)
  stages: [ { subs: [ { do: 'carry'|'fly'|'land'|'deliver'|'survey'|'accept', from?, to?, load?: {kg, pax, bulk?},
                       when?: {before:'dusk'|…}, crit?: [ {k:'seats'|'emptyKg'|'tas'|'endurance'|…, op, v, at?} ] } ] } ],
  pay: { base, perKm?, bonus?: [ {crit, by, pct} ] },
  rep: { provider, gain },
  unlock?: { stage: '<track>:<n>' },    // §11 construction
  needs?: { rep, after?: [contractId] },
  repeat?: false | { every: <contracts done> }
}
```

- Jobs are **generated** from each provider's table (seeded, deterministic per career seed and completed count):
  routes between its fields, loads drawn from its goods list, and pay from the formula (§12.3).
- Arcs (the contracts) are **authored data**, one file per provider, written with the narrative pack and gated by a
  node checker: every field exists, every route is flyable by at least one validated design, every text key resolves.
- **Where it lives:** the contract tables are game data (`src/game/contracts_<provider>.js`, node-loadable). The
  career's progress is in the player document (§13). Construction stages are in the premises record (§11).
  GAME-LAYER's "missions are data in the premises record" is kept in spirit. Contracts reference premises ids (fields,
  sites) but are not stored inside the world record, because they are per-career state, not geography (§16 GQ16).

## 8. THE MAP AND THE CONTRACTS — SnowRunner's shape, fixed where players complain

### 8.1 What SnowRunner does (researched 2026-10-06; sources at the end of this section)

- **One map screen** reached with one key (M), showing the region's maps, the fog of war lifted by watchtowers, garages,
  cargo pickups, contract and task markers, and **every truck where it was left** [2][3][4][17].
- **Three objective kinds**: Contracts (main, chained, better pay), Tasks (side, any order), Contests (timed, repeatable,
  bronze/silver/gold) [9][11]. All share one data model: name, description, recommendation, required rank, blockers,
  **stages in order with sub-stages in any order**, rewards, and `ModelBuildingSettings`, the visible construction a
  stage triggers [5][6][8][10].
- **Employers** are named on every contract, with a logo and a background in the details [2b][21]. Whether the list is
  tabbed by employer is **unconfirmed** (the wikis were unreachable).
- **Accept many, track one**: tracking puts the objective's stages top-right and its markers on the map [11][22][23].
- **An Objects tab** lists every vehicle owned and switches to any of them, across maps [4]. **Recovery** returns a
  truck to the garage (free in normal mode; paid in Hard Mode, ~$25-48k) [3][26].
- **Garages** buy, sell and upgrade; some start broken and are restored by tasks [9][27]. Bridges and buildings go up as
  contracts land, opening the way [3][5][8].
- **What players complain about**:
  - you cannot inspect a job's requirements remotely [24];
  - contract text does not say where the cargo is [28];
  - tiny, look-alike cargo icons [14][28];
  - vague exploration tasks [24];
  - a busy screen [14][29];
  - **contracts your fleet cannot do are not flagged** [14];
  - no self-clearing route markers [18].
- Comparables:
  - **MSFS 2024 career**: a world mission board, licence gates, unnamed AI crew for passive income, criticised as
    grindy [30][31][32][34].
  - **ETS2**: hired drivers with skill points, garages with slots, wages and costs per job [35][36].
  - **OnAir / AirwaySim**: pilots as stats and morale [37][38].
  - **None gives pilots real personalities** (§9 is new ground).

### 8.3 The detail card — what SnowRunner forgets

1. **Everything needed, remotely** [24][28]: where the load is, where it goes, the strip lengths and surfaces at both
   ends (`stripSurface`, metres), the payload (kg, passengers, bulk), the conditions offered, the pay, and the
   provider's line.
2. **Your fleet against this job** [14]: for each airframe the card states **what the certificate says**, without
   judging the pilot's skill. For example: "payload 120 kg: Cub ✓ (148 kg spare) · Jodel ✗ (94 kg) · C172 floats ✓ —
   but the mine strip is 250 m and its certified take-off is 310 m". These are facts from the plaque against the
   job's numbers. **It never forbids** (free choice and the spine): flying the mismatch is allowed, and the card has
   already told you.
3. For a **build contract**: the criteria, each with a ✓ / ✗ / "needs a flight" against any design on your drawing
   board, and a button that opens the garage on a fresh design or a copy of the closest one.
4. **The route**: a planned line (DEST-TO's `flightLeg`: circuit or cross-country), distance, and an estimate of time and
   fuel for the chosen aeroplane. Waypoints are optional, editable, and clear themselves when flown [18].

## 9. THE PILOTS YOU HIRE

The user: *"We need personalities for the pilots to recruit, map them to Mixamo characters and generate (using another
AI) portraits for them."*

### 9.1 A pilot is

```
pilot = {
  id, name, portrait: 'media/portraits/<id>.<hash>.webp', who: '<chars_table key>',
  bio, voice,                                 // text keys (the narrative pack)
  profile: 'club'|'bush'|… | { custom knobs } // PILOT-PERSONA: PILOT_PROFILES / PILOT_PROFILE_KNOBS, clamped
  style: 'cautious'|'normal'|'brisk',         // PILOT_STYLES
  traits: [ 'float-rated', 'night-shy', 'loves-taildraggers', … ],   // §9.3
  wage: { perHour, signOn },
  skill: { landings, hours, byClass: {tw, tri, float, ski} },        // grows (§9.4)
  home: '<aero id>', mood?: …                 // later
}
```

- **The flying is PILOT-PERSONA's profile** (one pilot, a profile, never a fork). A recruitable pilot is a *person*
  wrapped around a profile: the knobs are their hands; the story says why.
- **The body is a Mixamo character** (`pilotWho`). Today there are six; the roster needs ~12-20 people, so either more
  Mixamo characters (download, the user's OK) or one body worn by several people with different textures/clothes
  (§16 GQ13). **The texture budget ruling (LIVE-CREW) gates this**: ~170 MB GPU per character. **Only the pilot in the
  flown aeroplane is loaded** (the fleet's props have no crew, `emptyCockpit`), so the roster costs nothing in the
  world.
- **The portrait** is a 2-D image generated by an external AI from the prompt pack (deliverable 3). The user decides
  which AI and runs it. Each portrait is ~60-120 KB webp, lazy (the Pilots tab only).

### 9.2 The roster and hiring

- **Recruitable pilots** appear in the Pilots tab: 3-4 at a time from a pool of 16, refreshed with the market. Each has
  a portrait, a few lines of bio, their profile as plain words ("quick hands, short fields, slips everything; bank
  limit high"), their traits, their wage and their sign-on fee.
- **The first pilot comes with the opening** (the narrative's companion; §10).
- **Who flies a flight**: you choose a pilot (or "I fly", i.e. manual, or the expert AP as "the ferry service", at a
  high hourly rate). The pilot travels with the aeroplane: a pilot is **where their last aeroplane is**, so pilots are
  physical too (§16 GQ14, default **on**, with a free "the pilot takes the boat home" to HOME between flights).
- **Taking the stick**: at any moment in flight; the pilot's personality yields to you. The logbook records who flew.
- **Wages accrue per flown hour** (ax). Firing is free. A pilot may **refuse** a flight that breaks a trait ("Marie
  won't land on water") with a line of text; you choose another or fly it yourself.

### 9.3 Traits — personality you can feel, all on existing hooks

| trait | effect | hook |
|---|---|---|
| short-field / bush | `technique.field 'short'`, slips | PILOT-PERSONA knobs |
| float-rated / not | refuses water, or the hydro profile | refusal + profile |
| night-shy | refuses legs that end after dusk | the day clock |
| ham-fisted, smooth | the knobs | profile |
| comfort-g low (a careful one) | bank and g limits | `limits` |
| loves taildraggers | +skill growth on tailwheel types | skill |
| chatty / grumpy | radio lines (Sound Coordinator's radio voices) | text + voice |
| a past (crashed once at East Point) | refuses nv_strip until a story job | narrative flag |

### 9.4 Skill growth

PILOT-PERSONALITY §4.9: a profile is data, so it can grow. Each logged landing moves the knobs a small step toward the
"expert" end within the pilot's **ceiling** (a student never becomes a bush pilot unless their story allows it). This
is deterministic from the logbook, so it can be replayed and gated.

## 10. THE NARRATIVE

The user: *"We need to get the narrative right, and that's something we should probably use another AI for."*

**This study fixes the frame; the prompt pack (deliverable 3) asks the external AI for the words.**

### 10.1 Premise (default; the user's call)

> Jolene Island, south-east Alaska. The old Army airfield has been a weed field for decades; the Field Trust leases it
> to anyone who'll bring it back to life. You arrive with a crate of tools, one small aeroplane (a Cub, or a
> drawing-board design you build with your grant) and one pilot who believes in you. The island needs air links. The
> mine wants to reopen, the dock wants its mail, the resort wants guests, the survey office wants eyes on the coast.
> You design the aeroplanes that do the work nobody else's aeroplanes can.

- **The derelict WWII field opens the game** (GAME-LAYER (bg), ISLAND-ANNETTE §10). This study takes it as the
  default for a **career**. The sandbox keeps today's restored field.
- **Tone**: warm and practical, bush-flying, small community; humour from pilots and clients; danger is the weather
  and your own engineering, never villains.
- **Arcs**: one per provider, 4-6 contracts each, and each arc ends with something built (§11). There is a light
  thread across them (the field's restoration) and no failure state beyond money.

### 10.2 Respect for the real place

The island's terrain and place names come from **Annette Island**, which is the **Metlakatla Indian Community's
reserve** (a real, living Tsimshian community). **Default rule for every text and portrait**: all people and
organisations are fictional. Do not depict real tribal governance, culture, regalia or ceremonies, and do not invent
Indigenous lore. Community members appear as ordinary islanders with jobs, never as exotic set dressing. Geographic
names stay. Before anything community-specific is written, consult or omit. This is §16 GQ20, and the prompt pack
carries it as a hard constraint.

## 11. BUILDINGS THAT MISSIONS PUT UP

The user: *"The buildings will move with the missions, getting constructed, etc."*

### 11.1 The mechanism — stages in the world record

- **A premises amendment (v1.x, "stages")**: any site, object or zone may carry `stage: { track: 'mine', show: [2, 9] }`.
  The composition reads the career's **world progress** (`career.tracks = { mine: 3, dock: 1, … }`) and stands an
  element only when its track's value is inside `show`. A track is a small integer, advanced by contract rewards
  (`unlock: { stage: 'mine:3' }`), which is SnowRunner's `ModelBuildingSettings` [5].
- **Three looks per project**: *absent* → **construction** (a fenced plot, crates, a crane or scaffold, a partial frame)
  → **finished**. The construction look is a **construction kit** of existing props (crates, drums, trestles from
  `props_table.py`) plus, at most, two new props (a scaffold section and a small crane; §16 GQ18, asset licences
  CC0/credit-only).
- **Sandbox = every track at max.** Today's island is the finished state, so the sandbox's world is byte-identical
  (gated). **A career starts with the tracks low**: fewer buildings, cheaper frames.
- **When it changes**: at composition only, i.e. at load and at a roll-out / roll-in through the house worker, **never
  in flight** (the trip lessons: HYBRID-TRIPS, LOC-SWITCH). A completed contract shows its new building the next time
  you are on the ground there, with a short reveal shot (the roll-out machinery).
- **Gate**: PREMISES extended. Every stage value composes; sandbox = today's bytes; a stage never puts a building on a
  taxi route or a stand (MILL-TAXI's rules).

### 11.2 The first projects (one per provider)

| provider | track | construction → finished | gameplay effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Field Trust | `field` | the second WWII hangar shell → restored (the HOME.2 plot becomes buyable cheap) | a side-hangar plot at home |
| Jumbo Mine | `mine` | headframe frame → headframe + ore shed | ore jobs (heavy, short field) appear |
| Dock & Cannery | `dock` | pier extension → cold store + slipway | the SEA plot takes a club shell |
| Skyline Resort | `resort` | lodge frame → lodge; the snow strip lengthened (a runway edit) | bigger guest jobs; altiport gets longer |
| Survey Office | `survey` | a proven site gains a windsock + tie-downs → a station | a new aerodrome node (GAME-LAYER §4.2) |

A runway edit (lengthening) is a stage on the **runway's** record. It changes physics (`terrainH`, `surfaceAt`), so it
is applied at composition like every stage, and DEST-TO's choices read the new length.

## 12. THE ECONOMY

### 12.1 Units

**Credits** (₵). The ledger already prices in them (the stock Cub ~30 000). Credits are **not dollars**
(DEFORM §10); they are calibrated as ratios to the airframe prices the ledger already produces.

### 12.2 Costs

| cost | rule (default) | source |
|---|---|---|
| an airframe | ledger price × labour factor (scratch) / catalogue (maker) / listing (used) | §5 |
| **fuel / energy** | **what the sim burned** × the fuel price (avgas 2.2 ₵/L, mogas 1.6, jet 1.4, electricity 0.3 ₵/kWh; fuel sold at fields with a pump, a field property) | the energy module's real flow |
| maintenance reserve | 0.6 % of the hull value **per flown hour** (engine overhaul + airframe) | hull = ledger total |
| pilot wage | per flown hour (8-40 ₵/h by skill), plus a sign-on fee | §9 |
| hangar | own (buy) or rent: 1 % of the price per flown hour (GAME-PREMISES) | `PREM_RATES` |
| landing fee | 0 at your fields; 5-30 ₵ at providers' fields (optional, §16 GQ10, default **off**) | — |
| repair | the bill (DEFORM §10), charged on Repair (dm9), × labour factor; grounded until repaired (dm10) | DMG-D5 |
| recovery ("bring it home") | 200 + 25 / km (career) | `playerRecover` |
| insurance | **none in v1** (§16 GQ11); a write-off (≥ 75 % hull) is lost value | DEFORM §10 |

### 12.3 Income

- **Jobs**: `pay = base(provider) + perKm × km × (1 + payloadFactor) + surface bonus (short / water / snow / altiport)
  + conditions bonus`, tuned so that a well-matched aeroplane nets **~2-4× its running cost per flown hour**, and a
  mismatched one (a big aeroplane on a small job) loses money. This is **ruling az made into a formula**: running cost
  scales with hull value and burn, so no aeroplane flies every route profitably.
- **Contracts (arcs)**: fixed, generous, plus reputation, plus a stage unlock.
- **Build contracts**: the agreed price (default: 1.4 × the ledger cost of a design that just meets the criteria,
  estimated by the provider's table) plus margin bonuses; re-orders at the same price.
- **Selling**: airframes at 0.5-0.8 × hull by condition; salvage (dm10) at 0.1.
- **The starting grant** (career): 60 000 ₵ (GAME-PREMISES Q2: two Cubs' worth) or the Cub itself + 30 000.

### 12.4 Harshness

- **Normal (default)**: everything above. The damage rules dm9-dm12 apply when damage is ON.
- **Relaxed**: no running costs except fuel; free bring-it-home.
- **Hard**: SnowRunner's Hard Mode spirit [20]: recovery doubled, no free reset, salvage only on a write-off.
- dm14 stands (ordinary operations ≤ 2/3 of certified yield), so a normal landing never grounds an aeroplane.
- **Bankruptcy**: the wallet may go negative only through charges that are not choices (GAME-PREMISES Q18). Below
  −20 000 the Trust offers a loan (a story job); no game over (§16 GQ6).

### 12.5 Progression without gates

- **Reputation per provider** (0-5 stars) is the only rank. It scales the size and pay of what that provider offers.
  It never hides a field, a part, a maker or a tab.
- **The money ladder** (GAME-LAYER §5) does the rest: a Caravan-class design is buildable from day one if you can pay
  for it.

```
      tracks: { field:0, mine:0, dock:0, resort:0, survey:0 },

      market: { used: [ listing ], seen: n },

```

## 17. RULINGS PROPOSED (taken as defaults, reversible)

- **(g1)** Build contracts state performance and acceptance criteria, never a configuration; verified by the certificate
  where it can tell, by a flown acceptance leg where it cannot (§6).
- **(g2)** Three property concepts: the main hangar (builds), at most two side hangars (park, repair), stations on any
  runway (§3).
- **(g3)** Planes are physical: an aeroplane stands where it stopped, visible as a prop there; "bring it home" is the
  only teleport (§3.4, §4).
- **(g4)** The fleet props cost "almost nothing": ≤ 6 drawn, L3-only on the light presets, no capture/bake in flight,
  bake on save (§4.3).
- **(g5)** Designs and airframes are different things: designs are free and shared, airframes are owned and cost (§5.4).
- **(g6)** Only validated builds are sold by makers; a validation adds a model (§5.1).
- **(g7)** No progression gate hides the island, a maker or a part; reputation scales offers only (§12.5).
- **(g8)** The sandbox is today's game byte for byte (world tracks at max, no wallet); the game is opt-in (§14).
- **(g9)** Buildings change at composition only, never in flight (§11).
- **(g10)** All people and organisations in the narrative are fictional; the real community is not depicted without
  consultation (§10.2).
- **(g11)** v1 resolves one flight at a time; background resolution is the later observatory (§2).
