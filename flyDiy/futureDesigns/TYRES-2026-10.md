# TYRES — a study of real light-aircraft tyres (G3850 draft, G3851 finished, 27 Oct 2026)

works, redo a study on real aircraft tyres, ensure the Tundra wheel are good, because fitting huge wheels is really all
the rage today, so these need to look good. The tread options can go, the normal wheels should stay, and tiny planes
should still be able to get tiny wheels."

Ratios: W/D = section width / outside diameter (OD); rim/D = rim (bead seat) diameter / OD; section height H =
(OD - rim) / 2, and H/W its aspect. In the code (tools/_gear_gen.js TYRE) `hw` = W/D and `bead` = rim/D.

How it was checked (G3851): the Goodyear data book PDFs (goodyearaviation.com) and Aircraft Spruce's spec PDF are
refused by this cloud session's network policy, so the 6.00-6 and 8.00-6 rows were re-checked through dealer pages
that reproduce the data book's rows (Boeing Distribution, aircraftsupply.com, Aircraft Spruce). Midpoints of the
data book's max/min are used for the ratios.

## 2. What the numbers say

- **Three families, three shapes.** Small tailwheel / ultralight tyres sit on a BIG rim for their size (rim/D
  0.47–0.50 for the 8" tailwheel, 11x4.00-5 and 13x5.00-6; the 4.00-4 is the exception, 0.33). GA Type III tyres are
  rounder (rim/D 0.31–0.36, W/D 0.34–0.40, H/W ~0.9). Bush tyres are balloons on a SMALL hub (rim/D 0.19–0.29, W/D
  0.42–0.46) — the hub almost disappears inside the tyre.
- **The rim does not grow with the tyre.** Everything from the 5.00-5 to the 8.50-6 rides a 5–6" wheel; the bush tyres
  26–31" a 6" one, the 35" a 10" one. Today's model scales the standard hub with the tyre radius.
- **Width grows faster than the diameter** across the GA sizes: 0.34–0.36 up to the 6.00-6, 0.40 at the 8.00-6.
- **The crown is round, not flat, at the shoulder.** The 6.00-6's shoulder is at 90 % of the OD and 88 % of the width:
  a flat tread band that rolls off ~0.10 R into a short sidewall, widest about half-way up the section.
- **Tread.** GA and small tyres: 3–4 circumferential grooves (2 on the smallest), shallow (the Desser 8.50-6 skid
  depth 0.160" = 0.014 D). Bush tyres: a round crown that runs straight into the sidewall, covered with LOW (3/16–1/4",
  ~0.6–0.8 % of OD) wide lugs with wide voids; no circumferential ribs. A slick balloon (today's tundra on whTread 1)
  and a ribbed balloon (whTread 0) both match nothing.

## 4. In code (G3851: tools/_gear_gen.js tyreLook(), TYRE_LOOK, tyreLugs(), wheel())

Today (G3548): `hw` / `bead` = 0.40 / 0.42 standard, 0.44 / 0.25 tundra, 0.29 / 0.46 slim; the standard and slim hubs
scale off R, the tundra's off `Rh = R bead / 0.42`.

The proposal, **display only and off by default** (`GEAR_GEN.TYRE_LOOK.on`, `?tyres=new` on the page):

- **The rim follows a real hub, not the tyre:** rim radius `0.031 m + 0.20 R`, so `bead = clamp(0.031 / R + 0.20,
  0.26, 0.50)` for the standard and slim carcasses; `Rh = R bead / 0.42` for EVERY profile (the hub follows its rim).
- **The tundra's hub is the 6" wheel** under 26–31" and the 10" under the 35": rim radius `0.0762 + max(0, R - 0.40)
  1.1` m, `bead = clamp(that / R, 0.18, 0.30)`; W/D interpolated on the Bushwheel table (0.46 / 0.45 / 0.42 / 0.43 at
  R 0.33 / 0.368 / 0.394 / 0.445, held at the ends).
- **Standard W/D 0.36** (from 0.40) to R 0.22, rising to 0.40 by R 0.245; the widest point half-way up the section
  (`bead + 0.45 (R - bead)`, G3548's 0.60 R); grooves by size (2 under R 0.12, 3 to R 0.235, 4 above), 0.034 R deep
  (G3548 0.052 R); a plain sidewall (no moulded rings).
- **The tundra stays G3548's** under the proposal (tyreLook(R, 1) answers null; the gate holds the drawing equal).
- **The lugged variant** (TYRE_LOOK.lugs, `?tyres=new&lugs=1`; tyreLook(R, 1, { lugs: 1 })): low chevron lugs on the
  torus (tyreLugs), each bar ONE swept section with shared vertices (a slightly crowned top, a bevel on each edge, a
  short wall into the carcass; both ends drawn in to 62 %: the bevel round the ends), so the top shades smooth; `round(20 R / 0.394)` pairs held 16–24, height
  0.014 R (0.7 % of D: 5.5 mm on a 31"), bars from 0.05 rad off the crown to 1.22 rad onto the shoulder, 40 % lug /
  60 % void round the tyre, each bar running 0.42 pitch round as it goes out (the two rows read as a V), the rows offset
  half a pitch (the offset-block chevron), the bar's height running out over the shoulder's last fifth.
- **Floors and ceilings on the hub's small parts** (metres, proposal only), so a 10 cm wheel is not a hub with a ring
  of rubber and a 35" tundra not a dustbin lid: bolt heads r 3.5–7 mm (4 bolts at most at R <= 0.10), hub cap r 12–45
  mm, valve stem drawn on a 0.10–0.17 m hub scale, rim wall 2.5–7 mm, brake disc 1.28 x the rim radius (a Cleveland's
  7.6" disc on a 6" wheel; G3548's is 1.48 x), its bolt ring 3 mm, caliper 32–60 x 48–90 x 24–48 mm.
- **Tread per carcass, whTread and whRibs ignored** (G3852, AIRCRAFT-LOOKS): ribbed grooves on the standard / slim,
  lugs on the tundra. whTread stays in every file as loaded: it is a hashed cage row (bench.js benchStripCosmetic keeps
  every non-default cage row), so it is read and ignored, never rewritten or stripped - procure keeps writing 1.
- Display only: s1R/s2R/twR (what the flight reads) never move; no PHYSICS_V, no certificate withdrawn. With the
  switch off the wheel is G3548's byte for byte (the same positions and indices for every profile x tread x size).
- Known reader of TYRE outside wheel(): the weathering's wheel record (`_cage_join.js` / `_cage_ui.js`: hw = R x
  TYRE[profile].hw, the mud band's width). It still reads G3548's table; the integration should read `tyreLook(R,
  profile).wd` when the switch becomes the default.

Sizes to render (R in m): tiny 0.07 (5.5"), tailwheel 0.10 (8"), 11x4.00-5 0.135, 5.00-5 0.18, 6.00-6 0.222,
8.00-6 0.243, tundra 26" 0.33, 29" 0.368, 31" 0.394, 35" 0.445. The renders (reports/evidence/TYRES/): the user's Cub
(s1R 0.22) on its own tyres and on 26 / 31 / 35" tundras, a Chinook-like ultralight on 8" mains and a 5.5" tailwheel.
