## 2. The answer to "voxels?"

Not voxel MESHES (a Minecraft cloud is neither realistic nor shades). **A voxel-ish DENSITY
FIELD sampled by rays** — the Guerrilla / "Nubis" method (Schneider 2015/2017; Horizon,
RDR2, MSFS 2020 are all this family): a 2D WEATHER MAP (coverage, type, height) drives a
3D noise field (Perlin-Worley base, Worley detail eroding the edges), a ray per pixel
marches the layer between base and top, accumulates extinction by Beer-Lambert, and at
each step marches a short second ray toward the sun for self-shadow. This is the standard
that "looks good, shades good, coherent and realistic" means in 2026, and every piece of
light it needs already exists in `atmo.js`. It is GLSL on today's WebGLRenderer (WebGL2 has
3D textures: `Data3DTexture` is in the r186 vendor), port-cheap under §4k (one standalone
program, ONE explicit `ATMO.inject`, no `#include` beyond the tone-map tail).

**Two layers, not one.** Real skies are a LOW layer (the volumetric one: stratus /
stratocumulus / cumulus at the day's base) under a HIGH veil (cirrus / cirrostratus at
8–10 km: thin, no self-shadow worth marching, bright toward the sun). The high veil is a 2D
layer on the dome shader — a few lines, and it is most of what makes a sky look like a
photograph rather than a render. Towering cumulus / cumulonimbus is the low layer with a
tall height profile; anvils are a later type.

## 3. Architecture (every consumer derives from one field)

```
world.day  (exists)   cloudCover, cloudType, cloudBase, rh, wind, the clock
      │
CLOUDS (src/viewer/clouds.js; the field's CPU mirror in src/core/08_cloud_field.js, pure)
  ├─ THE WEATHER MAP  512² tiling, 60 km span, RGBA = coverage | type-mix | height-scale | wetness
  │     = f(seed, day.cloudCover, day.cloudType, drift)   drift = wind at the base × the clock's seconds
  │     generated on the CPU (a few ms; re-generated only when cover/type change, drifted by a uniform)
  ├─ THE NOISE  base 128³ RGBA (Perlin-Worley + 3 Worley octaves), detail 32³ RGB (Worley) — Data3DTexture,
  │     generated ONCE in a Worker at boot (~1 s, 8 MB), deterministic seed; a curl 2D texture for wisps
  ├─ THE DENSITY  rho(p) = remap(base, 1 − coverage(p), 1, 0, 1) · heightProfile(type, h) · erode(detail)
  │     the height profile: st flat + thin (200–400 m), sc lumpy (600 m), cu a rounded bottom + towering top
  │     (500–1800 m by the map's height-scale), cb tall; the base = day.cloudBase for every type
  ├─ THE MARCH (the pass): per pixel, the ray's [t0, t1] through the slab (camera under / in / above),
  │     stopped at the scene's depth (the AA target's DepthTexture — mountains poke through the layer),
  │     N adaptive steps (long where empty, short in cloud), blue-noise jittered start,
  │     per step: 6-step light march toward the sun (Beer + the powder term), dual-lobe HG phase,
  │     Wrenninge's multi-scatter octaves (3 × [0.5, 0.5, 0.5]), ambient = skyIrradiance(top) above /
  │     ground albedo × sun below, the sun's colour = sunTransmittance(altitude) from the LUT,
  │     then the SAME aerial perspective and mist the scene gets (the AP atlas at the cloud's mean depth),
  │     tone-mapped in the pass (the AA target is display-space), premultiplied alpha over the frame
  ├─ RESOLUTION  half-res colour+alpha target, 2×2 checkerboard over 4 frames with reprojection
  │     (the previous frame's view-projection; clouds are far, the aeroplane's motion is small on them),
  │     depth-aware upsample at the terrain's silhouette; a `full` tier for screenshots (every pixel, every frame)
  ├─ THE SHADOW  a 2D optical-depth map from the weather map (an analytic column, no march) projected
  │     along the sun onto the world in ONE chunk splice (`lights_fragment_begin`: directLight.color ×
  │     cloudT(world p)) — terrain, water, houses, the aeroplane, the trees (their own shader reads
  │     directionalLights[0] at trees.js:204 — a line there); the key light at the CRAFT's position
  │     through SKY_LIGHT (under a cumulus the cockpit dims; under overcast the sun is diffuse, the
  │     hemisphere relatively brighter); the probe's bake draws the layer (the WATER REFLECTS THE CLOUDS)
  ├─ IN CLOUD  the density at the eye (CPU mirror) feeds the mist's uniform: the white-out is the mist's
  │     own machinery at the cloud's density, the sun through it the light march's answer
  └─ THE HIGH VEIL  cirrus / cirrostratus on the dome: a 2D noise sheet at 9 km, coverage from the type
        (cu days carry a thin veil by cover), lit by the sun's T at 9 km with a forward peak; the 22° halo
        as a later dial
```

**What is deliberately NOT here:** god-rays (a later pass off the same shadow map),
precipitation (rain / snow: the wetness channel is reserved), lightning, a second low layer.
Nothing streams; the field is a pure function of (seed, the day, the clock) so gates and
screenshots reproduce.

## 5. Traps known before the first line

- **The AA target is display-space.** The cloud pass must tone-map its own output (the dome's
  idiom: `#include <tonemapping_fragment> <colorspace_fragment>`) and blend premultiplied in
  display space — alpha over linear light would double-darken the edges.
- **A DepthTexture on an MSAA target** needs `DepthStencilFormat` + `UnsignedInt248Type` because
  the target carries the G131 stencil; verify `resolveDepthBuffer` in the vendor before C1's pass.
- **`cloneUniforms` nulls render-target textures** — the shadow map sampler goes through the
  prototype hook / `ATMO.inject`, exactly as the AP atlas does; the scalars on a shared
  Float32Array.
- **The units bug, a fourth time in waiting:** the cloud's radiance is in the sun's units
  (E_sun = 1) × `U.scale` — never judged at exposure ~1; the night's clouds are lit by the moon
  through `eMoon` like the dome.
- **Reprojection under a rolling aeroplane:** the camera turns fast; clouds are far so parallax
  is small, but the ROLL is not — reproject in NDC with the full previous view-projection, not a
  translation; fall back to the fresh sample where the history's depth disagrees.
- **The Browser pane is hidden** — prove with the CDP rig (`sky_shots.js`), sample pixels.
- **The peers sweep the working copy** — every edit exact-string from the worktree.

## 7. A6 (2026-09-20): the slab's cause, and several decks — LANDED (HANDOVER G436)

The playtest's "blocky shapes, clear bands" was the noise, not the march: the baked
Perlin-Worley sat at 0.63–0.85 (measured with the probe's percentiles) and the density's
`remap(pw, lf − 1, 1)` raised it further, above every coverage threshold — a cloud was its
weather texel extruded between the base and the cap. The bake is stretched to the byte by its
measured percentiles and the erosion subtracts the Worley fbm by a per-type `erode`.

**Several decks.** `day.cloudUpper = [{ cover, type, base? }]` (two at most) over the low
layer; `CLOUD_FIELD.layers(day)` stacks them without overlap; `clouds.js` marches each live
deck's segment in the order of entry along the ray, one transmittance, one step budget; the
weather maps are one 2D array texture; the shadow tile bakes every deck with the relative shift
along the sun; a deck under another takes the upper deck's softened shadow. Types gained
altocumulus / altostratus (and every type its `erode`, `period`, `alt`). The cover is fitted to
the SKY's share as seen from the ground (skyFraction), by inflating the map, never a gain.
`?cloud=0.45,cu;0.35,ac`; the rail, the editor and F8 carry the decks. The rig:
`tools/cloud_shot.js`. Proof: `screenshots/clouds-2026-09-20/`.
