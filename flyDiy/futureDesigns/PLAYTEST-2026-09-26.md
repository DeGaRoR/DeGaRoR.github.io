### 0.1 The rig

`tools/rollout_perf.js`

**Why it exists.** Every earlier rig measured a *paused* scene after the streamers had settled, or only the boot, and always in a RIG browser. G586 keeps a rig browser on the legacy one-step-per-call clock. So no number had ever been taken of the window the user found unplayable.

**What it runs.**
- A HEADED Chrome with vsync on, at the user's viewport of 2216x1023.
- The live G586 clock, with the pilot taxiing and the premises stream in flight.
- `--cold`: a fresh profile, meaning no shader cache, no service worker and no bake cache. That is a first visit from Pages.

**What it records, per rendered frame.**
- The loop's JavaScript (`FLYDIY_PACE.end`'s `workMs`).
- Solver ms, steps owed, `worldUpdate` ms.
- CPU time of every `renderer.render` call and of three's shadow pass.
- Premises stream steps and their ms.
- The frame cap, the phase, AGL and CG.
- Long tasks from the first byte.

**How it reads the frames.**
- stand / taxi / takeoff / air come from ground speed and AGL, not from the pilot's phase name (TAXI covers the stand).
- It prints the four ROLLOUT targets as PASS/FAIL (§4).

**Variants, none of which needs a code change.**
- `--build <json>`: loads a garage build into the WIP slot.
- `--variant nomet`: filters `mk_*` out of the premises through the WORLD rail's saved-copy slot.
- `--variant nopremises`
- `--gfx '<json>'`
- `--world none`

### 0.3 What the numbers say

1. **Metlakatla is the single largest cost.**
   - Taxi goes from 12-15 fps to 30 (the cap), and the frame p90 from 167-183 ms to 33 ms.
   - Render CPU falls from about 40 ms to 12, and the stream from about 20 ms per frame to 0.
   - The catch-up spiral stops: 3.5-3.9 steps per frame become 1.9.
   - The cut the user chose is right.
2. **The remaining premises (AFB, mine, native village, tramway) cost the SOLVER five times**, and the ring build 7-8 s.
   - Stock solver: 2.1 ms per frame with no premises, against 11 ms without Metlakatla. The composed terrain (road grades, flattening, stamps) is queried per node per substep.
   - The same query makes the roll-out's ring prewarm take 9 s instead of 1.4.
   - **Fix: bake the composed terrain into a height raster at load**, which the solver, ring and cover then sample. This was added to A1-PHYS.
3. **The aluminium Cessna's solver** is 64 ms per frame (about 16 ms per step) with the stream on, and 20 ms median with a **72 ms p90** without it.
   - That is the step debt: a slow frame owes 3-4 steps, which makes the next frame slow.
   - A1-PHYS's softened flight box, plus the terrain raster, plus the step-debt guard, is the answer.
4. **Shadows are secondary** for FPS: -8 to -11 ms of render CPU, with no change to the taxi fps while the town streams. They are **first-order for looks**. The stripes are CONFIRMED as the reversed-depth bias regression: `rollout_shot_base_3s.png` shows the hachures on the wing and tail, and `rollout_shot_depthlog_3s.png` (`?depth=log`) is clean.
5. **The auto frame cap wastes performance.**
   - The procedural island does 16 ms of loop JS but sits at the 30 cap 94 % of the time.
   - The run with no premises, starting clean, holds 60.
   - Auto drops to 30 during a hitch (the reveal, a stream item) and trials 60 again only when the one-step work is under 12.5 ms, with a 20 s to 5 min backoff.
   - **The rule should recover.** Added to A1-PHYS, which owns the frame clock's step logic.
6. **Loading.**
   - The `world` step is one 15-18 s task, the premises make plus the 3 km drain; without premises it is 1.9 s.
   - The ring takes 9 s.
   - Jolene's compile is 13-15 s warm and 36-37 s cold; the procedural island's is 1.2 s, so the Jolene-only programs (splat ground, pavement, premises) dominate.
   - These are A4-FREEZE's targets. The cold compile is the first impression.
7. **Cold vs warm.** Cold costs about 20-25 s more on the roll-out screen (compile) and slightly worse frames. The warm profile's second run is the fair "later runs" the user described as better.

### 0.8 After A2-FADES (G670-G673)

A cell planted in view grows in over 0.8 s from its birth (`aBorn`, per instance). ### 0.10 After A2-GLINT (G960)

The user, flying at 200-330 m toward the AFB and the houses beyond: "all landmarks, houses and little towns emit crazy flashes, and I think the bloom effect has them explode." And earlier: "Even the soft bloom is far too bloomy. One fifth or one third of the intensity would feel better."

**The source was not specular. It was the lot patches' alpha, extrapolated.**
- **The rig:** `tools/glint_probe.js` and `tools/glint_bisect.js`, two page expressions run as a view's `report` in `tools/shadowsky_shots.js`.
  - The probe reads the resolve target: the frame as radiance, half float, what the bloom's threshold reads.
  - It counts NaN, Inf and negative pixels, the pixels over 1.6 / 4 / 16 / ... in luminance, and the lone fireflies.
  - It raycasts the hottest pixels.
  - The bisect hides one scene subtree at a time and descends into whichever one removes the bad pixels.
- **Where:** eight bearings round the AFB, 3 km out at 300 m AGL, looking at it, 2216x1023, gamer (8x MSAA, linear compositing, soft bloom).
- **What the probe found:**
  - The hot pixels were isolated single pixels on the horizon, 4.7-6.5 km out, in the procedural village south of the AFB (x 300..1450, z -2500..-2800).
  - Their radiance reached **8 188 = 65 504 / 8**: ONE of a pixel's eight samples at the half-float ceiling.
  - Many carried **negative** channels, which read as magenta, blue or cyan after the tone map.
  - Nothing was NaN or Inf.
  - The centre raycast named matte surfaces (Lambert patches, roughness-1 town boxes), which cannot glint.
- **What the bisect found:** one culprit in all three views, `premises:lots` batches, the lot patches of `lot_tex.js`.
  - They are a transparent MeshStandardMaterial whose alpha and splat weights are per-vertex varyings.
- **Live debug variants of that material** (`dbgAlpha` / `dbgDiffA` / `dbgNorm` / `dbgDirSpec`) settled it:
  - `vAlpha` was outside [0, 1] on 294 pixels, and the colour weights (vSplat, vTone) likewise.
  - The normal and the specular were clean.
- **The mechanism:** under MSAA a pixel on a sliver's edge is shaded at its centre, OUTSIDE the triangle, so the varyings are extrapolated. 5 km out a lot's triangles are slivers.
  - The blend `src x a + dst x (1 - a)` then wrote +65 000 or a negative radiance into one sample.
  - The resolve averaged it into a white or coloured pixel.
  - The bloom blew that pixel into a flare the size of a wingtip.
  - Which pixels do this changes with every camera move: the flicker.

**The fix (G960):**
- `lot_tex.js` clamps every weight it reads (alpha, splat, tone) to [0, 1].
- The same guard goes on the other shaders whose blend weight is a varying: the house ground skirt (`vSkA`), the chimney smoke and the plume (`vAge`: `pow(1 - vAge)` was a NaN past 1; `vFade`).

**The bloom (post_fx.js):**
- The first downsample takes its four pixels through three steps:
  - a NaN / Inf guard;
  - a luminance clamp (16 in linear);
  - Karis's 1 / (1 + luma) average, so a lone hot pixel stays under the threshold.
- The gains are cut to a third of their visual strength: soft 0.35 -> 0.12, strong 0.6 -> 0.16, display tier 0.32 / 0.7 -> 0.11 / 0.2.

**The far glass (`_house_gen.js` shadeGlass, the town's glass rides it):**
- A pane a few pixels wide gets rougher (up to 0.5) and loses its Fresnel lift and its own 2.3 environment gain. The factor is `gFarK`, the pane's metres per pixel, from `fwidth` of the position.
- Its effect in this scene is nil: past HLOD.near (150 m) a cell's houses are the far mesh or the boxes, one roughness-1 / metalness-0 material with NO glass, and the probe found no glint on either.
- It stands for the glass that does draw far: detail-cut houses outside the HLOD cells, and the near town from low passes.

### 1.1 FPS at the stand, roll-out and taxi, ranked

**1. The far-premises stream runs while the aircraft is parked.**
- **What streams.** The fixture `tools/fixtures/island_jolene.json` rev 21 holds Metlakatla (`mk_`: 61 sites, 62 roads, 40 objects, 24 zones, 17 terrain types), plus the mine (`mn_`), the native village (`nv_`) and the tramway (`tw_`).

- **The stream.** The rest streams at one item every 3rd rendered frame, from any distance, parked or not (`render_world.js:5516`).
- **What one item costs** (`render_premises.js:1939-1956`, `buildHouse` :1586-1608), about 45 ms per house (PERF G555):
  - A new 25-material finish.
  - `HG.build` with a voxel AO bake.
  - Props.
  - A vertex walk for the obstacle raster.
  - The lot dressing, which is a second house build for the outbuilding.
  - A full 1024² `paintWear()` repaint (:806-820), which the game probably never reads.
  - `LIFE.dirty()`.
  - `freezeStatic` over 8 groups.
- **Optimizations blocked while it runs.** Every town optimization is gated on the WHOLE queue being empty (`hlodTick` :1338-1340, `freezeStatic` :92-93, `settleTick` :1370, `scenery_life.js:958`):
  - HLOD G559 and the G560 glass cut.
  - Near bake G566 and the texture-array town G574.

  - The GRAPHICS "town detail" row.
- **The burst when it empties.** `hlodBuild()` runs twice (the TARR `onReady` clears the signature, :1216). `TARR.build()` produces about 84 MB of DataArrayTextures through canvas `getImageData` (`house_tarr.js:274-308`), and new program variants link synchronously.
- **Why no benchmark saw it.** No worker exists (the G555 note asked for one), and no benchmark ever measured this window.

**2. Ground physics** (`futureDesigns/PHYSICS-PERF-2026-09-24.md`).
- **The substep cap.** The user's aluminium Cessna flies at the 200-substep cap because of its metal wing box (WB-WB beams on about 0.9 kg nodes). Before G572-G580 it cost 48.7 ms per step on the Jolene taxi.
- **Where the time goes.** On the ground about 47 % of the solver is composed-terrain sampling, 28 % of it the premises road grades. Every node in contact reads `terrainH` every substep (`src/core/30_solver.js:1336-1373`); the obstacle pass runs per substep (:1434-1448).
- **The frame clock multiplies it.** G586 owes 2 steps per frame at the 30 cap, and up to 4 when frames are longer. This is a catch-up spiral: a slow frame owes more steps, which makes the next frame slower.
- **Why the air is better.** In the air the ceiling skip removes the ground pass, hence "much better once off the ground".

**3. Draw load at the stand.**
- **Club hangar:** `genHangarBuild`, 278 meshes, all casting, unmerged, with a LOD swap only at 320 m (`render_world.js:4793-4898`, +279 draws measured). It sits in the near-shadow box, which is redrawn every frame (`render_world.js:886-903`).

### 1.3 Flicker, shadows, sky, water

**Shadow stripes (140339, 145145) are a REGRESSION.**
- Commit `90a0eae5` ("PERF wip 1", 2026-09-23) switched to a reversed depth buffer (`app.js:153-158`).
- three r186's PCF path still does `shadowCoord.z += shadowBias` with no reversed-depth case; only VSM has one.
- So both negative biases now push every receiver into its own shadow:
  - world sun: `bias -0.0009`, `normalBias 0.18` (`render_world.js:866-873`);
  - aircraft map: `-0.0004`, `normalBias 0.02` (`shadow_near.js:43`).
- The aircraft has received its own shadow since G436.3 (`e08f2d83`, `shadow_near.js:122-124`), and the wing skin is double-sided (`aeroskin.js:3966`), which gives the wavy chordwise stripes.
- **To confirm:** `?depth=log` should remove them.

**Aircraft shadow: blurred, crawling, trailing.**
- **Crawling edge:** r186 PCF is 5 taps on a disc rotated by a fixed screen-space noise, designed for TAA that the game does not have. On the 6 cm near-map texel the edge dithers and crawls.
- **The trailing ghost:**
  - Above about 22 m AGL (or with the shadow more than 21 m away) the aircraft is also drawn into the WORLD map (`shadow_near.js:143-145`).
  - The world map redraws every 2nd frame (`SHADOW_RATE.every = 2`, `render_world.js:5606`, from `a3a1a7f6`, 2026-09-23). That is 15 Hz at the 30 cap.
  - Inside the 60 m box the ground takes `min(near, far)` (:59).
  - Result: a coarse, stale copy that blinks on alternate frames.

**Forest-floor square (150251).**
- It is the COVER pass (`render_world.js:607-621`, 750-800): a top-down render of the tree billboards over ±1400 m into a 2048² mask.
  - Re-rendered every 20 m of eye movement.
  - Read at mip 5.5 (about 62 m).
  - Multiplies the colour by `uFloor = 0.30`.
- It is applied only on the far-terrain material (`canopyHook` :1987,1993,2176). Away from home that shows as a dark 2.8 km square with a hard edge.
- Every regeneration shifts the coarse mip grid, which is the flicker.
- **A deterministic mask already exists:**
  - the ground stack's `shade` layer (multiply 0.7 from `ISLA.canopy`, `render_world.js:1288-1294`, :1454, :1324);
  - the baked 512² `forestMask` (:1126, 1146-1164).
- **To confirm:** `WORLD_RIG.set({ floor: 1 })`.

**Runway and road flicker (141608, 144959, 145145, 142931).**
- **Depth precision is fine:** reversed float depth, near 0.5.
- **Ground pierces the pavement.**
  - Pavement is transparent, `depthWrite: false`, lifted only 5-8 cm (`render_premises.js:730,767,929`; `render_world.js:4740`).
  - The airfield ground patch changes LOD (2/4/8/16 m) with camera distance, with up to about 40 cm of error (`render_premises.js:549-594`).
  - So ground triangles poke through, and which ones do changes with the camera, even when paused.
- **Overlapping runways.** They share `renderOrder = 2` at the same origin, so the draw order is the creation order, which changes with streaming. That is why "it changed between two runs". `pavedKeep` (:623-637) fades only the gravel band, per vertex (6 × 3 m), so the sides stay visible at intersections.
- **Treads and cracks.** The tyre-lug treads (16 cm pitch), cracks and rut slopes are per-pixel sharp, which MSAA does not help (`pavement.js:650-679`, 784-867). The tiled look switches on at 900 m in one hard step (`gPlain = step(...)`, :692).
- **Normals blur at distance.**

**Downwind limit cycle (142036, 142343).**

### 2.3 Is the web the limit?

**Partly, but not where this playtest hurt.** WebGL2 has real limits:
- One JS thread submits everything.
- The driver cost per draw is higher than native.
- There is no compute and no indirect draw (WebGPU lifts both).

**The failures measured here are ARCHITECTURAL.** A native engine given the same design would choke the same way:
1. **Content generated at runtime on the main thread.** 641 unique procedural houses, each with a 25-material finish and a voxel AO bake, built while you play.
2. **Material and draw explosion.** 7,805 house materials (1,447 distinct), a 278-mesh hangar, and about 90 uncullable transparent pavement draws with per-pixel loops.
3. **Physics sharing the frame.** A 200-substep solver pays 2-4 steps per frame on the ground, on the same thread that renders.
4. **Shadow maps re-rendered wholesale.** Every frame or every other frame, over whole-scene traversals.

**What state-of-the-art engines do instead (MSFS, UE/Unity open worlds): cook, instance, thread.**
- **Cook.** Content is processed OFFLINE into streaming-ready packs: merged, atlased, LODed, HLOD'd, with baked AO and lightmaps. The runtime only uploads.
- **Instance.** A town is a few dozen archetypes × instances, and far cells become impostors or low-poly HLODs. Materials are few (atlases, texture arrays), so draws are few.
- **Thread.** Simulation, streaming, decoding and generation run on worker threads with a per-frame budget. The render thread only submits.
- They also cache shadows (static cascades, only moving casters redrawn) and use TAA, which makes cheap soft shadows and sub-pixel detail stable.

### 2.4 The recommended direction (chantiers after batch B)
1. **Physics in a Web Worker.**
   - The core already runs in node (`tools/flight_core.js`), so it is portable.
   - The frame then costs max(render, physics) instead of the sum.
   - The pilot moves with it, and the render reads node positions from a transferable or shared buffer. On GitHub Pages, SharedArrayBuffer needs COOP/COEP headers, possible through a service worker; plain `postMessage` of about 100 nodes per frame is already cheap.
   - Biggest structural win for the ground phases.
2. **Cooked premises.**
   - Extend `tools/world_prep.js`, which already bakes the terrain pack, to bake the premises: merged per 256 m cell, texture-array atlas (G574's work moves offline), LODs and HLOD impostors, obstacle rasters, lot grounds.
   - The page streams cells by aircraft distance on a 2-4 ms budget and generates nothing.
3. **Towns as an instanced kit.**
   - 20-40 house archetypes baked offline, one atlas material, InstancedMesh per archetype, per-instance tint and wear.
   - 641 houses in under 50 draws.
   - This is the scalable form of Metlakatla, and the way to bring it back.
4. **WebGPU (three r186 `WebGPURenderer` + TSL):** later, not now. Every `onBeforeCompile` hook and custom ShaderMaterial (pavement, splat, water, clouds, trees) would have to be rewritten. The gain (compute culling, indirect draw) matters only after items 1-3.

**Larger maps.** Yes, with tiled terrain packs (already the Jolene shape), cooked premises and proximity streaming. No, with runtime generation: this playtest is the proof, since a few hundred buildings saturated the frame.

**Is the town approach wrong?** For scale, yes: a unique procedural build per house with a runtime AO bake. The procedural GENERATOR is still valuable. Its place is the offline cook, and the kit's archetypes are generator outputs.
