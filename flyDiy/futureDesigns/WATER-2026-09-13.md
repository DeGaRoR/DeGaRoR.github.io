# WATER — the seaplane, the hull, and the surface it lands on

2026-09-13, from the user's design session: *"we have an island, it's all about
little planes, and it's Alaska, so obviously we need seaplanes… the key is the
management of drag and particularly sudden and abrupt changes. Kerbal does that
really bad — the full drag is applied as soon as you touch something, so it's
impossible to land… no corners cut."* Three separations the user asked for and
this document keeps: **the plane**, **water physics**, **water visuals**.

Measured on this tree unless marked INFERRED. Nothing landed.

**A NAMING TRAP, flagged before anyone falls in it.** In this codebase

namespace — free; `test_hydro.js` is world data and would be renamed or
joined by `test_hydrodyn.js`).

## 1. THE PLANE — three configurations, and the step

### 1.1 Floats are UNDERCARRIAGE in the architecture and a BODY in the physics

That seam is the whole design decision. `gearLayout` is already a `starter`
row (tail / trike). Floats become the third option and **amphibians the
fourth** — and amphibians are not a luxury on Ursoy: retractable wheels in
floats let ONE aeroplane use both the strips and the water, which is a real
gameplay fact and exactly the "collect planes, fly the island" loop.

But a float is not a gear leg with a different mesh. A leg is a contact point
with a spring; a float is a **distributed planing surface**. It needs its own
physical representation (§2), while keeping its place in the undercarriage
row, the mass ledger and the editor's part tree.

### 1.2 The three configurations

| | what it is | what it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **twin float** | two pontoons on struts | the float generator, strut geometry, water rudder |
| **single float + tip floats** | one main float, small wingtip floats | the same generator at two scales; asymmetric roll behaviour |
| **flying boat** | the HULL is the fuselage | a hull section family for the cage's fuselage: deadrise, chine, a step; plus sponsons or tip floats |

An amphibian is any of the three plus retractable wheels, and it inherits the
existing `retract` row.

### 1.3 MODEL THE STEP — this is the one non-negotiable

A float or hull has a **step**: a transverse break in the planing bottom,
roughly under the CG. At rest the bottom is held by suction; the step is what
admits air and lets the hull break free and rise onto the plane. Aft of the
step the bottom unwets once planing, and the wetted area collapses.

**Get the step into the geometry and the hump falls out of the physics. Leave
it out and the take-off must be fudged forever.** It is one parameter
(station) plus one (depth), and it is the difference between a seaplane and a
boat that flies.

### 1.4 The water rudder

A small retractable blade at the float's stern. Without it you cannot taxi —
a seaplane weathervanes hard (the fin is in the air, the pivot is in the
water) and has no brakes. Architecturally this is the same shape of thing as
`twSteer` (`62_gen_aero.js:937`), and §2.6 says why it is not optional.

### 1.5 Mass, CG, and the join

Floats are heavy and sit low and forward. **The join must MEASURE the drawn
float** — planing length, beam, deadrise, step station, and the buoyant volume
— exactly as `TAIL-CHANTIER-2` is teaching `Sh`/`Sv` to be measured. A float
whose drawn size does not reach the physics is the tail defect in a new organ,
and this document exists early enough to prevent it rather than audit it later.

## 2. WATER PHYSICS

### 2.1 The diagnosis is right, and it names the design rule

KSP applies full drag on contact because it treats water as a BOOLEAN: in or
out. The physical truth is that **immersion is continuous, and force scales
with wetted area and depth**. At touchdown a float kisses the surface with a
few centimetres of immersion over a small area — the drag is small, and grows
as the aeroplane settles. **That gradient IS the landing.**

**THE RULE: every hydrodynamic term is a smooth function of immersion depth,
computed PER PANEL, and there is no boolean anywhere in the model.**

### 2.2 Hydro panels — the aero strip, in a different fluid

The solver already sums strip forces over a node-beam frame: each strip has an
area, a chord, a local frame and attach weights `w: [[nodeIdx, weight]]`
(`62_gen_aero.js:158-172`), and the forces land on real nodes
(`30_solver.js:688`). **A hydro panel is the same object with a different
force law.** Nothing structural needs inventing.

Per panel: area, position, normal, **deadrise angle**, station along the hull,
and whether it lies **forward or aft of the step**.

Per substep, per panel: `d = waterH(x, z, t) − panelY` — negative is dry and
contributes exactly nothing, which is how the gradient is guaranteed.

Four force terms, all smooth in `d`:

1. **Buoyancy** — `ρ g × (submerged volume attributed to the panel)`. The
   hydrostatic restoring force. Gives correct draft at rest, checkable against
   Archimedes (§5).
2. **Planing lift** — a planing surface's dynamic lift, `∝ ρ V² × wetted area
   × f(trim, deadrise)`. **Savitsky** (§2.3).
3. **Drag** — skin friction (`∝ wetted area × V²`), form drag, and
   **wave-making drag**, which is what rises steeply toward the hump and falls
   away past it.
4. **Added mass / impact** — `∝ d(depth)/dt`, the impulsive force of entering
   water. **This is what makes a hard landing a SLAM rather than a spring**,
   and it is also the term that carries the stability risk (§2.4).

The four regimes then emerge rather than being scripted:
**displacement → hump** (maximum drag, nose high, the hull climbing its own bow
wave) **→ planing** (the hull rises onto the step, wetted area collapses aft of
it, drag falls) **→ flying**.

### 2.3 The literature — well-trodden, and at the right epistemic level

- **Savitsky (1964), "Hydrodynamic Design of Planing Hulls."** THE empirical
  planing model, still used by marine engineers: lift coefficient as a
  function of trim angle, beam, deadrise and speed coefficient. It is a CURVE
  FIT, which is exactly the same epistemic status as this project's aero
  polars — not a pretence of first principles, and honest about it.
- **Von Kármán / Wagner water-entry theory** — added mass for a wedge entering
  water. **A deadrise float bottom IS a wedge**, so the closed forms apply
  directly to term 4.
- **NACA float towing-tank reports (1930s-40s)** — public domain, real
  hump-drag and resistance curves for real float shapes. A **calibration
  anchor** of precisely the kind this project already builds gates around
  (as the Cub's fiche anchors the aero).

### 2.4 THE STABILITY QUESTION — measured, and it is NOT where it was expected

The solver is explicit, so the first concern was that buoyancy — a very strong
spring — would diverge. `genSubsteps` (`62_gen_aero.js:672-690`) states the
measured envelope:

> *"Two limits, and the DAMPING one binds first in practice. Measured on the
> fleet at their own substep counts: worst omega\*dt is 0.50 (Jodel) and worst
> c\*dt is 0.73 (Cub) — both stable. Spruce+ply on the Cub's 24 substeps ran at
> omega\*dt 0.615 and c\*dt 0.947 and **diverged**, which is the whole bug."*

Run the arithmetic (INFERRED, to be confirmed by the spike):

- A 0.5 m² panel gives a hydrostatic stiffness `k = ρ g A ≈ 1000 · 9.81 · 0.5
  ≈ 4.9 kN/m`. On a ~3 kg node, `ω = √(k/m) ≈ 40 rad/s`.
- At 45 substeps and 60 fps, `dt = 1/(60·45) ≈ 3.7e-4 s`, so **`ω·dt ≈ 0.015`**
  — two orders inside the proven 0.50.

**So buoyancy is comfortably stable, and the earlier worry was misplaced.**
This retracts the "biggest technical risk" framing given in conversation.

**The risk is term 4.** Added mass / impact is proportional to `d(depth)/dt` —
it is a **DAMPING term**, and the damping limit is the one this project has
already been bitten by (`c·dt 0.947` diverged). So:

- **The impact coefficient must enter `genSubsteps`' envelope** as a `c`, and
  a float-equipped build must be allowed to raise its substep count the way a
  stiff airframe already does. The mechanism exists; it needs a new
  contributor.
- The spike (§5) reports `ω·dt` and `c·dt` for the water terms as its first
  output, against those same measured bounds. That is the go/no-go.

### 2.5 The water surface must be ONE surface — and it must grow a time argument

`waterH(x, z)` is time-independent today. Waves require `waterH(x, z, t)`, and
**the physics and the renderer must sample the SAME function** or the
aeroplane floats above or sinks into the visible surface. This is
`WORLD-CONTRACT.md` §0 applied to water, and it is a deliberate amendment to
the contract, not a renderer convenience.

It follows that **waves live in the world data, not in the shader** — which
decides the wave model in §3.

**Sea state belongs to THE DAY.** Wave height and direction come from
`setWeather` (`20_world.js:426`), the same object `SKY-ATMOSPHERE-2026-09-11.md`
§1 extends. One more consumer, no second model. And a gift falls out of it:
**glassy calm is HARDER to land on than a ripple** — no depth cues, a genuine
and well-known hazard — so the weather dial produces real airmanship for free.

### 2.6 The autopilot cannot taxi a seaplane — a chantier hiding inside this one

`40_autopilot.js`'s ground steering (`groundSteer`, the TAXI Stanley law) is
built for a tailwheel with brakes. A seaplane weathervanes hard, steers on
water rudder and differential power, and **cannot stop**. Left alone, the AP
will fail comically and it will look like the physics is wrong. Budget it as
part of the water arc, not as a surprise.

### 2.7 What the physics must NOT do

- **No boolean contact**, anywhere, at any depth.
- **No GPU.** Same standing ruling as the solver (`RENDERER-DECISION` (al)):
  the hydro model is headless-gateable JS or the battery cannot judge it.
- **No separate water "mode".** A float is submerged or it is not, by depth —
  the same code path in the air, on the step and at rest.

## 3. WATER VISUALS

### 3.1 The surface

**Gerstner waves, not FFT — and §2.5 is the reason.** Gerstner is a closed-form
analytic function of `(x, z, t)`: the physics can sample it directly and cheaply,
which is the requirement. Tessendorf FFT is prettier on open ocean but lives in
a texture, and sharing it with the solver means a readback and a sync problem.
For an island of bays, inlets and rivers, **a few Gerstner octaves plus a
normal-map detail layer is both the right look and the only one that satisfies
the shared-surface law.** Revisit FFT only if the open sea is ever the subject.

### 3.2 Shading — and the highest-value term is depth

Three terms make water read as water:

1. **Fresnel** — transparent looking down, reflective at grazing angles. The
   single most important term, and cheap.
2. **Reflection** — the sky probe. Note the dependency: `SKY-ATMOSPHERE` **S5
   (the dynamic probe)** improves the water for free, and the water is the
   second surface that already reads `scene.environment`
   (`render_world.js:153`).
3. **Refraction + depth absorption — THE ANSWER TO "gradients with the
   terrain".** Sample the depth buffer, compute the water-column thickness,
   apply Beer-Lambert absorption. Shallow water shows a tinted bottom; deep
   water goes to its own colour. **For an island this is the term that makes a
   shoreline read**, and it is the best value in the whole visual section.

### 3.3 The shoreline

- the depth ramp above;
- a **soft intersection fade** where water meets terrain, to kill the hard
  geometric seam (a depth-difference fade — the same trick the tree fill
  already uses for band dithering);
- **shore foam** where depth is small, and crest foam on the waves;
- optionally a **wet band** on the terrain side of the waterline.

### 3.4 Wake and spray — DRIVEN BY THE PHYSICS, NOT AUTHORED

The same per-panel immersion and velocity that produce drag should emit the
spray. Then everything is correct by construction and scales properly:

- **spray sheets** — the twin fans thrown sideways by a planing float; the
  signature seaplane image, bright, mostly alpha cards;
- **the step's rooster tail**;
- **touchdown splash** — impulsive, scaling with vertical speed, because the
  impact term (§2.2 term 4) already does;
- **the Kelvin wake** — the V astern; either a projected decal following the
  aeroplane or a displacement injected into the wave field;
- **prop spray** at high power in the displacement regime.

### 3.5 Underwater

A cheap full-screen tint plus fog when the camera goes under (a dug-in float, a
crash). Low priority, needed for completeness.

## 4. SEQUENCING — and the split that matters

**Water PHYSICS is version-independent.** Pure solver JS, headless, gateable.
It does not wait for the three.js migration and can proceed in parallel with
anything.

**Water VISUALS are a large new shader system.** By `RENDERER-DECISION` §4b's
own argument, writing a full water shader on r128 and recalibrating it after
the upgrade is exactly the waste to avoid. **The water shader lands after the
migration.**

**One exception, deliberately carved out:** the **cheap shoreline read** —
depth-based colour plus Fresnel on the flat plane that already exists. Small,
enormous value for an island, and cheap enough that redoing it after the
migration costs little. Worth taking early on its own merits.

| | size | depends on |
|---|---|---|
| **H0 — the float-drop spike** (§5) | S | nothing |

## 5. H0 — THE SPIKE, AND IT IS THE GO/NO-GO

**One float, one panel set, dropped onto flat water, headless in node. One day.
No renderer.** It answers the only question that could invalidate the approach:

1. **Does it settle to the right draft?** Checkable against Archimedes from the
   declared displacement — an exact, arguable number, not a look.
2. **Is it stable?** Report `ω·dt` and `c·dt` for the water terms against the
   measured bounds in §2.4 (proven 0.50 / 0.73; diverged at 0.615 / 0.947).
   **This is the go/no-go.**
3. **Does a tow at increasing speed show a HUMP** — drag rising then falling as
   the hull rises onto the step?
4. **Does a descent at 1 m/s touch down with a gradient**, not a step change in
   drag? Report the drag-vs-time trace. This is the KSP failure, measured.

If (2) fails, the answer is more substeps in contact (the mechanism exists) or
a semi-implicit treatment of term 4 — decided with a number in hand rather than
by argument.
