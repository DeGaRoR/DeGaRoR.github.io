# FOG & MIST — fog saves a frame by licensing the renderer to draw less, not by being cheaper to shade (study, 2026-09-21)

The user: *"flydiy fog and mist. Do a study on that. Do we have any? Looking at 2 goals;
performance optimization and good lookingness. Hope we can do both, and that the feature can
save us on performance rather than costing us, but anyways we need a good one."*

Ruling taken while scoping: **study + measurements only** — nothing lands in `src/`; the
sessions in §5 build it. **The headline, measured (§1d): the fog we already have costs less
than this rig can see, and the distance it licenses us to stop drawing is worth −45 % of the
frame at the stand.** Measured on this tree (G471, `cbb28fef`) unless marked INFERRED. The
rig is `tools/frame_perf.js` (the GPU timer per pass) with a `--pre` helper that resets every
probe to the base state before applying its own, and every quoted delta is an **A/B/A** (the
probe between two baselines, scored against their mean - see SS1a for why nothing else is a
number). Rows in `tools/perf/frame_perf_fog{,_aba,_aba2}.json`, runners in `tools/perf/`.

## 0. THE STATE — yes, we have fog, three layers of it, all in ONE place

the head of `tonemapping_fragment` (`src/viewer/atmo.js:480-487`), in LINEAR radiance, on

**Owed on the record before this study:** the mist's terrain term (HANDOVER G419: *"mist in
the valleys and over the water by the hydrology's own map rather than one world altitude"*),
underwater fog (WATER-SHADER §4 item "a tint and a fog when the eye goes under"), the shed's
room haze (POST-FX §3), the eye pass (SKY F).

**What the state means for the two goals.** Looks: the machinery is physical and in the
right place — but the mist is a flat, static slab that ignores the ground it lies on, and it is
the ground (valleys, lakes, the sea, the muskeg) that makes Southeast-Alaska mist read as
mist. Performance: fog is applied to every fragment the renderer draws, and the renderer
draws exactly as much on a 1.2 km morning as on a 60 km afternoon — the far plane is 100 km
(`app.js:225`), the 16 far-terrain quadrant meshes (POST-FX §1a: 12 M tris in the scene,
14.5 M in frustum at the stand) are never distance-culled, the forest streams to
`R_ACT = 9100` m (`render_world.js:96, 3090`), the cloud march runs to `maxKm 60`, the far
shadow cascade and the cover mask bake 2048² each. **Nothing reads the visibility.** That is
the whole performance opportunity, and it is the opposite of a cost.

## 1. THE COST TABLE, MEASURED

**Measured 2026-09-22**, RTX 3080 at 1920x1080 through ANGLE/D3D11, headless Chrome, Jolene,
GRAPHICS `medium` (aa msaa, clouds half, shadows full, compositing linear), tier `full`, 120
frames a row, the streamer settled. Rigs and rows: `tools/perf/frame_perf_fog.json` (the
27-row census), `frame_perf_fog_aba.json` and `frame_perf_fog_aba2.json` (the A/B/A passes),
runners in `tools/perf/run_fog_*.js`.

### 1a. THE THREE THINGS THE INSTRUMENT TAUGHT US FIRST

**(1) A cross-probe delta taken minutes apart is not a number.** In the 27-row run the SAME
workload (5453 calls, 17.1 Mtris, bit-identical scene) read 62.1 ms at the start and 70.5 ms
twenty minutes later, and two near-identical culled configurations read 33.5 and 42.9 ms. The
card boosts to 2100 MHz cold and settles to ~1935 under the SW power cap (`nvidia-smi`:
throttle 0x4, 296 of 340 W) while the rig's own Chrome runs flat out with vsync off. Every
number below therefore comes from an **A/B/A**: each probe sits between two `base` rows and is
scored against their mean. `frame_perf.js` has no interleave of its own - a `--probes` list is
measured in order - so the A/B/A is spelled out in the runner.

**(2) The rig's `--quiet` gate can lie, and a peer can land mid-row.** `--quiet` exits its wait
when `nvidia-smi` FAILS (utilisation reads -1) exactly as if the card were idle; check
`gpuUtilBefore` on every row. And `gpuUtilBefore` includes the rig's own Chrome, so 80-98 % is
NORMAL and is not contention - the reading that matters is the one taken before Chrome spawns.
Mid-pass-1 a peer session started `tools/light_shot.js` on another port: the base row jumped
from 63.5 to 95.8 ms on an identical scene and came back to 59.3 when it finished. Rows taken
under it are void; the A/B/A is what makes that visible at all.

**(3) The rig CANNOT time the cloud march, and reports no error.** `clouds.js:555` opens its
own `TIME_ELAPSED_EXT` query around the march; `frame_perf.js:107` opens one around every
`renderer.render`. GL queries cannot nest, so the rig's query is invalid and the `clouds:march`
tag is ABSENT from all 27 rows - which reads exactly like a pass that never ran. It runs:
`CLOUDS.stats.gpuMs` = **0.67 ms** at cover 0.2. Read the clouds' own timer, not the rig's.
(A fix for a later session: the rig should skip its wrapper while `CLOUDS.timer.on`.)

**The noise floor that follows from all this is +-10-15 %** (~+-8 ms at a 60 ms stand). Nothing
smaller than that is resolvable with this rig on this box.

**The two culls are SUPER-ADDITIVE and must land together.** -15.2 ms and -4.2 ms separately,

cannot. Shipping the cheap-looking one alone buys a third of the win.

The 12 big meshes at the stand (the census's own count): 524 288 tris is the +-4.5 km inner
ring (always in view, never culled by this test - the eye is inside it), the other eleven are
the island's far quadtree quadrants, 184 320 to 2 170 880 tris each, 12.4 M together.

## 2. WHAT FOG CAN SAVE — the visibility contract

### 2a. One number, derived from what the shader draws

The renderer may only stop drawing where the player cannot see, and "cannot see" must be the
SHADER's opinion, not an authored one — or the cut shows as a wall the moment the two disagree.
So the number is derived, per frame, on the CPU, from the same two terms the splice applies:

```
T(D) = T_ap(D) × exp(−mistOD(eyeY, dir, D))          // the atlas's α at D × the mist's closed form
visM = the D where T(D) < 0.01 along the WORST ray (level, toward the brightest horizon)
```

**`visM` is a property of the day AND THE EYE, never of the day alone** (§1f: the same 98 %
morning gives 2.1 km at 150 m and no limit at all at 600 m, because the layer's top is 60 m).
An eye above the layer is limited only by the aerial perspective, and the contract must then
keep the far plane long - a cut sized from the day's humidity alone puts a wall in front of a
climbing aeroplane. The number is recomputed per frame from `cameraPosition.y`.

`T_ap` is the AP atlas's own alpha (the CPU mirror `sunTransmittance` / the atlas readback
already exists for the flare's calibration); `mistOD` is the same function as the GLSL,
evaluated with `dir.y = 0` at the eye's height (a level ray is the longest path in the layer
for an eye inside it; for an eye above the top, the ray that grazes the top). At 1 % residual
transmittance a surface contributes less than the dither (aa_resolve's TPDF is ~1/255); the
cut is invisible by construction, and GATE FOG proves it with a screenshot diff (§5).

What the mist alone licenses today (INFERRED: the AP's own path transmittance at turbidity
2.5 is still ~0.4–0.5 at 60 km, Rayleigh 1.2e-5/m plus the Mie term — the clear atmosphere
never cuts anything; the MIST does), an eye inside the layer, `T < 1 %` at `4.6 / rho0`:

| rh | rho0 (per m) | T < 1 % at | `day.visibilityKm` says | the far plane could be |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ 0.70 | 0 | never (AP only) | 60 km | 100 km (as now) |
| 0.80 | 0.00028 | 16.6 km | 55 km | 21.6 km |
| 0.90 | 0.00111 | 4.1 km | 37 km | 5.4 km |
| 0.95 | 0.00174 | 2.6 km | 24 km | 3.4 km |
| 0.98 | 0.00218 | 2.1 km | 15 km | 2.7 km |
| 1.00 | 0.00250 | 1.8 km | 9 km | 3.0 km (the floor) |

The two formulas disagree by 4–7× today (the F8 readout's "vis" is `3 / rho0` — a 5 %
contrast, 1.2 km saturated; Koschmieder's 2 % is `3.9 / rho0` = 1.6 km; `visibilityKm`'s
`× 0.15` floor gives 9 km for the same air). One number (the next paragraph) ends that.

`day.visibilityKm` stays what it is — the DAY's authored statement, the number METAR would
report — and the study recommends it becomes the input the mist's density is fitted to
(today rho0 and visibilityKm are two separate formulas of rh that happen to agree at the
ends). One formula, `rho0 = 3.912 / visM_day` (Koschmieder: the 2 % contrast threshold), then
`visM` is exactly the METAR number on a mist day and the shader agrees with the readout.

The hysteresis rule matters more than any single row: **a visibility that breathes must
never make the streamer breathe with it.** The forest's regeneration on a chunk crossing was
the G420 pop; the fog must not re-import it.

### 2c. Where the saving is NOT

- **Not in the splice.** §1c bounds the mist and the whole aerial perspective BELOW the rig's
  noise floor (~8 ms at a 60 ms stand) — an upper bound, not a measurement, but low enough to
  decide by. There is nothing to win by moving fog into a
  depth-buffer post pass — and three things to lose: the pass needs `needRT` (at tier `off`
  there is no target, POST-FX §1b), it would fog the cloud composite a second time (the march
  already applies AP + mist at the cloud's own depth), and it breaks RENDERER-DECISION §4k
  rule 3 (ONE splice, `fogNode` under TSL). **Not recommended.**
- **Not in the mist's optical depth.** The closed form is four `exp` per fragment; a
  per-fragment 3-D noise march would cost more than the whole splice today. Spatial variation
  goes in as a 2-D field on a sampler the material already binds (§3).
- **Not in the cloud march, and not in the far shadow cascade.** Both were probed and both are
  already too cheap to matter: the march 0.67 ms whole, the cascade 0.00 ms at 0.3 calls a
  frame (§1a, §1b). They stay as they are; the contract does not touch them.
- **Not in the atmosphere's tables.** The sky-view and the AP atlas are rebuilt every frame for
  **0.05 ms** together. Re-baking them less often would save nothing.

## 3. WHAT FOG CAN LOOK LIKE — ranked by atmosphere per millisecond

### 3a. The terrain term — mist that lies in the valleys and on the water (owed since G419)

Today `yTop` is one world altitude (60 m ASL). Jolene's valley floors run from 0 (the sea,
the muskeg pools) to ~200 m; the ridges to 1000 m. A flat 60 m slab drowns the shore and
misses every inland valley. The term the G419 entry owes:

```
mistTop(x, z) = blur_200m( max(waterH(x, z), valleyFloor(x, z)) ) + MIST.top_rel
```

`world.waterH` (`20_world.js:332, 495` — the hydrology's rivers, the lakes at spill height,
the sea at 0) and `terrainH` exist — and on Jolene they are already rich enough to carry the
picture: the rig's own readout reports **206 lakes, 170 rivers, baked in 428 ms**
(`FLIGHT_PROBE.world().hydro`, `hydro: 'blend'`); `valleyFloor` is the terrain's height opened by a 400 m
minimum filter (the floor a valley shares, not the ridge). Baked ONCE per world on the CPU at
64 m cells (Jolene's square / 64 ≈ 400², a few ms), uploaded into the AP ATLAS's free rows —
the atlas is 2048 × 546 (`AP_ATLAS_H = 34 + 512`): the AP slices use rows 0–31, the cloud
shadow tile rows 34–545 and columns 0–511; **columns 512–2047 of the tile rows are empty**,
room for three 512² maps. The island's ground programs stand at 12/14/15 of 16 samplers
(`render_world.js:1007-1010, 1350-1353`) — a new texture would not link (G424; C2 measured
"the terrain vanished"). The atlas is the one sampler every fogged material already binds;
this is exactly how the cloud shadow tile got in.

In the splice: one more tap, `yTop = texture2D(uApAtlas, uvOf(wp.xz)).r` at the FRAGMENT's
world position (`vAtmoV` through `cameraPosition` gives it; `vWPi` already exists on the
ground programs) and at the eye (a uniform, written by `ATMO.update` from the same CPU map).
The closed form takes ONE top; use the mean of the two — an approximation, honest at the
scale the field varies (200 m blur against a 1.2 km visibility), wrong only where a ray
crosses a ridge into a deeper valley, where the deeper valley's mist would be a little thin.
State it in the code and in the gate. No new chunk, no new uniform lane beyond `uMist[2].w`
(free) for the eye's top.

What it buys: the shore mist ends at the beach, the muskeg pools smoke, the inland valleys
fill while the ridges stand out — the G419 "valley morning" everywhere instead of at one
altitude. Cost: one texture tap per fragment on a sampler already bound: INFERRED
< 0.05 ms at the stand (the cloud tile's own tap measured nothing, C2).

### 3b. PATCHES — mist that drops into the valleys and hangs on the trees

The user, 2026-09-22: *"can we have patches of mist? Like that mist that seems like it drops in
valleys and mountains, stays floating like hung on the trees?"* That is the Southeast-Alaska
picture and it is THREE behaviours, one slot each in the closed form (§3a gives the first):

| what the eye sees | the term | the field |
|---|---|---|
| it lies in the hollows, the ridges stand clear | `yTop` per location (§3a) | `blur_200m(max(waterH, valleyFloor))` |
| patches, not a blanket - thick here, clear 200 m on, sliding downwind | `rho0 × n(xz − wind·t)` | the cloud field's own 2-D noise, drifted as the veil already is |
| snagged on the timber, banked against a slope | `H` per location + an upslope bias | wetness (muskeg / forest floor / north aspect) + terrain gradient·wind |

**THE CATCH, AND IT DECIDES THE COST.** `mistOD` is a closed form because the layer is assumed
HORIZONTALLY UNIFORM along the ray - only `y` varies. The moment `yTop` and `rho0` vary with
`xz`, that is no longer true, and a patch standing between the eye and a far ridge, sampled by
neither endpoint, is simply missed. Two answers:

- **TWO-POINT** (the field at the fragment and at the eye, averaged): ONE extra tap on a sampler
  already bound, the closed form untouched. Honest for a field that varies slowly against the
  distance it is seen over - the valley pooling of §3a. **Visibly wrong for small patches**: the
  patch either covers both endpoints or does not exist.
- **A SHORT MARCH**: 4-8 samples of `(rho0, yTop)` along the ray, each contributing its own
  closed-form segment. Patches become real - you fly INTO one and out the other side, a strip
  goes in and out, a bank stands against a hillside. Costs 4-8 taps per fogged fragment.

**TAKE THE MARCH.** §1c bounds the whole splice BELOW the rig's noise floor, §1b shows the frame
is 97 % scene pass, and §1d hands back 45 % of it. A few taps on the one thing the player looks
at all the time is the best trade in this study: the contract PAYS for the mist. Guard it as a
GRAPHICS row (`mist: off / flat / relief / patches`) so the Low preset keeps the closed form, and
measure it with the A/B/A of §1d - the march is the first fog term this study expects to be able
to SEE in a frame time, and it must be checked rather than assumed.

**CUTTING THROUGH IT** (the user, seeing §1f's homogeneous layers: *"can we cut through it"*).
Today you CANNOT, and the reason is structural: the layer is horizontally infinite and uniform,
so it has no edge to cross. You are above it or inside it, and the only way to change that is to
climb or descend - the mist is always "over there", never a thing you fly INTO. That is the whole
difference between a wash and weather.

The field of the table above gives it edges, and the entry is already built:

- **The boundary.** With `rho0` varying over `xz`, a bank has a near face. Flying at it, the
  optical depth to the near field climbs over a second or two, the ground goes, and you come out
  the far side into clear air. A strip that goes in and out, on one run.
- **The white-out is C4's, not new.** `uMist[3]` already carries a slab the eye is IN and adds it
  to the optical depth (`clouds.js:706-720` writes it from the cloud deck). A fog BANK is the same
  machinery driven by the 2-D field instead of the deck: `rhoC` = the field at the eye,
  `base`/`top` = `mistTop` and the ground under it. Entering a bank and entering cloud are one
  code path, which is why this is cheap.
- **The march is what makes the face honest.** Two-point sampling (§3b) cannot see a bank the eye
  is not yet in - the face would pop as the eye crossed it. With 4-8 samples the face is where it
  is, and it arrives.
- **NOT on the table: the aeroplane carving it.** Prop wash tearing a hole, a wingtip vortex
  drawing a thread through the layer - that is an ADVECTED density (a simulated field written back
  each frame), a different project an order of magnitude dearer than everything above. Named here
  so it is not mistaken for a small extra.

Two notes for whoever builds it: the march must sample the SAME field the CPU uses for `visM`
(§2a) or the contract will cut where the shader still draws mist; and `ATMO.GLSL.MIST` is shared
with the cloud march (`clouds.js:273-277`), so the patches reach the clouds' own compose for free.

### 3c. Burn-off — the mist obeys the clock

Radiation fog forms under a clear night and burns off as the sun heats the ground. Today
`rho0` is `rh` alone: a 98 % morning stays 98 % at noon unless the player moves the slider.
CPU-side, zero GPU cost, in `07_day.js` where `visibilityKm` already lives:

```
rhoDay × max(0, 1 − burn)     burn = clamp((sunEl − 5°) / 20°, 0, 1) × clearness × hoursSinceSunrise / 3
```

with `clearness = 1 − cloudCover` (an overcast holds the fog). The day's `version` bumps
only on `set()` — the clock's tick must write through `setDay` or the mist freezes (trap
§4.1). What it buys: a dawn departure into a valley fog that is gone by ten, the way the
day already gives a sunrise. GATE DAY grows the monotone-burn-off assertion beside its
monotone-visibility one.

### 3d. The room haze — the shed's last colour wall retired (POST-FX §3)

`Fog(0x1a1712, 40, 120)` is a smoothstep to near-black in display space; the aeroplane on
the shed floor reads darker than the same aeroplane on the apron for no physical reason (the
L1 session's "one light contract"). Replace with the mist's own machinery at a room density
(`uMist[3]`'s slab shape: rho 0.004/m between the floor and the roof, the lamps' colour):
`setAP(false)` stays for the AP (no sky in the shed) and `mistApply` runs — one flag more,
`uMist[0].w` already gates it. Deletes `AP_FOG_FRAG`'s only user; the legacy branch can go,
and GATE ATMO's "both paths gated on one flag" becomes "one path".

### 3e. Underwater — the eye below the sea

WATER-SHADER §4: when `eye.y < sea.h` the whole frame is a slab of water — an absorption
`exp(−σ × D)` with σ the sea's `(0.45, 0.08, 0.04)/m` and the in-scatter the water's own
colour. It is `uMist[3]` with a colour: the in-cloud slab, upside down. One CPU branch in
`ATMO.update`, no GLSL beyond letting the slab carry its own colour (a lane in `uMist[2].w`
or a fifth row). Cost nothing; buys the float-drop and the capsize an honest picture.

### 3f. Not now

- **Volumetric light shafts through the mist** — the POST-FX `rays` pass (0.65 ms) already
  does the screen-space version and is gated on the flare's occlusion; a real march is the
  clouds' cost again for a rarer picture.
- **A 3-D density field** (froxels, UE's volumetric fog) — not at this frame budget in
  WebGL2; the 2-D field + the height law is what UE ships as the default "exponential height
  fog" and is what the reference photographs of the Inside Passage show.

## 4. TRAPS KNOWN BEFORE THE FIRST LINE

1. **`dayApply` returns early under `rigCur.manual`** (`render_world.js:4348-4349`): the mist
   uniforms and the AP atlas freeze while F8's sun rows are live. The visibility contract
   must read `ATMO.MIST` after the update, not before, and must not run when the update did not.
2. **`ShaderLib.*.uniforms` are CLONED at module load** (SKY-CHANTIER §1 fact 3b): scalars
   reach every program only through the shared `Float32Array` (`mistScalars`), samplers only
   through `inject()`. A new lane is a new index in `mistScalars`, never a new uniform.
3. **The sampler ceiling** (G424, C2): 16 units, the premises patch at 15. Every new map rides
   the AP atlas's free rows. `apSample()` reads rows 0–31 only; a map in the tile rows is read
   by its own `uv` — keep the two-row gap.
4. **GATE ATMO's chunk-count rule**: exactly five `SC.*` writes in `install()`, the splice at
   the HEAD of `tonemapping_fragment`. New GLSL goes INSIDE `MIST_GLSL` (which the clouds' pass
   concatenates too — `ATMO.GLSL.MIST` — so the march gets the terrain term for free).
5. **The 7 ms trap**: any extra draw into the MSAA target after `render()` resolves it twice
   (`aa_resolve.js:395-398`). Nothing here draws; the contract only hides.
6. **The streamer's pop** (G420): a ring radius that moves with the mist re-imports the
   chunk-crossing regeneration unless the two-radii hysteresis of §2b is kept.
7. **Culling by the far plane culls the dome too** if the dome's sphere is smaller than 1.3 ×
   visM — it is `frustumCulled = false` (`render_world.js:258`); keep it so.
8. **The A/B is not a cross-checkout** (G471's warning, HANDOVER 54065-54073): every number
   in §1 is the same boot, the same place, the probe applied and the streamer re-settled.
9. **Landing**: from an LF worktree, `-c core.autocrlf=false`, the G number taken at landing
   (the fork-collision and CRLF traps of this week).

## 5b. THE CLIMATE CHANTIER OWNS THE PRODUCER (agreed 2026-09-22)

The climate session (`claude/flydiy-climate-system-baa60c`, K0-K3 landed: one wind field in
`src/core/09_climate.js`, a layered atmosphere, a front on the clock, `atmosWater`) reached the
same Koschmieder law independently and asked who writes it. **Agreed: (a) - the climate
PUBLISHES, F1 CONSUMES.** `world.climate.haze()` hands over `visibilityKm` (the day's, with a
front's murk folded in - measured 60 -> 26 km through one passage), `rho0 = 3.912/(visKm x 1000)`,
`top`, `H`, `rhSfc` and the LCL; `atmo.js`'s `MIST.rhoDay` line is NOT touched by K4. F1 wires
the read.

Three consequences for F1, from this study's measurements:

1. **Take the INGREDIENTS, never a single distance.** `rho0`/`top`/`H` let the consumer integrate
   along the EYE's ray, which §1f proves is mandatory: a distance derived from surface visibility
   alone is a wall on the skyline at rh 0.90.
2. **Visibility is now LIVE** - it moves with the front and the diurnal swing during a single
   flight. §2b's two-radii hysteresis stops being a nicety and becomes the thing that keeps the
   forest streamer from thrashing.
3. **`dayApply` returns early under `rigCur.manual`** (§4.1). Harmless while the mist is a
   constant per day; once it follows a front, the weather stops whenever an F8 sun row is touched.

And K2's humidity COLUMN (`rh(h)`, `Td(h)`, the LCL) is the vertical law F2's terrain mist should
sample rather than inventing one: the column gives density with height, §3a's field gives where
the top sits. Column x field is the picture this study was asked for.
