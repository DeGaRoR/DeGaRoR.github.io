Conclusions and next steps

  • The light, faithfully built aeroplanes reproduce their parents closely on all three axes: the Cub, the Jodel and the Chinook sit within a few percent on stall, cruise, top speed, climb and take-off roll, at the weight their book was written for.
  • The ref-matched recreations (Stearman, Tiger Moth, Camel, DC-3) reproduce their parents' shape to within 0-5 % on span and length. Their weak side is the mass model on old construction (the Camel +37 % empty, the Tiger Moth +13 %, the DC-3 -15 to -22 %) and a 10-25 % climb deficit, with low static margins on the biplanes (1-8 %).
  • The corrected 172 is right on shape (98) and specs (94); its performance (76) loses on the cruise (-11 %), the climb (-10 %), the climb-out to 50 ft (+48 %) and a landing roll the handbook schedule makes 18 % SHORTER than the POH (the brakes or the tyre friction too strong). See reports/CESSNA-DOSSIER-2026-11-08.md.
  • The model's own residuals: climb 10-20 % short on matched engines, the climb-out to 50 ft long, the landing roll the pilot's.

Next steps

  • The landing brake law (the pilot's, not PHYSICS_V): brakes from touchdown with a firmer ramp, the approach re-planned for the load.

How the scores are computed

Every reference figure carries an uncertainty, by what it is (σ, a fraction of the figure): span ±3 %, wing area ±6 %, length ±4 %, root chord ±6 %, tip chord ±8 %, stab span ±5 %, stab area ±10 %, fin area ±10 %, track ±6 %, height ±6 %, power / engine ±3 %, prop diameter ±4 %, empty weight ±6 %, max weight ±5 %, fuel ±10 %, stall (clean) ±6 %, cruise 75 % ±6 %, top speed ±6 %, rate of climb ±12 %, climb (2nd weight) ±12 %, best glide ±10 %, take-off roll ±25 %, take-off to 50 ft ±25 %, landing roll ±25 %, landing from 50 ft ±25 %, water run ±25 %, water run (2nd weight) ±25 %, water time ±25 %, water to 50 ft ±25 %. A reference row's own tolerance wins when it is wider. A span is a span; a climb moves with the weight, the air, the engine and the technique; a landing roll with the surface, the wind, the pilot and the brakes.

An item is scored on containment. Our reading at the reference's weights is a range (a span when the reference does not say its weight); it should hold the figure. The figure's distance outside our range, in σ, gives the score 100 × exp(−z² / 2): held 100, one σ out 61, two σ out 14. Held, a light centring term costs up to 15 points when the figure sits at the very end of our range.

Confidence is a separate grade, never mixed into the score: each performance figure counts by its source (a handbook chart 1, a type sheet or the maker 0.9, a review 0.6, an encyclopedia 0.5, a ruling 0.5, single-source or THIN 0.3), geometry and specs a tenth each; the sum E gives 100 × (1 − e^(−E/4)): thin under 45, fair to 70, solid above. A plane matched on two thin figures reads "99 · thin".

The axes and the overall: geometry, specs and performance are the weighted means of their items (geometry 30 %, specs 30 %, performance 40 % of the overall). One reading per performance indicator: the airframe's (no pilot), else the handbook-flown; the shipped pilot's gap is shown apart.
