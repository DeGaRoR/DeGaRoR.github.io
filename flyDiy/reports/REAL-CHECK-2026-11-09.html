Real Check: the planes against their real parents

13 planes, each measured at three loads (one light pilot, solo with half fuel, the type's gross) and compared with its real parent's published figures, each read at the conditions it was stated at. Scored on three axes: geometry (do we reproduce the shape), specs (did we pick the right engine, propeller and weights), performance (does it fly like the real one).

Conclusions and next steps

  • The light, faithfully built aeroplanes reproduce their parents closely on all three axes: the Cub, the Jodel and the Chinook sit within a few percent on stall, cruise, top speed, climb and take-off roll, at the weight their book was written for.
  • The ref-matched recreations (Stearman, Tiger Moth, Camel, DC-3) reproduce their parents' shape to within 0-5 % on span and length. Their weak side is the mass model on old construction (the Camel +37 % empty, the Tiger Moth +13 %, the DC-3 -15 to -22 %) and a 10-25 % climb deficit, with low static margins on the biplanes (1-8 %).
  • The corrected 172 is right on shape (98) and specs (94); its performance (76) loses on the cruise (-11 %), the climb (-10 %), the climb-out to 50 ft (+48 %) and a landing roll the handbook schedule makes 18 % SHORTER than the POH (the brakes or the tyre friction too strong). See reports/CESSNA-DOSSIER-2026-11-08.md.
  • The model's own residuals: climb 10-20 % short on matched engines, the climb-out to 50 ft long, the landing roll the pilot's.

Next steps

  • The Cessna (this week): FLAGSHIP's final corrected 172 and its Wipline 2350 version, measured with this tool against the 172R POH and Wipaire; the dossier lists every number and the corrections the load door would otherwise undo (edit the cage's tail rows, not spec.tail).
  • The landing brake law (the pilot's, not PHYSICS_V): brakes from touchdown with a firmer ramp, the approach re-planned for the load.
  • The propeller's climb-speed efficiency (physics, PHYSICS_V): the 10-20 % climb deficit and the long climb-out.
  • The pilot's climb speed from the measured Vy instead of 1.38 Vs, and a Vx obstacle climb-out (the handbook's short-field technique; the sweep has none yet).
  • The mass model on old construction: the Camel bills 37 % over its empty weight, the DC-3 15-22 % under.
  • Redo the Petrel on water once claude/seaplanes-merge flies it.
  • GATE REALBAND on the official planes: the airframe bands and the approach-trim row (REAL-CHECK-2026-11-07 §8).

How the scores are computed

An item scores 100 × max(0, 1 − gap / 0.3). The gap is 0 when ours sits inside the reference band (the figure ± its tolerance), otherwise the relative distance to the band's nearest edge: 10 % outside scores 67, 30 % outside scores 0.

Geometry: the weighted mean over the key dimensions the reference states. Weights: span 2, wing area 2, length 1.5, root chord 1, tip chord 0.5, stab span 1, stab area 1, fin area 1, track 0.5, height 0.5. Tolerances: span and area 3 %, chords and tail 5 %, length 3 %, height 8 %. Span, length (spinner to rudder) and height (standing on the gear at its settled deck angle, the blades left out) are measured on the DRAWN aeroplane, the editor's own scene through the load door; the wing area is the projected planform the sim flies; chords and tail areas are the built spec's.

Specs: the engine family (named right or not), power per engine (3 %), the engine count, the propeller diameter (5 %) and blades, the empty (3 %) and maximum (5 %) weights, fuel (10 %), seats. Weights: engine 1.5, power / engine 2, engines 1, prop diameter 1, blades 0.5, empty weight 2, max weight 1, fuel 0.5, seats 0.5.

Performance: real_check's verdict bands (our MIN–MTOW band at the reference's weights against the reference's condition band), one reading per indicator - the airframe's (probes, no pilot) where it exists, else the handbook-flown one. That is the aeroplane's capability; the shipped pilot's extra cost is shown apart as the pilot's share. Weights: stall (clean) 1, cruise 75 % 1, top speed 1, rate of climb 1.5, climb (2nd weight) 1, best glide 0, take-off roll 1, take-off to 50 ft 0.5, landing roll 0.75, landing from 50 ft 0.5, water run 1, water run (2nd weight) 0.75, water time 0.5, water to 50 ft 0.5. The best glide is shown but weighs 0: our probe glides with the propeller removed, a handbook glide has it windmilling (worth 10-20 % of L/D), so the two are not like for like.

Overall = 0.3 × geometry + 0.3 × specs + 0.4 × performance, over the axes a plane has (the rest renormalised). Bands: ● ≥ 80 close, ▲ 60–80 fair, ■ under 60 far.

A reference marked THIN in tools/real_refs.js (one source, or sources that disagree) carries a wider tolerance or a range: a thin figure can neither condemn nor clear a plane by much.
