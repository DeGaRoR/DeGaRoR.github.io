  The load is right and the force is right (the root bending moment
  checks out by hand), but the ALLOWABLE is coarse: `A = lin/rho` gives
  ONE area for the whole wing class, and the worst member here is not a
  spar cap — it is the 3.4 m LIFT STRUT, which a real aeroplane sizes
  separately. The wood row is the loud case because it pairs a big
  section (12.9 cm2) with a small allowable (39 MPa crushing), i.e. a
  WOODEN lift strut, which nobody builds: wooden aeroplanes use steel
  struts, and the Jodel is a cantilever precisely to avoid the joint.
  Gating this would be gating the proxy, not the aeroplane. What it is
  really saying is that strut sizing wants its own area, and that is a
  materials-model change, not a load-test result.
