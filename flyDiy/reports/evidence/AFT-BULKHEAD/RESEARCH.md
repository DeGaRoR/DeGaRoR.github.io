# AFT BULKHEAD: what is really on the wall behind the last seat (AFT-BULKHEAD-LOOK, 3 Nov 2026, cloud, for CABIN-CRAFT via A0)

Part 1 of the brief. These are facts for CABIN-CRAFT, which will turn them into 3-4 garage rows. Everything is in our
own words: no copied text or pictures, and at most one short quote per source.

## 2. Per family, the details

**Cessna 172 / 182.**
- **Limits [S]:** the 1977 172N POH gives baggage area 1 (station 82-108) 120 lb, and area 2 (108-142) 50 lb. The
  two together are capped at 120 lb (C172N POH, 172R manual).
- **The net [S]:** a standard nylon net with tie-down straps on six eyebolts. Two of them are on the floor at the
  sidewalls, just forward of the baggage door. The limits are on a **placard on the baggage door**.
- **Area 2 [S]:** a training site puts area 2 behind the aft cabin bulkhead. It is reached through the lockable LH
  door or from the cabin (simtuts).
- **The hat shelf [S]:** a Cessna 170 owner found that an interior kit was about 1.5 in too tall for a hat shelf set
  lower than standard. Another owner closed the bulkhead behind the shelf to stop draughts (cessna170 forum). On a
  Cessna 120/140 the shelf is "very suspect" above about 10-15 lb (cessna120140.com).
- **Lighting [S]:** a **rear dome light** in the headliner lights the rear cabin. It shares a push-button with the
  under-wing courtesy lights (172S POH, simtuts).
- **The ELT [S]:** it sits in the tailcone just aft of the baggage bulkhead, behind a cover, with its panel facing
  forward (cessna170 forum, 172P manual). **It is not visible from the cabin.**
- **The rear window [S]:** the "Omni-Vision" rear window (1963) is above the hat shelf (flyingmag).
- **Looks [M]:** one moulded plastic panel in the trim colour, with a curved top following the rear window, a carpeted
  step, and a dark net hanging loose or hooked up.
- **The game's C172:** the game's own C172 already has the rear window (its `drawnPane`, aft of the panel, see
  MEASURE.md). What it lacks is the shelf and the wall below it.

**Cub / Champ / Chief / Taylorcraft.**
- **Limits [S]:** the J-3 type data sheet gives 20 lb at +49 in, with the rear seat at +36 in (seaplane: 4 lb). The
  PA-18 takes 50 lb at +57 in.
- **The sling [S]:** sold in off-white or olive drab (Aircraft Spruce). A Taylorcraft forum post describes the
  build: a U of tube supports it, the bottom of the U is the rear support, the two arms are the side supports, and
  the bag is fastened to the back of the seat sling.
- **Why the limit is low [S]:** a Cessna 120/140 thread says it is partly the fabric back wall, which keeps the cargo
  off the control cables.
- **Aeronca [S]:** no 7AC baggage limit was found. The type club points to drawing 7-450 for the frame.
- **Looks [M]:** through the side window you see a tube frame. The sling is a sagging canvas box about 50-60 cm wide,
  hung below the longerons, with the rear diagonals forming triangles behind it.

**Jodel / Robin (wood).**
- **The structure [S]:** the D11 is spruce and ply with fabric covering, a box fuselage and a curved spruce top
  decking. The D112 plans give a baggage floor of 1.05 x 0.45 m.
- **Limits [S]:** a DR400 owner reports a small plaque limiting the baggage to 40 kg, and a 1988 DR400/100 listing
  gives a 40 kg rear shelf. The DR401 is listed at 60 kg on the 180 hp version.
- **Upholstery [S]:** grey leather appears in sale listings.
- **Looks [M]:** under the DR400's canopy you look down onto a flat carpeted or ply shelf at about seat-back height,
  with a plain painted ply panel or the curved decking above it.

**Bush types.**
- **Extended baggage [S]:** STC kits for the PA-18 add almost 3 ft aft (LAS). Owners report 170-180 lb with
  third-seat or extended kits (backcountrypilot; anecdotal).
- **Husky [S]:** the rear seat comes out on quick-release pins. A listing gives 50 lb in the baggage compartment
  plus 30 lb in an aft stowage compartment.
- **Maule [S]:** clamshell cargo doors open the right side, about 42 cu ft (M-7 / M-9), and the rear seats fold up.
- **Kitfox and Savage Cub [S]:** the Kitfox FAQ gives about 150 lb. The Savage Cub's baggage is a fabric or leather
  bag strapped to the frame.
- **The ELT [S]:** often bolted to a bracket on the tubes or on the bulkhead just aft of the baggage.
- **Looks [M]:** a dark floor and wall, a bright net or orange / red ratchet straps, and a dark tunnel leading aft.

**Modern composite / LSA.**
- **Cirrus SR22 [S]:**
  - the compartment is about 39 x 40 x 36 in (H x W x L), with a 130 lb limit and a door on the LH side aft of the
    wing;
  - straps hook onto loop fittings in the floor and the rear bulkhead, and a retractable net is fitted;
  - the CAPS box is just aft of the baggage bulkhead, reached on later models through an access panel in it;
  - the outside rocket warning is small black text that the UK AAIB calls "barely discernible" (avweb).
  - [M]: the red CAPS T-handle is in the ceiling above the front seats, not on the aft wall.
- **Diamond [S]:**
  - DA40: a standard compartment, a "baggage tube" behind a cloth cover, or an extended compartment. Nothing may be
    carried without the net (DA40 AFM). An EASA AD covers defective DA40 nets.
  - DA20: a shelf behind the seats above the fuel tank, 20 kg, net required.
- **Other types [S]:**
  - Tecnam P2008: 20 kg in about 225 L behind the seats, with a net as standard;
  - Bristell: 15 kg behind the seats, plus two 20 kg wing lockers;
  - Pipistrel Virus SW: baggage behind the seats, with its own LH door; the rescue rocket's cover carries a warning
    label (ATSB);
  - Icon A5: 60 lb behind the seats, enclosed by floor and sidewall panels.
- **Lamps on the wall [S]:** homebuilders mount LED lights on the baggage bulkhead (an RV-9A, an F1 Rocket), and the
  Australian regulator (CASA) warns that they must be shielded from the baggage.
- **Looks [M]:** a smooth light-grey or cream moulded tub, radiused corners bevelling into the sidewalls, a black net
  across it, and the outline of an access panel.

**Ultralight / open frame.**
- **The structure [S]:** the Quicksilver began as a hang glider with a seat, wheels and a small engine. It has a
  central cockpit cage, a tail boom of one or two tubes, and the engine behind the seat. Some microlights build the
  tank into the seat.
- **Looks [M]:** no wall at all. Behind the seat you see the engine and the prop, tube triangles and wires against
  the sky.

## 3. The features that would read best FROM OUTSIDE, THROUGH A SIDE WINDOW, at 2-4 m (ranked)

The ranking uses the game's own facts from MEASURE.md and the stills, not only the real aeroplanes:

- **The face is seen only from forward of the beam.** On the four closed cabins, the aft passenger pillar hides it
  from the side. From 15-45° forward of the beam, 10-31 % of it shows through the rear side glass, and almost all of
  that is the band **above the rear seat backs**.
- **It renders near-black.** The bulkhead is an "inside" section (`tools/_cage_ui.js:825`, role `struct` at
  `src/viewer/aeroskin.js:444`, `:470`). On such a section the cabin shader multiplies every light by
  1 - uCabin.x, the cabin lamps included (`src/viewer/aeroskin.js:3805-3825`). uCabin.x is 0.81 x coverage
  (`:3867`, `:3897`), so 19 % is kept at full coverage. The runtime value in the garage was not read; the stills show
  the panel near-black. Only emissive light is spared.

So what reads is high on the wall, has a horizontal edge or its own light, and is bright against dark.

1. **A lamp in the wall: a dome or an LED strip along the top edge.** [S presence, M sizes: a dome lens 10-15 cm, a
   strip 1-2 cm x 60-100 cm]
   - It is the only thing that gives light. It sells dusk and night, and the Cessna rear dome light and homebuilt
     baggage LEDs are real.
   - In the game an emissive lens is spared by the cabin darkening, so it is also the one thing on this wall
     guaranteed not to go black.
   - The top edge is exactly the band the side glass shows.
2. **A stepped hat shelf at about seat-back height.** [S Cessna, M sizes: depth 30-50 cm, step 15-25 cm, full width]
   - A horizontal lip with a shadow under it reads at any resolution.
   - Its top face catches the roof flood.
   - It sits in the visible band, just above the seat backs.
   - It is THE Cessna look.
3. **A cargo net in a contrasting colour across the upper wall.** [S presence (Cessna, Cirrus, Diamond, Tecnam), M:
   2.5 cm webbing, 10-15 cm cells, about 90 x 50 cm, sagging]
   - A grid is the one texture that survives 3 m.
   - Light webbing on the game's dark wall gives the contrast for free.
4. **A bevel into the sidewalls and the roof (the moulded tub).** [S Cirrus box, M bevel 5-10 cm]
   - A chamfer turns a flat card into a volume.
   - The bevel faces catch the lamps at a different angle from the face.
   - From the forward three-quarter views (the only ones that see it) a side bevel is the first thing in sight.
5. **A two-tone wall: a light upper panel over a darker lower part (carpet / kick strip).** [M]
   - It moves the light area to where the eye is.
   - A light cream / grey upper panel (Cirrus, Cessna 172S trim) would read as a wall at all, which today's does not.
6. **An opening into a dark tail cone or baggage tube.** [S DA40 tube, Cub / Husky extended baggage; M 30-45 x 25-40
   cm]
   - It gives depth.
   - It needs a lit wall around it: on today's near-black panel a black hole has no contrast. Rank it after 5.
7. **The tube frame and a canvas sling, instead of a wall (the Cub family).** [S sling and colours, M: tubes 19-25 mm,
   sling 50-60 x 30-40 cm]
   - A real silhouette: triangles of tube, and a sagging off-white or olive-drab trapezoid.
   - It is what a Cub really has.
   - In the game it fits builds with `cons` tube where the side glass shows the band above the seat.
8. **Coloured ratchet straps and D-rings.** [M: straps 2.5-5 cm, D-rings 4-5 cm]
   - Thin bright diagonals (orange, red, yellow) on a dark wall: cheap, and they read.
9. **An access-panel outline with a placard (CAPS / ELT access).** [S presence, M: 30-40 cm panel, 8 x 5 cm label]
   - Readable only up close (`inClose`), and the real ones are "barely discernible" by design.
   - A detail for the inside view, not the outside.

**Not worth drawing on this wall:**
- **The ELT:** in Cessnas and most metal types it is hidden in the tailcone [S].
- **A fire extinguisher:** it is normally mounted by the front seats or on the floor [M].
- **The red CAPS handle:** it is overhead and forward [M].
