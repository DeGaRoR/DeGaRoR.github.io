Real Check: the planes against their real parents

14 planes, each measured at three loads (one light pilot, solo with half fuel, the type's gross) and compared with its real parent's published figures, each read at the conditions it was stated at. Scored on three axes: geometry (do we reproduce the shape), specs (did we pick the right engine, propeller and weights), performance (does it fly like the real one).

Conclusions and next steps

  • The light, faithfully built aeroplanes reproduce their parents closely on all three axes: the Cub, the Jodel and the corrected 172 sit within a few percent on stall, cruise, top speed, climb and take-off roll, at the weight their book was written for (the J-3's at gross, the D.112's light).
  • The ref-matched recreations (Stearman, Tiger Moth, Camel) reproduce their parents' shape to within 0-7 % on every dimension: the REF_MATCH tool works. Their weak side is the mass model on old construction (the Camel +37 % empty, the Tiger Moth +13 %) and a 10-25 % climb deficit, with low static margins (1-8 %).
  • The largest errors are build choices (engine, tail size) and the pilot (the landing roll on every plane, the climb speed), not the physics.
  • The model's own residuals: climb 10-20 % short on matched engines, the climb-out to 50 ft long on the 172 (the propeller's low-speed efficiency), L/D high on the metal 172 (the glide probe has the propeller off).

Next steps

  • The landing brake law (the pilot's, not PHYSICS_V): brakes from touchdown with a firmer ramp, the approach re-planned for the load. Landing rolls fall to the handbook's on every plane flown.
  • B-CESS's tail (a build item): stab span 2.72 to 3.40 m, elevator chord 0.265 to 0.44. It then trims its full flap at forward CG (0.41 against the 0.18 budget today).
  • The propeller's climb-speed efficiency (physics, PHYSICS_V): the 10-20 % climb deficit and the long climb-out.
  • The pilot's climb speed from the measured Vy instead of 1.38 Vs.
  • The mass model on old construction: a wire-braced wooden scout (the Camel) bills 37 % over its empty weight; read the frame's rows against REF_MATCH's three biplanes.
  • Redo the Petrel on water once claude/seaplanes-merge flies it; and the Birdman's arrivals (stalls in the flare).
  • GATE REALBAND on the official planes: the airframe bands and the approach-trim row (REAL-CHECK-2026-11-07 §8).

How the scores are computed

An item scores 100 × max(0, 1 − gap / 0.3). The gap is 0 when ours sits inside the reference band (the figure ± its tolerance), otherwise the relative distance to the band's nearest edge: 10 % outside scores 67, 30 % outside scores 0.

Geometry: the weighted mean over the key dimensions the reference states. Weights: span 2, wing area 2, length 1.5, root chord 1, tip chord 0.5, stab span 1, stab area 1, fin area 1, track 0.5, height 0.5. Tolerances: span and area 3 %, chords and tail 5 %, length 6 % (ours runs flange to rudder), height 10 % (the pose).

Specs: the engine family (named right or not), power per engine (3 %), the engine count, the propeller diameter (5 %) and blades, the empty (3 %) and maximum (5 %) weights, fuel (10 %), seats. Weights: engine 1.5, power / engine 2, engines 1, prop diameter 1, blades 0.5, empty weight 2, max weight 1, fuel 0.5, seats 0.5.

Performance: real_check's verdict bands (our MIN–MTOW band at the reference's weights against the reference's condition band), one reading per indicator - the airframe's (probes, no pilot) where it exists, else the handbook-flown one. That is the aeroplane's capability; the shipped pilot's extra cost is shown apart as the pilot's share. Weights: stall (clean) 1, cruise 75 % 1, top speed 1, rate of climb 1.5, climb (2nd weight) 1, best glide 0.5, take-off roll 1, take-off to 50 ft 0.5, landing roll 0.75, landing from 50 ft 0.5, water run 1, water run (2nd weight) 0.75, water time 0.5, water to 50 ft 0.5.

Overall = 0.3 × geometry + 0.3 × specs + 0.4 × performance, over the axes a plane has (the rest renormalised). Bands: ● ≥ 80 close, ▲ 60–80 fair, ■ under 60 far.

A reference marked THIN in tools/real_refs.js (one source, or sources that disagree) carries a wider tolerance or a range: a thin figure can neither condemn nor clear a plane by much.
