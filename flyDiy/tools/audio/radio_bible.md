# Radio Jolene — the persona bible (SND-RADIO-3, G1700)

The one page every line of Radio Jolene is written against. `tools/audio/radio_gen.js` carries the same cast,
businesses and threads as data; when this page and the generator disagree, fix the generator.

## The station

Radio Jolene is the island's community station. It runs out of the back room of the community hall, next to the
coat hooks and the coffee urn. Volunteers keep it going. It plays local roots music, and between the records Norman
reads out what the island needs to hear: the weather in plain words, the fire department, the harbour, the store,
lost dogs, potlucks, the school play, who to thank.

It is never a news service, it never quotes the outside world, and it never reads numbers off an instrument.
It doesn't announce a frequency. It says "Radio Jolene" and "the island".

## Norman

- Mid fifties. Fished for twenty years, then his knees told him to stop. Now he chairs the hall committee,
  organises the work parties and runs the radio.
- Rugged and practical, not sentimental. Proud of the island, and proudest when people show up. He keeps score of
  who came to the last work party and thanks them by name.
- The humour is warm and dry. A small joke at the end of a notice, never a punchline. He teases himself more than
  anyone else. He never mocks a caller.
- He talks like a neighbour, not an announcer. He uses contractions. He says "folks" and "good people". He talks
  about the weather the way a skipper does: what it means for your day.
- Things he cares about: the boardwalk, the float plane dock, the fire hall's pump, the school roof, the pie table.
- Things he never does: read digits, spell letters, say "zulu", give a phone number, put on a voice, sell hard.
- Sign-offs he uses, sparingly: "Keep your boots dry." "That's the word from the hall." "Be good to each other."

## Writing for the ear (the lint in GATE AUDIO's RADIO_LINT holds these)

- Short spoken sentences, between four and twenty-two words. One idea a sentence.
- Contractions. Natural commas where a speaker breathes. Full stops, question marks, the odd exclamation.
- No colons, semicolons, dashes, parentheses, quotes or lists. No digits. No acronyms in capitals.
  A four wheeler is a four wheeler. The volunteer fire department is "the fire department" or "the volunteers".
- Times and quantities in words, and only where a person would say them: "Saturday after the tide turns",
  "a dozen pies", "half past six". Never two number words in a row read like digits.
- No phone numbers ever. "Call the harbour office", "leave a note at the store", "find Norman at the hall".
- Every item is ONE whole take (or one take per full sentence): nothing is stitched from word clips.
- Names must be easy to say. A name the voice gets wrong gets a respelling in `voice_script.json` `say`.

## Respect

Jolene Island is the game's fictional island, drawn on real geography. Everyone and everything here is invented.
No real person, family, tribe, council, church, school or business from the real place is named or imitated. No
sacred or cultural specifics are borrowed. Nobody is a caricature. The texture is generic remote Alaska: skiffs,
smokehouses, berry picking, the barge, the float plane, gravel strips, the community hall, rain.

## The places Norman names

The harbour and its fuel dock, the float plane dock, the boardwalk, the community hall, the fire hall, the school,
the clinic, the library (one room above the store), the store, the cafe, the boat works, the sawmill at the head of
the bay, the old cannery, the point, the narrows, the creek mouth, the gravel strips: the field, Tamgas Hill strip,
East Point clearing, the Skyline strip up top.
