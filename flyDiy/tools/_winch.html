Glider Launch Winch

A twin-drum trailer winch, the kind a club parks at the far end of the field. It reels in up to a kilometre of cable at 20–30 m/s and pulls a sailplane up a steep climb to a few hundred metres. Drag to orbit, pinch or scroll to zoom.

What you are looking at

  • Trailer

    Tandem-axle chassis on leaf springs, chequer-plate deck, A-frame drawbar, jockey wheel and four stabiliser legs. The wheels are the Field Wagon van's, from the garage's own wheel builder. It is parked with the drawbar pointing away from the launch point.

  • Engine

    Up front in a louvred housing, radiator grille forward, exhaust stack up the side. Classic club winches run a big petrol V8; the presets also show a diesel and an electric build. A propshaft and gearbox drive the drum shaft.

  • Drums

    Two narrow, large-diameter drums on one transverse shaft, each with its own disc brake. One drum launches while the other cable is towed back out, so launches alternate.

  • Levelwinds

    A carriage behind each drum that runs side to side on two bars, laying the cable evenly as it comes in.

  • Guillotines

    The red boxes on the rear fairleads. If a glider cannot release, the driver fires the guillotine and cuts the cable at the winch.

  • Cage and cab

    Welded mesh over the drums, because a parting cable whips. The driver sits facing aft, behind a grilled window, and watches the cable and the glider. The amber beacon on the roof is lit while a launch is running.

  • Glider end

    The cable ends in a drogue parachute that slows its fall after release, then a strop, the weak link and the two rings that clip onto the glider's release hook.

  • Weathering

    Four dials: age, launches, grass and mud, rain. Age fades and chalks the paint, chips it at the edges down to primer and steel, and rusts the bare metal. Launches bring exhaust soot from the stack, grease at the drums, rollers and gearbox, and rollers polished bright by the cable. Grass and mud throw dirt from the wheels and leave a splash line up from the ground. Rain runs rust and grime down the sides and washes dust off the tops. The surfaces are the game's scanned painted, rusting and galvanised steel.
