What is being simulated?

This is a Greenberg–Hastings-style excitable cellular automaton, not a calibrated heart model. Each cell is resting, excited, or refractory. It only sees its eight immediate neighbours.
RESTING —enough active neighbours→ EXCITED
EXCITED —time→ REFRACTORY —time→ RESTING
Waves annihilate because each leaves temporarily inexcitable tissue behind. Spiral waves arise when a front breaks and its free end curls into recovered tissue. Walls, slowed conduction and competing pacemakers can create persistent re-entry. The square lattice introduces a small, deliberately visible directional bias. With Random reactivity enabled, a fixed fraction of cells receives a permanent local threshold offset: cyan cells are easier to excite, amber and magenta cells are harder. This is quenched disorder—the map stays fixed until reset or rerolled, rather than flickering randomly at every step. In Wave ancestry mode, color is observational only: a newly excited cell inherits the label of the active neighbour that triggered it. Higher spatial detail uses more cells and can therefore expose finer wave breaks, at a higher computational cost.
