degaror.github.io / laboratory

Cybernetic experiments

Four sandboxes about the same old question: how far can you get with feedback alone? No neural nets, no planners — sensors wired to motors, cells wired to neighbours, energy wired to appetite. Then you watch.

House rule

Each experiment is a full-screen canvas app that takes over the page. Opening one unloads this menu, so only a single simulation is ever running — nothing stacks up in the background, and phones stay cool. Use Back to return here.

EXP · 01

Machina Speculatrix

W. Grey Walter · 1948 · colony model

Grey Walter's light-seeking tortoises, rebuilt as a colony. Two photocells, two motors, crossed excitation — plus a lamp on the shell and a hutch to crawl home to when the battery dips. Because every machine carries a light, they end up dazzling each other, and the pack starts behaving like something it was never wired to be.

Lumen Society

social cybernetics sandbox

Braitenberg vehicles — sensor soldered straight to motor, no thinking in between — dropped into a shared arena of lights. Tap to plant a light, tap a vehicle to read its wiring, then change one rule: crossing, decay, population, variation. The society reorganises around it every time.

EXP · 03

Pulse Garden

excitable worlds laboratory

A field of cells with almost no intelligence at all: rest, fire, refract, rest. Draw a single pulse with your finger and watch fronts collide and annihilate, pacemakers compete for the tissue, and one broken wave curl into a spiral that looks disturbingly deliberate. Retune the timing and the whole topology reorganises.

EXP · 04

Trophic Vessel

closed ecosystem · heritable behavior

A sealed jar: plants, prey, predators, detritus. Biomass is conserved and every transfer stays in the ledger — energy is the only thing allowed in and out. Behaviour is heritable, so tighten the season or make speed expensive and selection quietly rewrites the population while you watch the charts.

How to read them

All four are laboratories, not games. There is no score and nothing to win — every one has a settings panel where a single parameter is worth changing at a time. Then leave it running for a minute.

On your phone

Built portrait-first: everything is one-thumb reachable, canvases scale to the screen, and each sim has a pause button in the top bar. Pause before you go digging in the panel — it saves the battery.

Under the hood

Vanilla JS, one self-contained HTML file each, no dependencies and nothing to install. Every simulation loop is requestAnimationFrame-driven, so a backgrounded tab does no work at all.
