## v1.16.0 — Tutorial leak, and contracts you can actually see (2026-08-16)

- **The tutorial banner no longer follows you into plants that have no tutorial.** `updateCoach()` is the function that *hides* the coach, and it was only called while the tutorial was active — so the moment you left Career the banner simply stayed, carrying its last step into whatever you loaded next. Loading the reference plant after playing Career showed a tutorial for a plant that has none. The render guard now also fires while the banner is still on screen, so it gets hidden exactly once. Smoke asserts the transition (`block → none`) and fails on the old code.
- **Contract cards show the actual bin truck and the bag colour.** Each supplier card carries its own truck livery sprite and a swatch of the waste type it collects, so the screen matches what you see driving around the yard; buyers get a dot in their product's colour.
- **Streams still locked behind R&D are listed too.** `Available` filtered on `supplierUnlocked`, so once you had signed the one supplier that starts unlocked the section was **empty** — with no hint that four other streams exist. They now appear tagged *Locked* with "Unlock it in R&D first", which is also the only place the game tells you what R&D is *for*.
- **Verified**: 581 QC checks / 67 suites, render smoke (now asserting 5 truck liveries and 4 unsigned streams on the contracts screen), i18n (300 literals) and the balance harness green.

## v1.15.0 — The 100% recycling plant ships as the reference (2026-08-16)

The shipped plant gets its own `ref-plant-100pct` suite (structure, the ring, the authored feeder rate, on-spec rate, nothing buried, nothing blocked, mass balance) and a dedicated render pass in the smoke.

- **Zone slab art is cropped to its solid content.** The source pad carried a ~70 px transparent margin on every side, and installing it uncropped left the concrete inset inside its own footprint. Both the container yard and the landfill now use the cropped pad (500×512, its true 0.98 aspect preserved by the cover-draw).
- **Removed the road painted onto the surround tiles.** The tile art already carries the road running straight up and down from the plant, so the strip added in v1.14.0 was a second road drawn over the first. The trucks' lead-in and lead-out land on the art as drawn.

## v1.14.0 — Export history, the through-road, and the first-load countdown (2026-08-16)

- **Mandate triggers now count FROM the gate, not from the start of the campaign.** Arming records the on-spec tonnage as a baseline (`pressure.baseOnSpec`), and the two mandates fire at **+150 t** and **+1000 t** past it via a new `onSpecSinceArm` metric. The absolute thresholds they replaced meant a careful builder who armed the gate late — already 800 t of on-spec into a considered plant — was handed **both** mandates the instant the gate opened, as a reward for being thorough. New `mandate-triggers-relative` suite arms at 5000 t and proves nothing fires until the relative tonnage is reached, and that the baseline never drifts.
- **Ten-day shipping history per export bay.** `recordOpexDay` now also snapshots each bay's bales shipped on-spec vs off-spec and keeps a `EXPORT_HIST`-day ring on the node (so it rides the existing save). The bay inspector charts the last 10 days as stacked columns — on-spec green above off-spec red — which turns "this bay is producing rejects" from a lifetime counter that only ever grows into a trend you can watch respond. It sits outside `#baleScroll`, which the 0.33 s live refresh rewrites wholesale. Verified in-browser: a film bay reading 37–41 px of off-spec against 1–4 px of on-spec, peak 30 bales/day.
- **The 6-products goal names which product is short.** It showed a single coarse percentage — the one thing it never told you was the thing you could act on. Now a chip per spec: `PET ✓ · FERROUS ✓ · ALUMINIUM ✓ · CARDBOARD 1/5 · FILM 3/5 · PVC 0/5`.
- **Belts queue back instead of smearing.** A blocked item clamps to `t=1` in the tick — a stall is a state, not a position — so a jam drew its whole backlog on a single pixel. The tail is now laid back along the belt behind the head, which is *more* truthful than the overlap: items have bodies and two cannot share a point. Deliberately a render change, not a physical one — a real queue-back needs visible spacing (~7 px) where a belt at its rated 10 t/h genuinely runs items **1.8 px** apart, so enforcing it would cut belt capacity about fivefold plant-wide. The separation is capped at `L/count`, so a genuinely full belt is untouched.
- **The haul road runs the FULL height of the neighbouring tiles.** Trucks drive in from off-site and continue out the far side rather than appearing and vanishing at the property line — they now travel from y −1260 to y +2520, the whole 3×3 world. A first attempt used an 8-cell stub, which was invisible in practice: `siteViewFit` frames exactly the main tile, so the approach sat off-screen and trucks still seemed to pop in at the boundary. Pathing still happens entirely on the grid (a straight lead-in bolted onto the ends of the BFS result), so no walk-set, memo or save migration is involved, with the road painted onto the tiles north and south to match.
  - **`truckMaxInflight` 2 → 3.** With a full tile of driving at each end, two trucks met a 5 t/h contract *exactly*, with no headroom — any further latency would have throttled it. A longer road genuinely means more trucks spaced along it, so this is the honest knob rather than making them drive faster. Re-measured: a single-bunker contract still delivers its full 5.0 t/h.
  - `site-trucks` now separates *on-site* waypoints (which must still be on road/apron cells) from the deliberate off-site ones, and `site-motion` samples legs at a fixed **arc length** instead of a fixed 240 samples — with a leg now several thousand px long, fixed sampling strode over corners and reported them as heading snaps.
- **A countdown for the first load.** A new site opens with empty bunkers and no stock, so the first truck has to accrue a full 10 t load and then drive in — realistic, but it presented as hours of a plant doing nothing with no explanation. The HUD strip now leads with **"First load arriving · 4h 42"**, then **"Unloading the first truck"** once it is on the apron, and hands the strip back the moment material lands. Measured on Atelier: **~4.9 h** to the truck appearing, **~6.9 h** to the first tip — of which only 0.94 h is driving; the rest is accruing the load. (Career primes the first truck and starts in ~1.2 h.)
  - It counts to the **load landing**, not to arrival, deliberately: an arrival-only countdown fell to zero and then jumped back up to a fresh 2 h of unloading. `first-truck-eta` asserts the number *never* increases, which the earlier version failed.
- **Balance**: the reference plant now runs **€11,087/day** at **5.0 t/h** intake (was €9,489 at 4.0 t/h) — the lift is the voluntary contract going 4 → 5 t/h in v1.12.0, not the road. Mass balance exact.

## v1.12.0 — Contract sequence, per-supplier trucks, a recycling rate you can actually move (2026-08-16)

- **The mandate sequence is now two contracts, sized against the 8 t/h ceiling.** A voluntary contract is **5 t/h** (was 4), the **small** mandate is **2.5 t/h** and the **large** is **5 t/h**. So: one contract of your own (5) fits beside the small one (7.5 of 8 — it *pushes* you); it cannot also fit the large one (12.5 against 8), which forces a **substitution of provider**. `m_commercial` is retired as a mandate — Poubelle Air stays a voluntary 5 t/h alternative to switch to during the squeeze, which is what makes the substitution a choice rather than a punishment. The full sequence, gate and triggers are pinned in `inbound-cap`.
- **Recycling % is now a 24-hour figure, not a lifetime one.** A lifetime ratio can never climb back to 100% once an early learning period is baked into it — a player who fixes everything still reads their old mistakes forever, which makes the number useless as feedback. `recordOpexDay` now carries tonnage (sold on-spec / off-spec / buried / accepted) alongside the money, and the HUD reads the last completed day, relabelled **"Recycled (24h)"**. It falls back to the lifetime figure, under the old label, until a full day is banked — so it never quietly shows one thing while meaning another. The lifetime figure stays in the balance sheet. New `recycling-yesterday` suite proves the 24h reading is *unmoved* by adding 5000 t of historical burial that collapses the lifetime number.
- **Steel was invisible on the belt.** Every item draws a 50%-alpha halo in its material colour, and steel's `#5B6670` sat on a belt bed of `#565149` and a frame of `#6F665C` — grey on grey. Now a pale cool silver `#BFD4E8`: still reads as ferrous, but **104 luma clear** of the nearest belt tone, and nowhere near PET's deep blue or alu's mint. The smoke test now asserts that gap rather than merely that the hex changed.
- **Six bin-truck liveries, one per supplier** (art by Denis). A truck's colour is now **company identity** — resolved from the supplier's own `truck` field — while the bag/bunker colour stays the **waste type**, which suppliers collecting the same stream legitimately share. The three old supplier sprites (`veh_0/1/2`) are retired from the registry and precache in favour of six named `sup_*` assets; the interim cab identity band is removed, since it existed only to tell apart suppliers that had to share a sprite. Smoke asserts every streaming supplier resolves to a unique livery and that all of them decode.
- **You can end a contract from the Contracts screen.** A contract *is* its bunker assignment — there is no separate agreement object — so cancelling meant opening each bunker and guessing that "None (idle)" was the cancel button. Each signed contract now shows how many bunkers serve it and carries an **End contract** action (with a confirm that says what happens); available streams are tagged **Not signed** with "Point a bunker at it to sign".
- **Fixed a misleading warning**: the Contracts screen blamed mandates for *any* squeeze, including one caused by signing two 5 t/h contracts into an 8 t/h site with no mandate running at all. It now names the actual cause.
- **`setView` is null-safe.** A shell missing a panel — a cached `index.html` served against newer JS — threw out of `begin()` and took the whole boot with it. It now degrades to "that view is unavailable".
- **Verified**: 542 QC checks / 63 suites, render smoke, i18n (288 literals) and the balance harness green. Confirmed in-browser: five distinct truck liveries all decoding at 60×150, the 24h stat reading 40% where lifetime read 36%, the End-contract action, and — end to end through the real boot path — that researched tech and claimed goals now survive a reload.

## v1.11.0 — The site intake ceiling, the diversion exploit, and the Contracts screen (2026-08-16)
Second playtest pass. The headline is that **mandates no longer stack tonnes onto your plant** — the escalation is now a materials problem, which is what the mandate system claimed to be from the start.

- **SITE INTAKE CEILING — 8 t/h total, imposed and voluntary combined (`LOGI.inboundCap`).** Imposed mandates were push-only (`M.active.push`, never reversed), so their rates *added*: 3 + 4 + 6 = 13 t/h forced on top of 8 t/h of voluntary contracts — 21 t/h against a line that carries ~10. Building a second plant was the only answer. Now mandates are served **first** (they cannot be refused) and **your own contracts share whatever is left**, scaled proportionally. Mandate rates retuned 3/4/6 → **1.5 / 2 / 2.5** so all three together take 6 of the 8, leaving a 2 t/h voluntary stream you still choose. 8 t/h in plus ~2 t/h recirculated fills one beefed-up line exactly. Their *compositions are untouched* — Skip Bizet is still 19% PVC and Watco still 24% PVC / 30% film — so late game is a contamination fight, not a tonnage one. The ceiling binds only **named contracts**: a scenario or harness that hand-sets `G.contract.feedTph` states an authored rate and is never silently clamped (NNG-6).
- **Fixed a real exploit: landfill diversion counted material you had merely *not buried yet*.** `diversionNow` was `1 − buried/accepted`, so everything sitting in your bunkers, balers and bays read as diverted. A plant that had **sold nothing at all** read **100%** and could bank the €150k grant — and since `bestDiversion` is a permanent high-water mark, it never fell again. Leaving a baler's output unconnected made that trivially reachable; so did simply being a new plant. It is now **`sold / (sold + buried)`** — of everything that has actually LEFT the site, what share left as product. Held material neither helps nor hurts until it moves, which is also the industry definition and matches the discipline `recyclingPct` already had. Verified: 1000 t accepted with nothing sold now reads 0% and the grant is not claimable. (Off-spec bales still count as diverted — they are *sold* at the off-spec price, not buried.)
  - The banking gate moved with it: a diversion *record* is permanent, so it now needs **200 t settled (sold + buried)**, not 50 t merely *accepted* — otherwise a transient early 100% (sell a few bales, bury nothing yet) would be banked for good. 50 t of intake was a few hours' work.
  - **Denis's baler hypothesis, tested and cleared**: disconnecting baler outputs *loses* money (cash fell 2.00M → 1.985M over ~7 days versus rising to 2.05M wired up), mass stays conserved, and recycling% correctly reads 0. Stockpiling is not profitable — it was only the diversion metric that was wrong.
- **CONTRACTS screen (new 4th tab).** A stream's rate, its composition and whether it ever ends were nowhere on screen — the bunker chips showed a gate price and nothing else. The new view leads with the **intake ceiling as a stacked bar** (imposed / yours / free), then lists **imposed** contracts (rate, gate, full composition, *Cannot be refused*, *Ongoing*), **your contracts** (showing the squeezed rate *and* the rated one when mandates are crowding them out, with an explicit warning), **available** streams, and **buyers** (on-spec and off-spec price, daily offtake). Live-refreshes on a signature guard like the Goals view.
- **Bag colour is a WASTE TYPE, not a company badge** — the art ships exactly three liveries, so five suppliers cannot each own one: **blue** = clean kerbside PMC (Wasteminster), **green** = commercial dry recyclables (Binfinity, Poubelle Air), **yellow** = contaminated residual (Skip Bizet, Watco). Suppliers sharing a type are still told apart on the road by the cab identity band.
- **A bunker now shows the dominant bag colour of what is actually in it**, not the supplier on its label — which matters precisely because an imposed mandate tips into *every* bunker regardless of what you assigned it. Tracked as tonnes per bag type and decayed lazily: loaders scoop non-selectively, so removal takes every type in proportion and the ratio is unchanged, making it a single multiply at read time with no hooks in the hot loop. Emptying a bunker forgets its mix, and the mix round-trips through save/restore.
- **Two blind spots in the i18n gate closed.** `tools/i18ncheck.js` only scans `tr("literal")` call sites, so `SPECS` labels were invisible the same way `OBJ`/`MANDATE` names were — the buyer tags rendered an untranslated "CARDBOARD". `rendersmoke` now checks all 18 against `LANG.fr`. Also fixed: buyer offtake printed a literal "Infinity" instead of *unlimited*, and off-spec prices printed "€-50/t" instead of "−€50/t" (an off-spec price is usually a disposal cost).
- **Recycling % was measured against total INTAKE, so material still on site counted against you forever.** A plant choked with 1300 t in its bunkers read 60% despite never having buried a tonne. It is now on-spec sold over everything that has **left** the site (sold + buried) — the same settled basis as diversion. Stockpiling still cannot inflate it; it simply does not count until it moves. The reported save reads **74%** instead of 60%. Still lifetime, so a bad early period stays on the record.
- **Orphaned imposed streams are reconciled on load (`reconcileMandateState`).** A mandate lives in `CAREER`, but the material it owes lives on the *nodes* (`b.mandDue`) and in trucks already dispatched — different objects. The v1.10.0 boot bug blanked the live career, and saves carried on being flooded by three imposed streams with `mandates.active` empty: trucks with no contract behind them, tipping into bunkers until they overflowed and the surplus was buried and billed. **This was the actual cause of a real player's "choked with too high inputs"** — 100% of the ongoing burial traced to the imposed-overflow path, on a career with no active mandates. Every load now drops dues for streams that are not imposed and turns unTIPPED forced trucks around. Mass-safe: a due is material not yet delivered, and a truck books its load only when it tips.
- **New named state: LOOP DEADLOCK.** A recirculation loop — routing residue back upstream instead of to a reject bay — can lock solid, because transfers are doubly gated (NNG-1): once every stage of the ring holds material with nowhere to put it, nobody can hand off and nobody can free room. The states it produced were JAMMED and OVERLOAD, which both read as *add capacity*, and no amount of capacity clears a circular wait. Cycles are detected on a cached topology signature, and a ring is called deadlocked only when **nothing on it has moved for 3 s and it is still holding material** — measured on movement, not on per-unit badges, since a locked ring is a mix of full units and units starved behind them. The inspector says what actually clears it: give the loop a drain, or break the ring.
- **New container-storage art**, installed at 512×512 (~1.8× the maximum on-screen size — 90 px footprint × DPR × zoom — for 62 KB). **Zone slab art is no longer stretched**: it is drawn aspect-preserved (cover, centre-cropped). That was invisible while every slab matched its zone, but the landfill art is 840×360 — the old 7×3 bay — and squashed 3.5× horizontally the moment that bay became 2×3 in this release.
- **Verified**: 532 QC checks / 62 suites, render smoke, i18n (280 literals) and the balance harness green. New suites: `loop-deadlock` (a closed ring is detected, named, stays mass-conserving through the lock, and clears when the ring is broken), `inbound-cap` (all mandates together fit inside the ceiling, with room left for a voluntary stream; measured intake honours it), `bunker-bag-mix` (a minority imposed stream does not flip the livery, a dominant one does, draining preserves the ratio, emptying forgets, survives save/restore), and a rewritten `diversion-metric` built around the stockpile case. Two existing suites were corrected rather than loosened: `mandate-truck-carries-its-stream` sampled a single instant for in-flight trucks and started landing in a cadence trough at the lower imposed rate (it now polls the window — dispatch was never broken), and `inbound-cap` asserts deliveries *broadly track* the rating because waste arrives in 10 t truckloads and five small streams cannot average an exact rate.

## v1.9.0 — Burden depth: rated throughput is not clean throughput (2026-08-01)
The lever that turns *one efficient line* into a *plant*. Until now a sorter's accuracy was completely independent of how hard it was driven, so there was never a reason to build a second train — only bigger numbers on the first.

- **A separator's rated t/h is its MECHANICAL throughput, not its clean-sorting throughput.** Drive it hard and material rides deep instead of spread: targets get buried and missed, and neighbours are dragged into the ejected stream. Deep burden now costs **selectivity**, not just speed — the one rule that makes a single line physically unable to run clean at high tonnage.
- **Burden = utilisation × queue, and both terms are required.** `did/rated` saturates at 1 by construction (the budget *is* the rated allowance), so "perfectly matched to its feed" and "drowning" look identical on throughput alone; a queue on its own only means the unit is blocked downstream, not that material is riding deep past the sensor. A unit that is busy with no queue is unpenalised; one that cannot keep up backs its buffer up and pays.
- **Measured, not guessed.** The reference plant's worst sorter runs at 0.65 utilisation with a near-empty buffer (burden ≈ 0.01), so the knee sits at 0.35 on the *product* — comfortably clear. Verified end-to-end: **op net €9,030/day and 31.9% recycling, identical to the pre-burden baseline, with burden k = 0 on every sorter.** An existing working plant is untouched.

## v1.8.0 — Imposed mandates, landfill teeth, belt overpasses (2026-08-01)
The build loop used to terminate: one good line sorted all six products and nothing ever forced a re-decision again. The world now moves on its own.

- **THE GATE — nothing bites until you can actually run a plant.** New objective **“Run a full line — all 6 products on-spec”** (`a_all6`, +300k): each product counts once it has cleared 5 t on-spec, so it means a working line, not a lucky bale. Meeting it **arms** the pressure system; until then there are no imposed contracts and no landfill escalation whatsoever — a player still learning is never punished, however profitable they get. The escalation clock also starts *at arming*, so taking ten in-game years to learn does not mean arriving at year-ten prices. (`CAREER.counters.onSpec` is now accumulated live in `sellBale`; it was previously written only by a settle path that continuous careers never reach.)
- **Imposed contracts.** Once armed, your own success metrics trigger a waste contract that is **forced** on you: a couple of days’ warning, then the trucks roll, with a capital grant to build against. Three chained mandates route through the existing (previously name-only) carriers — Skip Bizet (3 t/h, 19% PVC against PET’s 0.5% cap), Poubelle Air (4 t/h, alu+film rich), Watco Syndicate (6 t/h, 24% PVC / 30% film). Each is dirtier than a voluntary supplier and pays a higher gate: an imposed stream is a *materials* problem, not just more tonnes.
- **The punishment is automatic and emergent — no deadline, no fine, no compliance check.** An imposed truck **cannot be turned away**: it tips its whole load and whatever your bunkers can’t hold is buried and billed. Gate in (€70–95/t) minus landfill out means unprocessed imposed waste bleeds ~€40/t and worsens as the tax escalates. With no intake built at all, the stream still arrives and goes straight to landfill. It never pauses the sim — the bleed *is* the message.
- **Landfill acquired teeth, through pricing only.** The gate compounds +12%/pressure-year (TGAP-shaped: €110 → €173 by y5 → €305 by y10) on top of a self-scaling annual allowance (35% of what you *accept*, tapering 12%/yr toward a 10% floor, plus 200 t free) beyond which tonnes cost ×3. A percentage allowance means a buried tonne raises both numerator and denominator, so overflow is doubly punishing — the incentive falls out of pricing alone. Dumps straddling the allowance line are split-priced, so there is no dump-before-the-boundary exploit.
- **Belt overpasses.** Crossing belts no longer render as an ambiguous overlap: the horizontal run is the deck, and the belt below — casing *and* its in-flight items — is genuinely occluded (even-odd clip holes, so an item is eaten by the deck edge rather than popping), with a contact shadow and deck trim to sell it as a bridge. `hitTest` mirrors the same rule, so you can no longer tap the belt you cannot see. **`e.route` is untouched** — belt capacity, speed and capex all derive from it; the overpass is derived, cached render geometry keyed on an exact route signature (no engine hooks to forget).
- Inbound tipping is now one shared choke-point for the site and non-site paths, which also **fixes a latent bug**: a truck in flight used to tip whatever supplier the bunker pointed at on arrival, not the stream it was dispatched for.
- **Verified**: 438 QC checks / 52 suites, render smoke, i18n (253 literals) and the balance harness all green; the gate, the escalation anchor, the overflow bleed and the pixel-level occlusion were each confirmed in-browser.
